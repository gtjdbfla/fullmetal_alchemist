# Prologue: The Alchemist Who Can't Use Alchemy

"Not there."

The man's hand froze.

The alchemist crouched at the mouth of the mine, halfway through drawing his transmutation circle, looked up. "What?"

"Do it like that and the right side comes down first."

"...What?"

Edward Elric leaned against the wall with his arms crossed. Dust kept drifting out of the mine. Part of the shaft had just collapsed, and three men were still inside.

"There's a lot of iron in this rock. Push on it from that direction and your supports won't hold. They'll get shoved sideways."

The man frowned. "You a miner?"

"No."

"An engineer?"

"Not that, either."

"Then what are you?"

Ed thought about it. "...I studied something close."

The man looked at him like he couldn't believe this. "Studied what?"

"Alchemy."

The man's gaze dropped to Ed's hands, then climbed back up to his face. "You're an alchemist?"

"Well..." Ed scratched the back of his head. "Sort of."

"If you know so much, why don't you do it yourself?"

"..."

Ed shut his mouth. Then, almost on reflex, he clapped his hands together.

*Clap.*

He had made that motion countless times over the years. Hands together. Palms to the ground.

Nothing happened.

Of course nothing happened. Ed looked down at his hands. He knew that. He knew he had no Gate anymore, that he couldn't use alchemy. And yet, every now and then, his body forgot.

"...Yeah," he muttered.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Ed walked over to the circle the man had drawn. "Give me the chalk."

"You can't use alchemy, can you?"

"I can't."

"Then—"

"I can still draw."

He rubbed out one side of the circle and drew the line again. "Your calculation's off here."

"Calculation?"

"You're routing the force straight up, so the pressure piles up sideways. Spread it out. Here, and here."

The man leaned over Ed's correction. His expression changed by degrees.

"And there should be another empty space further in."

"How do you know that?"

"The wind."

"The wind?"

Ed pointed at the tunnel mouth. "If it were sealed solid, that dust wouldn't be moving like that."

The man stared at him for a long moment. "...You're really not a miner?"

"I told you, no."

A little while later, the man laid his hand on the revised circle. Blue light spread across the ground.

*Krrrrrrr.*

The rock at the mine's mouth began to shift. Ed watched the wall. The cracks changed direction.

"That's far enough!"

The transmutation stopped. A narrow gap had opened. Seconds later, a faint voice came from inside.

"Here—!"

"Someone's alive in there!"

The miners ran. One man. Two. Then the last. All of them alive. A cheer went up.

Ed stepped back and brushed the dust off his coat. The man who'd drawn the circle came over to him.

"Thank you."

"I didn't do much."

"Without you, another tunnel would've come down."

"You're the one who did the transmutation."

The man laughed under his breath. "What's your name?"

"Edward." Ed took his hand. "Edward Elric."

The man's face went rigid. "Elric?"

"What?"

"Wait."

He stared hard at Ed's face. "Could you be... the Fullmetal Alchemist?"

Ed's face crumpled. "Don't call me that."

"Seriously?"

"It's *former*."

"The Fullmetal Alchemist is this—"

The man's eyes slid downward. Ed's eyebrow twitched.

"This what."

"He's a little more..."

"A little more what."

"...young. Than I expected."

"You were about to say something else."

"I wasn't."

"You were!"

"I wasn't!"

"You were going to say *shrimp*!"

"I never said it! Not once!"

Laughter broke out in front of the mine.

"Quit laughing!"

Ed stomped back to his lodgings, fuming.

* * *

That night, maps and books covered the desk of the small inn room. A mineralogy text he'd picked up in a country to the west. Transmutation circles that differed from region to region. Techniques for working metal. Notes scribbled for him by alchemists he'd met along the way.

Since he'd set out traveling, Ed had been studying alchemy harder than ever. It was a little strange. When he could still use it, alchemy had been so obvious he'd never needed to examine it. Understand the structure of matter. Decompose it. Reconstruct it. Equivalent Exchange. He had believed that was simply how the world worked.

He had even seen the Truth once. He'd seen more there than any human was meant to. For a while, he'd thought that made him someone who knew a little more about the world than other people did.

But now.

Now that he'd lost the Gate and stepped back out into the world, he found it was full of things he didn't know.

Ed turned a page. A line written by a scholar from another country caught his eye. It explained the same phenomenon the Amestrian scholars did, in a completely different way. He read a few lines. Flipped back to the previous page. Read it again.

"...Okay. This one makes sense too."

The old Ed would have started by arguing over which side was right. But traveling had shown him this happened all the time. People looked at the same thing and described it differently. People used different methods and got similar results.

He rested his chin on his hand.

It had started after he lost alchemy. Strangely, that was when he'd begun to wonder what alchemy even *was*.

He didn't want it back. That had been his choice. He had gotten Al back. That was enough.

But with alchemy gone, he found himself noticing things he'd never seen before.

Ed looked at his right hand for a moment. Then back at the book. He turned the next page.

Maybe, for the first time, he wasn't looking at the world through alchemy. He was looking at alchemy itself.
