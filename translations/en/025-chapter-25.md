# Chapter 25: The Man Who Would Kill the Emperor

Fewer than twenty people had gathered outside Lansi's western gate. They carried no flags. None wore armor. They had only the dust-covered clothes and baggage of people who had traveled a long way.

Several wore swords at their waists. One had a short club tied to his pack. Among them was a man carrying nothing at all.

Kairun.

Up ahead, Chenluo was speaking to the gate guards.

"Leave your weapons here before you go in."

The swords went into a box one by one. The club too, and the short knives.

Kairun's turn came. A guard looked over his waist and sleeves.

"Is that everything?"

"Yes."

"You're meeting the emperor empty-handed?"

"I came to talk."

The guard asked no more and let him through.

Passing through the gate, Kairun looked around Lansi. Goods brought in from the west were piled high. Wagons passed, and people haggled over prices. Far away, an imperial flag flew above a tall building.

* * *

"The people from the An clan have arrived."

At Furong's words, Ed set down his food.

"Xiaolin?"

"No."

"Then Chenluo?"

"His group, yes."

Ed picked up his food again. "Then tell Ling."

"He already knows."

"Then why come to me?"

"I thought you would come with me."

"Why would I?"

"Aren't you curious?"

Ed's hand stopped. Furong smiled.

"I thought so."

"Thought what?"

"Shall we?"

"Who said I was going?!"

Ten minutes later, Ed was on his way to the administrative offices.

* * *

Ling had deliberately chosen not to use the large conference room. There was no throne or raised dais. The two sides sat facing one another across a long table.

Lan Fan stood to Ling's right. Luhan and Zhenha were a little farther away. Ed stood with his back against the wall.

The An delegation sat on the other side of the table. The men people called hardliners settled in around Kairun. Taking a seat toward the back, Kairun adjusted the hem of his trousers. It looked like nothing more than someone settling into his chosen seat.

Chenluo spoke first.

"Is it true that the imperial court is surveying An land and wells?"

"It's true," Ling replied.

"And that you intend to restrict land purchases by outsiders?"

"We're considering it."

"And regulate rights to use the pastures and wells?"

"Yes."

Chenluo fell silent for a moment. "Then the rumors weren't wrong."

"They weren't entirely right, either."

"What is different?"

Ling pushed a document across the table.

"First, we'll establish the rights of the people already using them. Stop outsiders from buying up all the land and water. Make sure the people living there now aren't driven out when the new road opens."

"Who decides?" Chenluo asked.

"What?"

"What those existing rights are."

Ling paused. "We'll investigate—"

"The imperial court will?"

"The An people will take part too."

"In a process the imperial court has decided on?"

The room went quiet. Arms folded, Ed watched them. It wasn't very different from what he'd heard in Mulanhe a few days earlier.

But this time it wasn't Chenluo who spoke. Kairun, seated behind him, opened his mouth.

"Why do we need your permission to protect our own land?"

A guard shifted. Lan Fan's gaze turned toward Kairun as well. Ling raised a hand to stop them.

"Go on."

Kairun looked at him. "Before, the order was to take it away."

"..."

"This time, the order is to protect it."

His voice wasn't loud.

"It was an emperor who took it. An emperor who returned it. An emperor who protects it. An emperor who builds the roads. An emperor who closes them."

Kairun asked, "In the end, you're the one who decides."

Ling said nothing.

"Whether our land is ours," Kairun said. "Does the emperor decide that too?"

"No," Ling answered.

Kairun narrowed his eyes. "Then what are you protecting?"

This time, Ling had no immediate answer either.

* * *

The meeting didn't end there. Chenluo discussed well rights. Someone else discussed pastures. An imperial official explained the current scope of the survey.

Ling asked questions several times. At others, he simply listened. No conclusion was reached.

"We're not deciding today," Ling said. "We'll review it again with your views included."

Kairun stood. "And then you decide?"

Ling looked at him. Kairun said no more.

People began to leave, one by one. Ed pushed away from the wall. Kairun passed him, and Ed's gaze caught on his hands. There were calluses on his fingers and palms. These weren't simply the hands of someone who worked hard.

"You."

Kairun stopped.

"Are you a martial artist?"

"Why?"

"Your hands."

Kairun looked down at them. "Are you on the emperor's side?"

"What?"

"You've just been watching this whole time."

Ed snorted. "I'm just an alchemist passing through... Well, someone who used to be one."

"An alchemist."

Kairun studied Ed briefly, then turned toward the door.

"Without an emperor..."

His foot stopped.

"...would Xing still function?"

Ed's eyes dropped to Kairun's foot. His ankle, to be precise. His trouser hem had risen, exposing a small line drawn on bare skin.

A circle.

"Ling!"

The floor surged upward.

* * *

Lan Fan shoved Ling aside. Stone burst through the table.

"Your Majesty!"

The guards drew their swords. Kairun struck the nearest soldier's wrist. The sword fell. A second soldier charged. Kairun dropped low. An elbow. A knee. The soldier hit the floor.

"Don't kill him!" Ling shouted. "Take him alive!"

Lan Fan moved and met Kairun head-on. Fists. Arms. Feet. In an instant, their positions changed several times.

Ed wasn't watching them. He was looking at the floor. The stone that had risen first. The broken table. The pillar.

"...What?"

Kairun evaded Lan Fan's attack. At that moment, the wall beside them burst.

*Boom!*

Fragments of stone flew through the air. Ed ducked.

"Where's the transmutation circle?"

There wasn't one. Nothing on the wall.

"What?"

Ed looked at Kairun's foot. At the beginning—on Kairun's ankle—there had been one. But there was none on the wall.

"Ling!"

"What?!"

"Don't read his movements!"

"Then what am I supposed to read?!"

Ed's eyes swept the floor. Floor. Pillar. Wall.

"The path the transmutation took!"

"What?"

"You can't just watch him!"

Ling turned. At that instant, the base of a pillar split. He dodged.

"You were right!"

"I'm surprised too!"

"You said that without knowing?!"

"I'm figuring it out now!"

"At a time like this?!"

"When else am I supposed to?!"

* * *

"Stop!"

Chenluo's voice rang out. Kairun glanced at him.

"We didn't come here for this!"

"Talking doesn't work."

"Kairun!"

"In the end, he decides."

Kairun pointed at Ling.

"We talk."

One step.

"He listens."

Another step.

"He decides."

"That's what I'm trying to change," Ling said.

Kairun laughed. "Who is?"

Ling's expression hardened.

"You?"

"..."

"You decide that too?"

Kairun stepped on the floor. There was no new transmutation circle. Yet stone at the edge of the ceiling moved.

Ed's eyes widened. *Again.*

Floor. Pillar. Wall. Ceiling. And then—back to the floor.

"One," Ed murmured.

"What?" Ling asked.

"Transmutation circle."

Ed shouted, "There's only one!"

"Then cut it!" Ling shouted.

"How?!"

Ed's eyes swept the floor. Stone floor. Pillars. Walls. Ceiling.

And the threshold.

It was wood.

The cracks running from the shattered floor to the pillars stopped dead at the threshold.

"Lan Fan! Drive him toward that door!"

"What—"

"It's wood! It stops at wood!"

Lan Fan didn't ask why. She backed away from the side where stone was rising and pushed Kairun toward the door.

Kairun caught on.

"...You read it?"

For the first time, Kairun looked at Ed instead of Lan Fan.

In that gap, Ed ran. He kicked a broken table leg across the floor and shoved it under Kairun's foot.

The foot that landed on the wood paused for a beat. The rising stone went out of true.

"Now!"

Lan Fan's fist drove into Kairun's side.

Kairun staggered back, but he didn't fall. He wiped blood from his mouth with the back of his hand.

"One time might work," he said, and laughed. "Not twice."

The floor shook. A shard of stone grazed Lan Fan's arm. Blood bloomed.

"Lan Fan!"

"I'm fine."

Kairun moved. Lan Fan blocked his way. This time, it wasn't simply an exchange of punches. Kairun twisted before Lan Fan moved. Where her foot was about to land, stone jutted from the wall.

She dodged. Kairun's fist was waiting. She blocked it and retreated. This time, the floor rose.

"That bastard..."

Ed watched Kairun. He wasn't only watching his opponent. The floor. The walls. The space around them. He was watching all of it.

Lan Fan closed in again. Kairun evaded her.

The desert canyon flashed through Ed's mind. Channels. Walls. Pillars. Connected lines.

"...Karsa?"

But something was immediately wrong. There were no such lines here. No channels laid out in advance. No structures to direct the flow. Yet things were moving.

"No," Ed murmured. "I don't know."

Not yet.

* * *

Kairun turned toward Ling. Lan Fan blocked his path. Kairun's foot skimmed the floor. Stone moved.

If Lan Fan dodged—Ling was behind her.

Ling realized it too.

"Lan Fan!"

Lan Fan didn't retreat. She twisted past the stone and drove inward. Kairun's hand moved. The floor that was about to rise toward Ling split.

Lan Fan was faster.

A short sound. Kairun's body stopped. Then he fell.

The conference room went quiet.

Chenluo ran to Kairun and grabbed his shoulder.

"Kairun! Hold on. Why would you go alone—"

Kairun was still breathing.

"...If we all came... everyone dies."

His eyes found Ling.

"That's why I came alone."

His gaze stopped on the ceiling.

Ling didn't move. Lan Fan looked down at Kairun for a long time.

"Is he dead?" Ling asked.

"...Yes."

Ling closed his eyes, then opened them.

"Next time, don't kill."

Lan Fan didn't answer.

"I know," Ling said. "Even so. Don't kill."

"...Yes."

* * *

"We didn't know," Chenluo said. "We truly didn't know he would do this."

The guards turned their eyes on the An people. Several had already been disarmed. Ling looked at them.

"I know."

"Your Majesty."

"Coming here with him doesn't mean you came here to kill me with him."

Chenluo closed his mouth.

"But there has to be an investigation."

"...Understood."

"Stay in Lansi until it's finished."

"We will."

Ling looked at the guards.

"Leave the other An people alone."

"But he attacked Your Majesty—"

"Kairun did," Ling cut in. "The An clan didn't."

No one answered.

"If the investigation shows otherwise, we'll judge again then."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Ling exhaled and looked at Ed. Ed was still crouching on the floor.

"...What are you doing?"

"Give me a second."

"A man just died."

"I know."

Ed brushed a hand across the floor. "That's why I'm looking."

"At what?"

"This."

Ling approached. "What's there?"

"Nothing."

"What?"

"Nothing."

Ed pointed at the lines remaining on Kairun's ankle.

"There was one here."

Then at the wall. "But not there."

The pillar. "Or here."

The ceiling. "Or there."

Ling frowned. "Alkahestry can work at a distance anyway."

"No," Ed said immediately. "This is different."

"How?"

Ed didn't answer. He couldn't explain it yet.

* * *

The imperial investigation began that evening. Kairun's lodgings. The people who had accompanied him. His route to Lansi. His connections within the An clan. The people he had recently contacted.

His belongings were brought from his room and laid out on a table in the administrative offices. Money. Dried provisions. Travel supplies. Clothes. A few documents. An old metal ornament and assorted odds and ends. Nothing looked remarkable.

Lan Fan checked the investigation's findings with the officer in charge of security, while Ling took separate statements from Chenluo and the others. No one emerged who had known Kairun would attack. At least, not on the first day.

Ed glanced at the table and walked past. He was heading for the conference room.

The traces of the afternoon were still there. The broken floor. The cracked pillar. The shattered wall.

Ed marked their positions on paper. The initial circle. The first transmutation. The second. The third.

"...One."

No matter how he looked at it, the result was the same. One transmutation circle.

Karsa had been different. There had been channels. Lines on the walls. Structures in the ground and pillars for the flow to pass through. They had made paths in advance.

But here—nothing.

Ed tapped the pillar. Ordinary stone. The wall was the same.

"Then how?"

It had looked as though Kairun were walking along lines that already existed, rather than drawing new ones.

Alkahestry. The Dragon's Pulse. Karsa. The flow of nature. The words joined and separated in his mind.

Ed recalled Kairun fighting that afternoon. He hadn't watched only Lan Fan. He had watched the floor. The walls. The entire space.

Yet the damage ran only along the stone floor and pillars. The cracks stopped at the wooden threshold.

Ed wrote on his paper.

*Stops where he can't read?*

The lines on the ankle had been drawn before he arrived. The visitors had surrendered their weapons, but no one had examined the bare skin under his trouser hems.

The body was part of nature too. Perhaps he had used the lines drawn on his own body to find the flow within this building.

For a moment, Ed remembered what Majid had said in Karsa—the lines Scar's brother had wanted to try using on his body.

But Kairun hadn't been experimenting. He had already been reading.

"...What did you see?"

No answer.

Ed wrote Kairun's name on the paper. Beneath it, a question.

*Where did he learn it?*

The door opened. Ling.

"Still at it?"

"Yeah."

"The investigation starts in earnest tomorrow."

"Right."

"So far, it looks like the people who came with him didn't know."

"Right."

"Apparently, Kairun was pretty hard-line, even among the An."

"Right."

Ling narrowed his eyes. "Are you listening?"

"I'm listening."

"You don't care, do you?"

Ed looked up. "Ling."

"What?"

"Tell me when you find out where Kairun came from."

Ling paused. "...That's what you want to know?"

"Yeah."

"Not who told him to kill me?"

"You're investigating that."

Ling stared at him in disbelief. "I'm the emperor."

"Exactly."

Ed looked down at the paper again. "It's your country."

"You really are—"

"And you're alive."

"Hey."

"But that..."

Ed pointed at the broken wall. "...I still don't understand."

Ling sighed. "All right. I'll tell you when we find his hometown."

"And where he learned alkahestry."

"That too?"

"Yeah."

"His teacher?"

"If he had one."

"Where he traveled?"

Ed thought for a moment. "That too."

Ling gave a small laugh. "You'll end up investigating more than I do."

"Shut up."

Ling went out and closed the door.

Ed looked at the floor again. One transmutation circle. A transmutation that hadn't ended. Similar to Karsa. But not the same.

He still didn't know the difference. So he would have to find out.

At the bottom of the page, he added one more line.

*What was Kairun looking at?*
