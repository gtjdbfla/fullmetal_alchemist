# Chapter 9: Into the Desert

The desert was hot from the morning. Ed covered his face with his cloak.

"...It's hot."

"It'll be worse at noon," said Hashim, walking ahead.

Hashim was a desert merchant who traveled between Ishval and the northern caravans, and he'd agreed to guide Ed's party. The condition was that he'd only go as far as the end of the road the caravans used.

Scar followed in silence. It was the third day since they'd left Ishval. The old road marked in the ancient land records faded the farther east they went. The wells had been filled in. The marker stones were broken.

When Hashim brushed away the sand, a flat stone showed. Ed touched its edge. It hadn't worn down naturally. It had been broken at regular intervals.

"They tore up the road."

Scar looked up. "It did not simply disappear."

"Somebody made sure no one could find their way back."

Ed started to rush ahead, then shook his empty canteen. "...Let's rest a bit."

The three of them sat in the shade of a rock. While Hashim shared out the water, Ed glanced at Scar. Scar was silently watching the eastern horizon. Ed screwed the cap back onto his canteen.

"You said you'd follow the road your brother walked. What does that actually mean?"

Scar turned his head. "Where my brother went."

He looked east again. "And why."

He paused, then went on. "I have wondered for a long time. Why my brother, an Ishvalan, dug into the forbidden."

Ed didn't reply.

"I want to see what my brother saw. Then I will judge."

This time, Ed looked away first. Silence passed.

Scar asked, "And you?"

"What about me?"

"What are you searching for?"

Ed thought for a moment. At first it had been Ishvala. Then the stone. Then Scar's brother's records. But the farther east he went, the more the questions multiplied. Amestrian alchemy. Xingese alkahestry. The records of ancient Ishval. People in different places who'd tried to understand the world in different ways.

"I don't know."

Scar looked at him. "You do not know?"

"That's why I'm going."

Ed grinned. "Maybe there's something else I don't know out there."

"Is that the reason?"

"It's reason enough."

Scar looked away. "That is a reason like you."

"I'll take that as a compliment."

"I did not compliment you."

"Shut up."

* * *

After the rest, they set out again. Before long, the terrain changed. Black rocks began to appear in the sand. Further east, huge cliffs split the desert.

Hashim slowed. "From here on, I don't know the way."

Ed turned. "Aren't you a guide?"

"I'm a guide where there is a road."

Hashim pointed ahead. Between two cliffs, there was a narrow gap. "Past that, there's no road."

Scar unfolded the map. The direction was right.

"Then this is as far as I go," Hashim said. "On my way back, I'll ask around the northern caravans. That road leads to Xing, so when your business is done, come out that way."

"Thanks."

Ed accepted a canteen. Hashim shared the rest of his water and headed back the way he'd come. Ed looked out at the canyon.

The wind blew. It was strangely cool for the middle of the desert. And—Ed lifted his head.

"Did you hear that?"

Scar stopped too. *Drip, drip.* A faint sound.

"Water?"

The two of them went into the canyon. The deeper they went between the cliffs, the colder the wind got. The sound of water grew gradually clearer. But before long, the path was blocked. Huge boulders sealed the passage.

Scar said, "It seems to be the end."

Ed didn't answer. He walked up to the wall.

"Fullmetal."

"Wait."

Between the rocks there was a thin groove. It looked like a natural crack. But it was too regular. Ed followed the line with his finger. Where it reached the bottom of the wall, it split in two on the ground.

"This is..."

At that instant. *Wooom.* A low vibration rumbled from beneath their feet.

"Ed!"

Scar yanked him back by the collar. *Boom!* A pillar of stone shot up from the spot where Ed had just been standing. Ed rolled across the ground.

"What the—?!"

People appeared on the cliff above. One. Two. Three. People in desert-colored clothes, looking down at them.

"Who are you?!" Ed shouted.

No answer. Only the young man in front grinned. He looked about Ed's age.

"You can't go in from here," he said lazily. "If the road's blocked, isn't it polite to turn around?"

One of the others put a hand on the wall. Ed's eyes went straight to that hand. Beneath it, there was a line. A transmutation circle—no. There was no circle. No completed figure. It was only part of a line running across the wall.

"Wait."

The wall moved.

"Run!"

Scar shoved Ed. Boulders pressed in from both sides and narrowed the passage. Ed and Scar sprinted forward together.

"Is that alchemy?" Scar asked.

"I don't know!"

"Alkahestry?"

"It doesn't look like that, either!"

Ahead of them, the ground split. Stone thrust up. Ed's hands came up on reflex. They almost clapped together—and stopped. Clap, press it to the ground, and it's done. His body still remembered. But now...

"Damn it!"

Ed threw himself aside. Scar's right arm struck the stone pillar. Deconstruction. The stone crumbled. Ed rolled through the gap.

"Behind you!"

This time it was water. Water poured from the middle of the canyon wall. It split quickly along the grooves in the floor.

"Why is water coming from up there—"

Ed broke off. The water was moving strangely. It wasn't flowing only downhill. It scattered between wall and floor along a network of grooves.

At that moment, the wall on the right began to rumble. Ed yelled, "Scar! Left!"

Scar twisted away. A chunk of stone grazed the spot where his head had just been.

"How did you know?"

"That line!"

"Which line?"

"On the floor!"

The man above moved his hand again. This time Ed didn't watch the man. He watched the line that ran out from under that hand. The wall. The floor. The channel.

"Three steps back!"

"Are you sure?"

"Probably!"

"Probably?"

"Just go!"

Scar moved. *Boom!* The ground where he'd been standing heaved upward. Ed laughed. "I was right!"

"You said that without being certain?"

"Results are what matter!"

Up on the cliff, the movements of the people paused for a moment. They seemed to have noticed that Ed was reading their structure.

"Huh."

The young man at the front muttered. "That kid's looking at the lines?"

Another one ran to the opposite wall and laid a hand on it. Ed followed the line. From the wall down. Across the floor. Into the channel.

"Scar!"

"Where?"

"Cut that waterway!"

Scar put his right hand to the ground. *Crack!* One side of the channel collapsed. The water spread sideways. At the same time, the moving stone wall stopped. Ed's eyes lit up.

"...I knew it."

But then. *Wooom.* A sound came from the other side.

"What?"

It was a place where no water flowed. A strong wind swept through the canyon. A thin stone slab standing on top of the wall shook. Another line ran from beneath it. *Boom!* This time the ground behind them heaved. Scar pulled Ed back. They rolled together.

"Fullmetal."

"Wait a second."

"What did you figure out?"

Ed looked back and forth between the cut channel and the wall that had moved.

"It's not... the water."

"Then what?"

"I don't know."

Ed's eyes moved fast. The wall. The floor. The water. The wind. The stone. And the lines.

"But something's off."

"What is?"

Ed looked at the stone pillar that had first shot up. There was something an alchemist always had to check first. What had been deconstructed. What it had been reconstructed into. Where did the material come from. What changed. The pillar. The floor. The wall. The grin vanished from Ed's face.

"...The numbers don't add up."

"What numbers?"

There wasn't time to answer. A chunk of stone fell from above.

"Damn!"

Ed ran. Scar moved at the same time. Ed didn't watch the attackers. He watched the floor.

"Right wall!"

Scar raised his right arm.

"The line, not the person!"

Scar changed direction immediately. He destroyed the groove at the base of the wall. The stone split. The falling chunk veered aside.

"That's it!"

"What is?"

"I don't know either!"

"..."

"But it's working!"

A vein stood out on Scar's forehead. "Explain it later."

"If I knew—!"

This time Ed ran first. He moved to avoid the lines on the floor. Scar followed. When a stone wall rose, Ed read its direction. Scar broke only what needed to be broken. Water flowed. Wind passed through. Stone moved. Cut one, and one stopped, but another kept moving somewhere else.

There was no center. At least, not to Ed's eyes. Where was the circle? Who was transmuting? What was the price? None of it fit.

Then a voice came from above. "Stop."

All movement ceased. The young man at the front pouted. "Just when it was getting fun."

"Daud."

The voice above called again, lower.

"Yeah, yeah."

The young man lowered his hand. Only the sound of water remained.

A little later, a man came down from the cliff. He looked about sixty. A sunburned face. Behind him followed Daud and another young man. The man looked at Scar's red eyes for a moment. His eyes seemed to be recalling an Ishvalan who had visited this place years ago. Then he looked at Ed.

"Who are you?"

Scar stepped forward. "I came to find someone."

"Who?"

"A scholar from Ishval. My brother."

"I want to know whether he came here."

The man's gaze stopped on Scar's face. Only for a moment. But Scar didn't miss it.

"You know him."

The man didn't answer. Then Ed cut in. "Before that."

Scar turned. "Be quiet, Fullmetal."

"Just one thing."

"..."

Ed pointed around them. "What was that back there?"

The man narrowed his eyes. "What was what?"

"The stones moving. The water moving."

Ed pointed at the floor. "Are those lines transmutation circles?"

No answer.

"Alchemy?"

Silence.

"Alkahestry?"

Still no answer. Ed stepped closer. "It's not, is it?"

The man's expression changed a little. Ed spoke fast. "I can't see any circle, and when we cut the water, one thing stopped but another kept moving. The water isn't the source. Some of it moved after the wind went through."

"..."

"And the strangest part." Ed pointed at the stone pillar that had risen. "What did you use?"

"What do you mean?"

"You moved this."

Ed spread his hands in frustration. "If you changed this much, you must have used something."

The man studied Ed quietly. "We used nothing."

Ed's face froze. "...What?"

Behind him, Daud snickered. "Weird thing to ask."

"What's weird about it?!"

Ed took a step forward. "How could this possibly happen without using anything—"

"Be quiet, Fullmetal," Scar said, stopping him. "My brother comes first."

"No, this is really important too!"

"Not to me."

The man looked from one to the other. Then he studied Scar's face for a long time. "You said you are a scholar from Ishval?"

"Yes."

"He asked a lot of questions."

The man nodded. "Follow me."

Scar's eyes changed. "You know my brother."

The man didn't answer. He turned and walked toward the blocked cliff face. He put a hand to the wall. Ed's gaze followed at once.

"Wait."

Lines again. They seemed to start from beneath his hand. But no. They were already there. On the wall. On the floor. In the channel a little way off. The man touched something.

*Wooom.* A section of the huge cliff began to move slowly. A path appeared behind it. And beyond that, stone houses lined the cliffs. Channels ran between the houses. Small fields lay among the rocks. Water poured down from above. People lived there.

Scar looked at the sight in silence. The end of the road his brother had walked.

But Ed noticed something else before the village. The walls. The channels. The floor. The pillars. Lines left everywhere. The same structure that had attacked them was spread across the whole village.

Ed murmured, "...Is it all connected?"

The man turned. "What?"

"Nothing."

Ed couldn't take his eyes off it. It moved stone. It moved water. It reacted to wind. There was no transmutation circle in sight. He couldn't tell what had been paid. It wasn't alchemy. It wasn't Xing's alkahestry either. At least, not in any way he knew.

It made no sense. And so the corner of Ed's mouth lifted.

Scar looked at his face. "You look pleased."

"Of course I am."

"A moment ago, you almost died."

"What's that got to do with anything?"

"Usually, it has something to do with it."

"Shut up."

The man said, "I am Nadir."

He turned toward the inside of the village. "I would say welcome to Karsa, but that would be difficult."

Scar followed. Ed didn't move. He was looking at the lines on the ground. Scar looked back. "Are you not coming?"

"Go ahead."

Even so, Ed glanced back at the stone pillar one last time. "...The numbers don't add up."

"What numbers have you been talking about?"

Ed thought for a moment. Then he shook his head. "I don't know yet."

He stepped into Karsa. Water flowed along the channels. Wind passed between the cliffs. Somewhere, stone gave a low hum. Ed looked at it all, one by one. There was something he didn't know. Right in front of him. And perhaps one of the things he'd taken for granted was wrong.

Ed laughed. "This just got interesting."

Scar said from ahead, "I do not find it so."

"I know."

They walked into Karsa along the same road. But Scar was looking at the road his brother must have walked. And Ed was looking at the flowing water.
