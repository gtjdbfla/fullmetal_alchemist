# Chapter 39: A Country Without an Emperor

**Lansi, Xing.**

Even after the swords were lowered, the problems remained.

Three An. Five Bai. That was how many had died in a matter of days. Far more had been wounded. Shops had burned, houses had been wrecked, and there were deaths whose killers were still unknown. Who had killed the Bai merchant. Who had set fire to the Bai shop. In the end, neither was ever found out.

Ling didn't force them into a single story. What was unknown, he left unknown.

Luhan was confined to a room in the imperial residence. He didn't try to escape. He didn't ask for forgiveness either. He had been like that ever since he laid down his sword. He had lost to Ling. It wasn't that he had admitted he was wrong. It wasn't that he had found a different answer either.

It was just—over.

* * *

"Your Majesty."

An official came into the meeting room and set down a thick stack of papers. "Both the An and the Bai have sent their demands."

Ling looked at the papers. One bundle. Another bundle. And another beside that.

"This is all of it?"

"As of this morning, yes."

"As of this morning?"

"Yes."

Ling covered his face. "Maybe I should quit being emperor."

"Not a bad idea," said Ed, sitting beside him.

Ling looked at him through his fingers. "Aren't you supposed to try and stop me a little?"

"A few days ago, people were swinging swords because some guy wanted to be emperor."

"You really can't comfort anyone, can you?"

"Did you call me here to be comforted?"

"No." Ling spread out the papers. "I called you here to look at these."

The An's demands. The range of travel on the southern trade road. Well rights. Compensation for pasture and land. A way to keep the imperial court from going back on its promises.

The Bai's demands. Protection for Lansi's trade. Preventing a sudden decline of the northern trade network. Compensation for damage from the attacks. A guaranteed right to self-defense.

Ling turned a page. "None of it's exactly wrong."

"That's what makes it a pain."

"Right."

Ed tipped his chair back. "So what are you going to do?"

Ling didn't answer. The imperial seal lay on the desk. There were several documents that would be settled with just a stamp. Ling picked up the seal. He looked down at it for a while. Then put it down again.

"This time, I'm not going to stamp anything."

Ed's chair came back down to the floor. "Why?"

"If I decide, it's fast."

"Sure."

"And I'm confident I'd do a good job."

"You've still got plenty of confidence."

"I'm the Emperor." Ling grinned. Then the grin went away. "I think that's the problem."

* * *

In the afternoon, people gathered.

From the An came representatives, including Chenluo and Xiaolin. Bandages were still wrapped under Chenluo's sleeve. Nine of the twelve An who had been detained for taking Zhenha's money had been released, and they sat in the back row.

From the Bai came merchants and elders. Zhenha was there too. He had given up his seat as head of the merchant house and was sitting as an elder. Luhan's seat was empty.

As soon as the meeting started, Chenluo asked, "Is the southern trade road going to be opened in the end?"

"That's what we're here to decide," Ling said.

"Isn't Your Majesty the one who decides?"

"No."

The meeting room went quiet.

A Bai merchant spoke first. "Then who decides?"

Ling thought for a moment. "We have to decide that too."

Ed, leaning against the wall, closed his eyes.

Ling saw it. "What?"

"Nothing. Just sounds like something I've heard before."

Ling understood. *Then I'll have to decide how to decide that first. You again?*

Ling cleared his throat. "This time, I'm not going to decide."

"Isn't that something Your Majesty decided too?" Chenluo asked.

Ling's mouth fell open. "...Well, if you put it that way, I guess it is."

Xiaolin held back a laugh. Chenluo didn't laugh.

"We were told to trust the Emperor's good will."

Ling's expression changed.

"We were told Your Majesty would guarantee it."

"I know."

"So I'm asking," Chenluo said. "Will the next emperor keep it?"

Ling didn't answer. It was a question he'd heard before. Back then, he hadn't been able to answer. He could say he would keep it as long as he lived. But after that? What if the next emperor changed his mind? Was he supposed to hope a better emperor would come along?

"I remember that question," Ling said.

"I never heard an answer."

"Because I couldn't give one."

"Do you know now?"

Ling thought for a moment. "A little."

* * *

Ling came out in front of the desk. "Whether to open the southern trade road. How far the wells will be shared. Where the pasture boundaries go. What compensation to give the Lansi merchants." He folded down his fingers one by one. "Up to now, I've ended up deciding all of it."

"Because you are the Emperor," Zhenha said.

"Right." Ling looked at him. "That's why Kairun tried to kill me."

The air went stiff.

"Because he thought it'd be enough if the emperor were gone."

Zhenha's face darkened.

"Luhan thought the opposite."

No one spoke.

Ling remembered the sword from a few days ago. The blade thrusting toward him. Lan Fan blocking the way. And at the end, the sword he'd had to draw himself.

"He thought if he became emperor himself, he could protect the Bai," Ling said. "The two of them were complete opposites. One tried to get rid of the emperor. One tried to become emperor. And one just hoped nothing would change."

Zhenha lowered his eyes.

Ling looked at the seal on the desk. "But all three of them thought the emperor mattered too much."

The meeting room was quiet.

"So did I."

Zhenha raised his head. "Your Majesty."

"I thought all I had to do was decide well," Ling said. "Listen to everyone. Look at it fairly. Pick a good answer." He shrugged. "And that would make me a good emperor."

"Was that wrong?" Xiaolin asked.

"It's not enough," Ling answered. "If it's a country that has to check every time whether I'm a good emperor or not..." He nudged the seal with his finger. "...what are you going to do next time?"

No one could answer.

* * *

"Then what is it you intend to do?" Chenluo asked.

"Decide what I won't decide."

"...So Your Majesty is still deciding."

"That's why I keep saying it's hard to put into words."

Ed snorted.

Ling unfolded a paper he'd prepared. "Land, water, travel, and compensation issues directly connected to the southern trade road." He touched the items with his finger. "I want to take these out of the imperial court's sole authority."

Faces changed.

"An representatives. Bai representatives. Lansi merchants. People from the areas the trade road passes through. And the imperial court," Ling said. "I'm going to create a place where they decide together."

A Bai elder asked, "Does the imperial court give final approval?"

Ling closed his mouth.

"If you do that," Ed said. People looked at him. "Then it's the same thing in the end."

Ling nodded. "Right."

The Bai elder asked again, "Then Your Majesty couldn't even refuse?"

"That's something we have to decide too."

Zhenha shifted. "Your Majesty."

"What?"

"You mean to reduce the Emperor's powers yourself?"

"Yeah."

It was far too simple an answer.

"Why?"

Ling thought for a moment. "Because the next emperor can't be me."

No one could go on.

"I remember what promises I've made," Ling said. "But I don't know if the next emperor will. And the one after that, even less."

Chenluo looked at him.

"So instead of making the emperor remember the promise..." Ling pressed the paper with his finger. "...we have to make it stay even if the emperor forgets."

Xiaolin raised her hand. "How long will these talks take?"

"I don't know."

"In the meantime, people in our village keep leaving."

The room went quiet.

"I know," Ling answered. "That's why I'm going to set separately what we can do before the talks are done. Starting with support for the cost of transporting Haojin's herbs."

"A temporary measure?"

"Yeah. Say it's temporary, out loud, in writing."

Xiaolin smiled. A smile close to tears. "...I like that it's honest."

For the first time, Chenluo spoke up. "Kairun's village."

"The people who escorted him came back." Ling turned over a sheet. "They say only one well is still working. I put that at the top of the temporary support list too."

Chenluo didn't answer. After a moment, he bowed his head. "...I won't say thank you."

"I know."

From the back row on the Bai side, the old man from the relay village raised his hand. "My grandson didn't get hurt because of the road."

"I know."

"But whenever there's talk of the road, I'm afraid."

"It makes sense to be afraid. That's why we'll hear that side first."

The old man looked at Ling for a long time, then bowed his head. "...Then that's enough."

* * *

After the meeting, Ed went out into the corridor with Ling.

"Not bad."

"What?"

"For an emperor."

"If you're going to compliment me, just do it."

"Don't want to."

Ling snorted.

Chenluo was standing at the end of the corridor. He was waiting for Ling.

"Something to say?"

Chenluo came closer. "Let me ask one thing."

"Sure."

"I hear Your Majesty says the An and the Bai are both Your Majesty's people."

The smile left Ling's face.

"Who said I was your people?" Chenluo said.

Silence settled over the corridor. Ed looked at Ling.

Ling didn't answer right away. Because he was the emperor. Because they were the people of Xing. It would have been easy to answer that way. But he'd said it a few days ago too. *The An. And the Bai. They're both my people.* He hadn't thought it was wrong. Because of those words, Luhan had chosen a different answer.

After a long while, Ling said, "...You're right."

Chenluo narrowed his eyes.

"I was deciding that too."

"Calling us that with good intentions doesn't change it."

"I know."

"Do you really?"

Ling looked back. There were still people in the meeting room. An. Bai. Lansi merchants. Imperial officials.

"I'll fix it."

"Just with words?"

"No," Ling said. "With a way to do it."

Chenluo looked at him for a long time, then turned away. "I'll be watching."

"Do that."

He walked off.

"Not many people talk to the Emperor like that, huh?" Ed said.

"There've suddenly been a lot of them lately."

"Isn't that a good thing?"

"I wish you were one less of them."

"No."

* * *

On the steps outside the meeting hall, Xiaolin was waiting for Ed. "Sorry for grabbing your sleeve that time."

"It's fine." Ed leaned on the railing. "I'm the one who brought up the road first."

"I know."

"...You knew?"

"But you only asked."

Ed screwed up his face. "I told you I hate that line."

"It was us who wanted the road. And I was the one who said it was good," Xiaolin said. "So watch it with us to the end."

"...Yeah."

At the bottom of the steps, Furong was looking up. "I've come to settle up."

"Money again?"

"I won't take anything for this one."

Ed blinked. "...Are you sick?"

"I sold information, and swords came out before a road even appeared." Furong shrugged. "If a merchant's last deal sits badly with him, the next deal doesn't happen."

* * *

That evening, Ling went to see Luhan.

Luhan was sitting by the window. When the door opened, he stood. "Your Majesty."

"Sit."

Luhan didn't sit. Ling didn't make him either.

"We had a meeting today."

"I heard."

"Bai representatives came too."

Luhan's eyes moved very slightly. "I'm glad."

"Aren't you curious?"

"Do I have the right to ask?"

"The trial will decide that." Ling leaned against the wall. "I just came to tell you."

Luhan said nothing.

"I'm going to reduce what the emperor decides."

Only then did Luhan look at Ling. "Why?"

"Because of you."

Luhan's face stiffened.

"And because of Kairun."

"..."

"You both gave me an answer. You were both wrong."

Luhan's eyes dropped. "Is that so."

"Yeah," Ling said. "But I think I understand now why you were wrong."

Luhan slowly raised his head.

"I don't want to make a country where someone has to take up a sword again," Ling said, "over which clan the next emperor comes from."

Luhan's lips moved. No words came out.

"What you did doesn't go away."

"I know."

"And what the Bai were afraid of doesn't go away either."

Luhan didn't answer.

"So I'm going to keep both."

"Keep what?"

"Your crime," Ling said. "And why the Bai followed you."

Luhan closed his eyes. "Is that possible?"

"I don't know," Ling answered.

Luhan opened his eyes. "Your Majesty says that?"

"I learned it recently." Ling grinned. "Some things you shouldn't decide if you don't know."

Luhan didn't smile. But he didn't look away either.

Ling headed for the door. "Luhan."

"Yes."

"You said you had to become emperor."

Luhan's face stiffened. "...Yes."

"Do you still think so?"

A long silence. Luhan looked at the floor. "I don't know."

Ling nodded. "Okay."

That was enough. For now.

* * *

That night, Zhenha came to see Luhan. It was a visit Ling had allowed. A guard stood at the door.

Luhan had been sitting by the window. He got up. "Elder."

"Please sit."

"I don't have the right to sit."

Zhenha didn't respond, and sat down in the chair across from him.

"I heard you only held the line," Luhan said first. "You kept the guards' line."

"And I heard you tried to stop me."

"I tried to stop you, but I didn't follow." Zhenha spoke evenly. "Even now, I believe the way things are is best. The Bai are alive, the warehouses keep running, and people eat. I won't take the lead in anything that shakes that."

"The way things are killed a merchant."

"...Did it?"

"All you told us was to hold on. Even when the merchant died."

"Because we held on, more people may have lived. Or perhaps not," Zhenha said. "I don't know. Holding on without knowing was my work."

Luhan said nothing for a while. "Did I cross the line because of the power you taught me?"

"No." Zhenha's answer was fast. "What I taught was power. What used it was you, Lord Luhan." He looked down at his own hands for a moment. "I sent money too. Not knowing a man named Kairun doesn't mean that money didn't reach anywhere."

"...Do you regret it?"

"I'm past the age when regret is any use." Zhenha stood up. "But keeping the Bai from taking up swords against each other. That's probably all I can protect now."

The door closed. Luhan looked at the closed door for a long time.

* * *

A few days later, the first document stamped with the imperial seal came out.

It wasn't an order to open the southern trade road. It wasn't an order to accept the An's demands. It wasn't an order guaranteeing the rights of the Bai.

*Issues of land, water resources, travel, and compensation related to the southern trade road are hereby removed from the sole authority of the imperial court.*

Below it were written the principles for forming the new council.

It wasn't a finished system. The emperor hadn't disappeared. The imperial court was still strong. Ling was still emperor.

But for the first time, the emperor's order was one that kept the emperor from giving orders alone from now on.

Ed looked at the document for a long time. "That's weird."

"What is?" Ling asked.

"You're stamping the emperor's seal on an order that cuts the emperor's powers."

Ling picked up the seal. "Should I use it a lot one last time?"

"Don't."

*Thunk.* The seal came down.

Ling nodded, satisfied.

Ed looked at the paper again. *Who decides?* He took out his notebook. He wrote one line.

*A good decision and a good way of deciding aren't the same thing.*

He looked at it for a moment and added another line.

*Rather than a country that needs a good emperor, a country that can hold up even when a bad one comes.*

"What are you writing?" Ling leaned over.

Ed closed the notebook. "Not showing you."

"It's about me, isn't it?"

"So?"

"Hey."

Ed went out the door first. Ling's voice followed him. "Ed!"

People passed by out in the corridor. There were An. There were Bai. There were imperial officials. They didn't trust each other yet. The road wasn't open yet. The dead didn't come back. The emperor hadn't disappeared.

But at least where the next road would go would not be decided by one person alone.

That was the first thing that changed in Xing.
