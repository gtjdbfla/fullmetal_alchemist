# Chapter 14: What His Brother Wanted

The next morning, Scar went back to Majid's house. This time Ed didn't follow.

The night before, Scar had said it first. "Tomorrow I go alone."

"Why?"

"I am going to hear more from Majid about my brother."

"I want to hear too."

Scar looked at Ed. "Are you not looking for your father?"

"I'm not looking for him, I'm following the traces—"

"Then do that."

"...Are you kicking me out?"

"Yes."

The answer came far too fast. Ed opened his mouth, then closed it. "Fine."

And when morning came, he really did go to the record room.

* * *

Majid was sitting by the channel in front of his house, scraping sand out of it with a small hook.

When Scar approached, Majid looked up. "You came back."

"I have something to ask."

"Brothers, all right."

Scar's eyebrow moved.

"Sit."

Scar sat down beside Majid. The water flowed slowly in front of them.

"When my brother left this place," Scar said, "what did he say?"

Majid put down the hook. "That he was going east."

"That much I know."

"You want to know more?"

"What he meant to do after he returned."

Majid thought for a moment. "I asked him that too."

Scar looked at him.

"He had a lot of plans."

"Plans?"

"He said he wanted to build channels. He wanted to try out ways to use wells more efficiently. He said if he learned more medicine in the east, he wanted to see whether it could be used in Ishval." Majid folded down his fingers one by one. "He even thought about how to get the merchants who cross the desert to come to Ishval again."

"Trade?"

"Yes."

Scar looked at him as if a little surprised. "My brother thought even of that."

"Just because a man's a scholar doesn't mean he lives without eating," Majid said. "Same goes for a village."

He looked down at the water in the channel and was quiet for a long time. "He was a man who wanted to do so many things." His hand slowed on the hook. "...Seems he didn't get to do them all."

Majid smiled bitterly. Scar didn't answer.

* * *

For a while there was only the sound of water.

"Did my brother not mean to bring back the old Ishval?" Scar asked.

Majid looked at him. "Why do you think that?"

"My brother studied many old records."

"He did."

"Our faith as well."

"That too."

"Lost knowledge."

Majid nodded. "But looking for old things and going back to the old days aren't the same."

Scar didn't answer.

Majid pointed at the wall of the channel. Grooves from many different eras overlapped there. The old ones were blocked. The new ones led off in other directions.

"Look at that."

"The channel?"

"In the old days, it flowed that way."

"Not now."

"Because the people there were gone." Majid pointed a little higher up. "So we cut a new path toward where the houses went up instead."

"You abandoned the old path?"

"We left it, didn't we?" Majid smiled. "Remembering something and living exactly as it was are different things."

Scar's gaze lingered on the old groove.

* * *

Around noon, in the record room, Ed wrote *Xerxes, Karsa, east* in his notebook and asked Sahara, "Is the road the traveler took east still there?"

"Part of it we erased, and part of it is buried in the desert. There are records of it leading as far as present-day Xing."

"Show me."

Sahara didn't move. "First you have to see Nadir. We're the people who erased that road."

Ed closed his mouth.

* * *

In the afternoon, Majid was in Nadir's room too. So was Scar.

The moment Ed sat down, he could feel something was off. "Why is everyone here?"

"Because it's time for you to leave," Nadir said.

"You're kicking us out?"

"If that's how you want to hear it."

"No," Ed corrected himself at once. "Let's just say it's time to go."

Nadir spread out an old map. A road leading east from Karsa. It was broken off here and there, but the direction was clear.

Ed leaned forward. "Is this the old trade road?"

"One of them."

"If I follow this, do I get to Xing?"

"In the old days."

"And now?"

"You can't go that way."

Ed looked up. "Why?"

"Because we won't open it for you."

The room went quiet.

* * *

Ed looked at the map for a moment. "I'm not asking you to tell me where the road is."

"That is also opening the road."

"I just need to pass through."

Nadir looked at Ed.

Ed stopped mid-sentence. He remembered what Sahara had said when she told them why they had erased the road.

> People didn't bring only goods along a road.

Ed scratched his head. "...Yeah. She did say that."

"You remember."

"Then is there another road?"

"There is."

"One that gets to Xing?"

"You'd have to go the long way around."

"How long?"

"Several days."

"Then I'll go that way," Ed said immediately.

Nadir looked a little surprised. "You give up that easily?"

"Give up what?"

"The old road."

Ed looked at the map. Before, he would have asked more. Why not? Where exactly? How had they blocked it? Was there another entrance?

But—

"It's your road," Ed said. "If you don't want it opened, why would I open it?"

Nadir looked at Ed for a moment.

"But," Ed added, "you said there are people who want to open it again."

"There are."

"Are you going to keep fighting them?"

Nadir's expression hardened. "I don't know."

"..."

"Didn't I tell you it was hard?"

Ed nodded. "Yeah, that's true."

* * *

Majid spoke up. "I think it's all right to open it again."

Ed looked at him. Nadir wasn't surprised. He seemed to know already.

"Not all of it, maybe," Majid said.

"Why?"

"Because Karsa survived."

Ed blinked. "How is that a reason to open it?"

"We succeeded in surviving. So now we can ask about other things."

Scar looked at Majid. It was close to what he had heard yesterday.

Majid went on. "We erased the road, and we protected ourselves."

Outside armies had a harder time finding them. They hadn't been pushed around by merchants. No officials from other countries had come. Even when wars passed by, Karsa had remained.

"But we lost things too."

Goods had become scarce. There had been times when medicine was hard to come by. Some of the young people wanted to go outside. Some wanted to learn the techniques of the outside world. And on the other hand, there were people afraid of outsiders coming in again.

"So you are saying it must be opened?" Scar asked.

"No," Majid said. "I'm saying we have to think about it."

"And the answer?"

"It shouldn't be me who decides that, should it?"

Something moved slightly in Scar's eyes.

* * *

"Majid and I have argued about this for a long time," Nadir said.

Majid smiled. "We still do."

"I have no intention of opening the old road yet."

"I know."

"And my mind may not change as long as I live."

"I know that too."

Ed looked back and forth between them. "But you're sitting together."

"There's no need to kill each other," Nadir said.

"That's a pretty low bar."

Majid laughed. Scar didn't.

Instead, he looked at the map of Karsa. The people who had erased the road. The people who wanted to open it again. The young people who wanted to go outside. The people who wanted to stay as they were. None of them was all of Karsa.

Suddenly, he thought of the meeting in Ishval. Adin. The young people who wanted to survey the mineral. The elders who said the sacred land had to be protected. The people afraid Amestris would take it away again. And Zahir. The man who had said that even a good order was still an order.

"In the end, who decides?" Scar asked.

"We do, I suppose," Nadir said.

"We?"

"The people of Karsa."

"Your opinions differ."

"That's why it takes so long."

Scar said nothing for a long while.

* * *

After the meeting, Majid called Scar over. "Let me tell you one more thing."

"What is it?"

"The last day I talked with your brother."

Scar turned around.

"It was before he left for the east."

Majid said he had asked Scar's brother that day why he had to go even farther east. Hadn't he already learned plenty in Karsa?

Scar's brother had answered:

> "Because there is still so much I do not know."

Majid smiled. "I understood that. So I asked him again how long he meant to keep looking."

Scar waited.

> "Until I know enough that, when I go back, people can choose."

Scar's expression changed. "Choose."

"That's right," Majid said.

> "Until now, there have been too many things we could not do. Because there was no water. Because there was no knowledge. Because of war. Because we were poor."

And last of all:

> "At the very least, I want there to be fewer things people give up because they cannot do them."

Scar lowered his head without a word.

He remembered what Adin had said.

> If you want to stay but you leave because there's no work, that isn't a choice.

A completely different person. A completely different time. And yet—the questions were alike.

* * *

As the sun went down, Ed was packing. He was getting ready to head east by the long way around.

The door opened, and Scar came in.

Ed asked as he shoved books into his bag. "Done talking with Majid?"

"Yes."

"They said we can leave tomorrow morning."

"I know."

"The long way's pretty long, but Sahara says there's somewhere to get water along the way. If we meet a caravan there—"

"Edward."

Ed's hand stopped. Scar rarely called him by his name.

"What?"

"I am not going."

Ed looked at Scar. "Going where?"

"East."

A beat.

"You're not going to Xing?"

"No."

Ed looked at Scar for a moment. "Your brother went."

"I know."

"You said you wanted to know how far he got."

"I do."

"Then why?"

Scar looked into the distance. "What I came to find was not the road my brother walked."

Ed said nothing.

"I wanted to know why my brother walked that road," Scar said.

"...Did you find out?"

"Yes."

Ed thought for a moment. Then he put his bag down. "Ishval."

Scar nodded. "The place my brother wanted to go was not Xing. Nor was the knowledge of the east his goal."

Alchemy. Alkahestry. Karsa's techniques. All of it—

"It was something to bring back."

"Bring back where?" Ed asked, even though he knew the answer.

"To Ishval." Scar looked out the window. "My brother left so that he could return."

The war hadn't given his brother what came next. Not the time to put what he had learned to real use. Not the time to build the Ishval that was to come.

But Scar was alive.

"So you're going back?"

"Yes."

"To do what your brother wanted to do?"

Scar was silent for a moment. "No."

Ed looked a little surprised. "No?"

"I know now what my brother wanted," Scar said. "But my brother cannot decide what the people of Ishval want now."

Ed's eyes widened slightly.

Scar went on. "Nor can I."

There were things he had seen in Karsa. Majid wanted to open the road. Nadir didn't. Both of them wanted it for Karsa's sake. Neither side was Karsa's answer. Ishval was the same.

"Then what are you going to do?" Ed asked.

Scar thought for a moment. "I suppose I must ask."

"Ask who?"

"The people who are alive."

Ed grinned. "That's going to take a long time."

"I know."

"There might be a ton of fighting."

"I know."

"And there's Zahir."

"I know."

"And Adin."

"That I know as well."

Ed looked at Scar for a moment. "Sounds like a pain."

"It is."

"And you're still going?"

Instead of answering, Scar adjusted the strap of his pack. "That is why I am going."

* * *

The next morning, two roads split from Karsa, one east and one west.

Ed stood at the start of the long road east. Scar got ready to cross the desert they had come through once more.

Majid handed Scar a small water skin. "Since I never got it back from your brother."

"Got what back?" Scar asked.

"The water skin I lent him."

Scar looked at it for a moment. "I will return it to you."

"I lent it to your brother." Majid let go of it. "You just take it."

Scar took the water skin. "Thank you."

"What do you plan to do when you get there?" Majid asked.

Scar looked west. "I do not know yet."

Majid smiled. "Good."

Scar looked at him.

"Your brother didn't know at first either."

* * *

Ed was waiting beside them. "All done?"

"Yes."

"Then..."

They looked at each other for a moment.

"When you get to Ishval, tell Miles something for me," Ed said.

"What?"

"When the mineral survey results come out, send them to me too."

"Is that all?"

"And tell Adin not to dig too fast."

"What else?"

"If Zahir starts saying weird stuff again—"

"Come and say it yourself."

"I'm going to Xing right now!"

"Then say it when you come back."

Ed scowled. "You really can't talk to this guy."

Scar turned away. A few steps.

"Scar," Ed called.

Scar stopped.

"About your brother."

Scar looked back.

Ed scratched his head. "I'm glad you found him."

Scar looked at him for a moment. "Yes."

And he walked on. That was all.

* * *

Ed watched Scar grow smaller beyond the desert. Going in the opposite direction from the one his brother had taken, back to the living people of Ishval.

Ed adjusted his bag. Inside it was the record of a man who had come from Xerxes four hundred years ago, learned in Karsa, and left for the east. It might be his father. He needed to find out what that man had learned in Xing, and what he had left behind.

Whether to open Karsa's road or keep it closed was for the people of Karsa to decide. Ed went around it and walked east.
