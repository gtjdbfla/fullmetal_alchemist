# Chapter 36: The Last Shot

**Ishval, Amestris.**

The gunfire was diminishing. An entire day passed without a death. Then the following morning, too.

The eastern checkpoint had been dismantled. Military supply vehicles went around the residential districts. There were fewer places where soldiers and residents met at all.

Miles put down the report.

"Two shootings overnight. Three wounded."

Mustang asked, "Any deaths?"

"None."

"Who fired first?"

"The first incident is under investigation. In the second, the two sides give different accounts."

"Good."

Miles's eyebrow moved. "Good?"

"You wrote that you don't know."

Mustang handed back the report. "That's enough."

Miles looked at him for a moment, then nodded.

Hawkeye was looking at the map on the wall. When they first arrived, red marks had spread across the city. Now it was different. There were hardly any new ones.

"No battle lines are forming," Miles said. "People have begun returning to their own districts."

"Where's Scar?"

"In the west. Talking with the residents."

Mustang picked up his coat. "Let's go."

* * *

There were more shovels than guns in the western square. Soldiers worked among the residents clearing a collapsed wall.

Scar was moving broken stones. When Mustang approached, he stopped.

"What orders today?"

"None."

"Then why are you here?"

"To see how things are."

Scar picked up another stone. "As you can see."

A little way off, two Ishvalans and a soldier were righting an overturned cart. A few days earlier, they would have been pointing guns at one another.

"People listen to you," Mustang said.

"They are not listening to me," Scar replied. "There were simply many people who wanted to stop fighting."

"Someone still had to say it first."

Scar looked at Mustang. "Do not misunderstand."

"I don't."

"It is not over yet."

"I know."

There was another small clash that afternoon. At the western entrance, a broken cart blocked the road. The residents said the military had deliberately obstructed it; a soldier said the residents had pushed forward first.

Miles stood between them.

"Lower your weapons first."

"But Colonel—"

"Are we at war?"

The soldier closed his mouth. "No, sir."

"Then do not act as though we are."

The gun barrels lowered.

Scar went to the residents. "You too. Lower them."

"They started—"

"If you fire, who fires next?"

The man couldn't answer. A moment later, the residents lowered their weapons too.

No shot rang out. Mustang gave no orders. There was no need.

* * *

Three reports lay on Graus's desk.

Western clash ended. Friction reduced after withdrawal from southern checkpoint. Nighttime shootings decreasing.

Bark stood across from him.

"The Fuhrer?"

"At the scene."

"Reinforcements in force?"

"He has not requested them."

"Deaths?"

"None since yesterday."

Graus's fingers stopped.

"Scar?"

"He has been mediating with residents alongside Colonel Miles more frequently."

Graus was silent. Mustang hadn't killed Ishvalans. He hadn't deployed a large force. Yet the war was cooling.

"Bark."

"Sir."

"Withdraw."

Bark's expression didn't change. "Is the mission over?"

"Yes."

"The original objective has not been achieved."

Graus looked at him. "The Fuhrer came."

"That was not the whole of the original objective."

The room went quiet.

"The Fuhrer was supposed to intervene in person and still fail to end the war."

"I know."

"The war is ending."

Graus's gaze fell to the map. A long time passed before he spoke.

"My prediction was wrong."

Bark's eyes moved very slightly.

"I thought Mustang wouldn't be able to end it," Graus said. "But he is."

He folded the map. "So it's over."

"We terminate with the mission unsuccessful?"

"Yes."

Silence passed. Graus spoke again.

"The order concerning civilians?"

"Still in effect."

"Repeat it."

"Do not target civilians."

"Good."

Graus waved a hand. "Bring your men back."

Bark saluted. "Sir."

* * *

Graus tore up the second copy of the telegram.

On another sheet he wrote a single line.

*Return.*

The signature was not blank this time either.

* * *

The four men returned. Bark did not.

He told his men to return as ordered. Alone, he looked out over a city from which the war was disappearing.

The Fuhrer had come. The Ishvalans had not been massacred. And yet the war was ending.

The mission had failed.

Bark calculated again.

Miles. Controlled the troops.

Hawkeye. Supported the Fuhrer.

Mustang. The center of command.

And—Scar.

An Ishvalan. Experienced in combat. A man who had once killed State Alchemists. Now he moved between residents and soldiers, making both sides lower their weapons.

He wasn't a civilian. At least, not by Bark's classification.

Bark turned away.

* * *

That night, Hawkeye didn't sleep. The reports of the first three incidents lay open on the table.

An adult man. An adult woman. A child.

They hadn't known one another. They had lived in different places. There was no obvious common thread. And in all three cases, no one had found the killer.

Hawkeye read the first report again. No witnesses.

The second. Shooter could not be identified.

The third. The same.

A gunshot had been heard. Someone had died. But no one had seen the person who fired.

Three times.

Hawkeye's hand stopped.

The shootings that followed were different. People fired at one another. Fleeing figures were seen. Some were caught. Guns were found, and traces remained.

That was what war was like. Noisy. Chaotic. People were visible.

Only the first three were different.

Too clean.

Hawkeye lifted her eyes from the reports. She knew that feeling. Long ago, in Ishval, she too had killed from a distance. Fired once, then vanished before the other side could find her.

She had considered where people would look. What they would discover. To take the next target, she could not leave herself behind.

She had been good at that. Because she was good at it, more people had died.

Hawkeye closed her eyes, then opened them. Regret wasn't what she needed now.

She looked at the reports again. There was no evidence it had been the same person. No certainty it had been the same gun. But if the three incidents were connected...

Hawkeye asked herself: What would I have done?

The answer came quickly. Being a good shot wasn't enough. You had to kill someone without leaving a trace of yourself.

Three times. Someone had managed it three times.

Perhaps it wasn't chance that they had failed to find the shooter. Perhaps an exceptional marksman had moved from the outset in ways that would keep him undiscovered.

Hawkeye looked at the locations of the three incidents. Once the war expanded, that method disappeared.

What if it hadn't disappeared? What if it had stopped? Because there was no longer any need to fire personally.

But now the war was ending again. If that person was still in Ishval, another shot might be needed.

Who would he shoot?

Mustang. No. The Fuhrer's death would be too great an event.

Miles. A soldier. His death might be understood as an attack within the military.

Hawkeye's thoughts stopped.

Scar.

An Ishvalan. He didn't trust the military. Yet he was working with it to stop people from fighting. The residents listened to him.

If he died, who had killed him would come later. Suspicion would come first.

Hawkeye stood. The soldier outside the door turned.

"Captain?"

"Where will Scar be tomorrow?"

"In the western square in the morning. The residents gather there these days."

"Every day?"

"Almost."

"And the troops?"

"Only on the perimeter. The residents are uncomfortable if we get too close."

The soldier asked, "Should we increase his escort?"

Hawkeye thought for a moment. Nothing had been proved yet.

"No."

"Then?"

"I will go and look."

* * *

The next morning, Hawkeye arrived at the western square first. She hadn't made a separate report to Mustang. There was nothing to report yet.

Whether the three shootings had the same perpetrator. Whether that person was still there. Whether Scar was the next target.

All hypotheses. She had come to check them.

Scar appeared a little later. Several residents gathered around him.

"Can we trust that the military has withdrawn?" one man asked.

"Do not trust it," Scar said.

The man frowned. "Then what are we supposed to do?"

"Watch," Scar answered. "What they do today."

Then he added, "Watch tomorrow too."

Hawkeye observed from a little distance. Nothing happened. A long time passed, and still nothing.

Perhaps she had taken her reasoning too far.

Scar moved around the square, talking to residents. Hawkeye followed, keeping her distance.

Then Scar stopped walking. Several residents followed him. It was a broad open space between ruined buildings. Few people were nearby. Scar was fully exposed.

Hawkeye stopped.

This was different from a moment ago. He was too visible.

At that instant, the three reports came back to her. Three people. Three shots. And a shooter they had never found.

She looked at Scar.

Would I fire now?

The answer came quickly.

Yes.

Hawkeye's expression changed. She took her eyes off Scar. Now she wasn't looking at him. She was looking for places from which he could be seen.

Windows. Rooftops. The upper floors of ruined buildings. The end of an alley.

Nothing.

Her eyes narrowed.

No. This was someone who had escaped discovery three times. She wouldn't be that close. Her former self wouldn't have been.

Her gaze moved farther out. Beyond the square. A spire rose between the old buildings.

Hawkeye stopped. For a while, nothing was visible. It was too quiet.

Then a tiny glint shifted. Once.

Hawkeye didn't look away. A shape emerged from the darkness. A person. And a long gun.

Strangely, she wasn't surprised. She thought she understood why he had chosen that place.

Because she knew it too.

She had known for a long time.

* * *

Inside the spire, Bark looked down.

Scar had entered the place he had been waiting for. Bark didn't hurry. A resident passed through. He waited. Someone else stepped in front of Scar. He waited again.

Scar came back into view.

Bark's hand moved.

* * *

Hawkeye was watching the spire.

Bark was watching his target.

Hawkeye was looking for the person watching the target.

The man inside the spire moved very slightly. Then he stopped. His gaze left Scar. Slowly, it moved.

Into the distance.

Toward Hawkeye.

Their eyes met.

Hawkeye knew. He had found her too.

No words were needed.

There was one shot.

* * *

Scar looked up. The residents ducked. But no one fell.

Inside the spire, Bark's body tipped sideways. The gun dropped from his hand.

The last shot intended for Scar was never fired.

Hawkeye remained still for a long time. Then she slowly lowered her weapon.

* * *

By the time Miles reached the spire, Bark was dead.

Miles checked his face. "Second Lieutenant Bark. He served under Brigadier General Graus."

Hawkeye looked at Miles. "You know him?"

"I have been in Ishval a long time. I remember most officers who have come here."

Miles looked at the gun on the floor.

"He was an excellent marksman. One of the best in his unit."

A short silence passed. His expression hardened.

"Was he responsible for the first three as well?"

Hawkeye was silent for a moment.

"I do not know."

"But you found him."

"Yes."

"Then—"

"Not yet," Hawkeye cut in.

Miles closed his mouth. After a moment, he asked, "How did you know?"

Hawkeye looked out of the spire. Scar was below.

"I thought about whom he would shoot."

"Scar?"

"Yes."

"And that led you here?"

Hawkeye didn't answer for a moment.

"I thought about where I would be watching from," she said, "if I were the shooter."

* * *

Scar was waiting below. When Hawkeye came down, he asked, "Was he aiming at me?"

"Yes."

"How did you know?"

Hawkeye looked up at the spire once.

"...I know people like him."

Scar's eyes turned to her. She didn't explain further.

After a brief silence, Scar asked, "So you killed him."

"Yes."

"Then is it over?"

Hawkeye shook her head. "That is not for me to decide."

Scar looked at her. Then he said simply, "I see."

He went back to the residents.

Hawkeye watched him go. Long ago, in Ishval, she had pulled the trigger from a distance too.

That fact did not change. One person surviving today would not change it.

Hawkeye turned her eyes away from the spire.

* * *

Graus received the report of Bark's death that evening.

"What was he doing?"

"He appears to have been aiming at Scar."

Graus closed his eyes. "Civilian casualties?"

"None."

He had told Bark to return. Bark hadn't returned.

He had told Bark not to kill civilians. To the end, Bark had obeyed that order in his own way. And he had continued the mission in his own way, too.

On the desk lay the map Graus had made. Where people would collide. Where the troops would move. Where the Fuhrer would move.

He had thought he could calculate all of it. Bark had been part of that calculation.

But at some point, the calculation had left him and begun to move on its own.

The door opened. Two soldiers entered. Mustang followed them.

Graus stood. "Your Excellency."

Mustang looked at the map on the desk. Then at Graus.

"Brigadier General Graus."

"Sir."

"Let's talk."

Graus didn't answer.

Outside, there was no sound of gunfire.

The war he had made was ending before he could end it.
