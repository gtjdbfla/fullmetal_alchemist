# Chapter 10: The People Who Erased the Road

Less than an hour after they entered Karsa, Scar lost Edward twice.

The first time was at a channel by the village entrance.

A thin line ran beside the water that flowed between the stones. The moment Ed saw it, his steps slowed, and by the time Scar looked back, he was already crouched along the channel.

"Look at this. The same line runs beside the water too."

"I am leaving you behind."

"Wait up!"

At a fork in the path, Nadir stopped. "Daud. Take these two to Sahara."

He gave Scar one more look. "That is her business."

"Me?" Daud grumbled, but Nadir disappeared down another path without answering.

The second time was in front of a line carved into a wall.

"Fullmetal."

No answer.

Ed was crouched about ten steps back. He had been tracing a shallow groove in the ground with his finger, and before he knew it his face was nearly pressed against the wall.

"Fullmetal."

"Hang on."

"You said that before."

"This time I mean it."

Daud, who was leading the way, turned around with the look of someone trying not to laugh. "Why do you keep touching that?"

Ed glanced back. "How far does this line go?"

Daud shrugged. "Follow it and you'll find out."

Ed was already looking at the wall again.

The lines weren't closed the way a transmutation circle was. Some broke off halfway up a wall. Others ran down to the ground and vanished beneath a channel. And yet, from a little distance, they connected in a strange way.

The floor of the underground prayer hall in Ishval came to mind. Lines with no clear beginning and no clear end. These weren't the same, but they were alike in that they were all connected. Whether that was a coincidence, he couldn't tell.

Wall, floor, channel, pillar.

"How far does it go...?" Ed muttered.

Scar walked past him. "I am leaving you behind."

"Wait up!" Ed sprang to his feet.

* * *

Karsa was bigger than it had looked from outside.

Stone houses climbed in tiers between reddish cliffs. Narrow bridges joined cliff to cliff, and small fields sat in every gap between the rocks.

And there was water everywhere. It ran along the channels and stone walls, disappeared under houses, and surfaced again somewhere far off. It was hard to believe they were in the middle of a desert.

Ed kept turning his head the whole way.

"Where does that water come from?"

Daud snorted. "Water's water. Where would it come from?"

"Is it groundwater? Are you using a pressure difference?"

"We bring it down from the mountains to the north," Daud said.

Ed's eyes snapped to him. "How?"

"Later." Daud jerked his chin toward Scar. "The person your friend's looking for comes first."

Only then did Ed shut his mouth. Scar followed in silence.

* * *

Daud took them to a small house deep in the village.

A woman with loosely tied black hair was waiting inside. She looked about fifty, but she was the kind of person whose face caught your attention before you could guess her age.

She looked at Scar's face for a long time. It was the look of someone remembering an Ishvalan guest who had come through this house long ago.

"Lady Sahara," Daud said, "Nadir wants you to have a look at these two."

Sahara didn't answer.

Scar spoke first. "Do you know a scholar who came from Ishval?"

Sahara raised her head. "The one with all the questions?"

"He was my brother."

There was a moment of silence. Sahara studied Scar's face for a long while, then said, "...So you're Idris's little brother."

Ed looked at Scar. "Idris?"

Scar didn't answer right away. "...That was my brother's name." His voice was low. "It has been a long time since I heard it from someone else."

"You don't look much like him," Sahara said, still watching his face. "Is he doing well these days?"

"...He is far away."

Sahara looked at his expression for a moment and didn't ask further. "He came here."

Scar's expression changed. "Here?"

"Yes."

"How long did he stay?"

"Quite a while."

"What did he do?"

Just as Sahara was about to answer, Ed cut in. "Did he ask about those channels too?"

Scar frowned, but Sahara laughed. "You've got a lot of questions. He was just like that at first."

Ed froze. "What?"

"He looked at the walls first, then wandered around all day following the water and the stone."

Daud shrugged. "I was too little back then. I barely remember."

Ed looked at Scar. Scar said nothing.

Ed turned back to Sahara. "Was he an alchemist?"

"I think he used that word, yes."

"So he wanted to learn this?"

"He did."

Scar caught Ed by the shoulder and pulled him back. "My questions come first."

"Okay, okay!"

"Why did my brother come here?" Scar asked.

"He heard about alkahestry from a merchant out of Xing, and said it resembled the old Ishvalan records. He went looking for the root of it and ended up following the trail all the way to our name. He asked whether there really was a way to use the flow."

Scar's eyes fixed on her. "How?"

Sahara thought for a moment, then shook her head. "Wait a little for that."

"Why?"

"There's something I want to show this one first." She got to her feet. "Come with me."

* * *

They arrived at a wide clearing at the edge of the village.

In the middle was a shallow basin. Channels branched out from it in several directions, and the lines Ed had been seeing everywhere were carved into the ground and the walls. A little way off stood a stone pillar taller than a man.

Nadir had gotten there first and was standing beside the basin.

The moment Ed stepped into the clearing, he scanned the area. "No transmutation circle."

"Does there have to be one?" Sahara asked.

"If it's alchemy."

"We've never said we do alchemy."

Ed turned to her at once. "Then what is it?"

"What is what?"

"This technique. What's it called?"

"Nothing."

Ed blinked. "What?"

"It has no name."

"It's a technique and it doesn't have a name?"

Sahara pointed at a channel. "Do you need a name to drink water?"

Ed's face scrunched up.

Sahara looked at Nadir. Nadir sat down beside the basin and placed his hand on the ground.

Ed immediately leaned in. "Move your hand."

He checked beneath Nadir's palm. There was no transmutation circle. Only one of the old lines in the ground. Nadir hadn't drawn anything. He had simply laid his hand on a line that was already there.

"That's enough," Nadir said, and put his hand back down.

Beside him, Daud shifted a stone slab.

Water flowed. The water that filled the basin drained off into another channel.

A moment later, there was a low hum.

The stone pillar slowly began to tilt.

Ed's eyes went wide.

The pillar didn't fall. It stopped at a fixed angle, as if someone were holding it up.

"..."

Ed ran over. He checked beneath the pillar, then the channels, then the surrounding stones, one after another.

"Again."

Nadir looked at Sahara. Sahara nodded. The pillar returned to its original position.

It moved two more times, and the amount of water and the change in the stone still didn't add up in Ed's calculations.

"What are you looking at?" Scar asked.

Ed didn't answer. He looked at the channels once more, then at Sahara. "What's the price?"

Sahara narrowed her eyes. "What do you mean?"

"You moved this."

"We did."

"So what did you use?"

"We moved a little water too."

"It's not just that." Ed rapped the pillar with his knuckles. "You moved a pillar, but there's no sign you brought in material or rebuilt anything. That water alone doesn't explain it."

"And?" Sahara asked.

"'And'?!" Ed raised his voice. "If you want to gain something, you have to give up something of equal value!"

"Why?" Sahara asked quietly.

Ed stopped. "Because that's how it works."

"It isn't how it works for us."

Ed's face went completely rigid. Scar looked at Sahara too.

Sahara laid her hand on the stone pillar. "Do you think we moved this stone?"

"Then who did?"

Sahara pointed at the ground beneath her feet. "The earth."

"..."

This time she pointed at the channels. "Water moves. So does the wind. Even stone doesn't stay in the same place forever."

Ed frowned.

Sahara didn't explain. Instead, she asked, "If you want to move a river, should you look only at the river water?"

Ed closed his mouth.

"You have to look at the rain, the mountains, and the earth too. So where does the river begin?"

Ed's eyes went to the channels.

"We've never taken anything out and used it separately," Sahara said. "That doesn't mean there's nothing there." She gestured around them. "It's all already here."

Ed slowly looked around. The channels and the wind. The cliffs and the stone.

"..."

Something caught. But he couldn't get hold of it yet.

"Then what's passing along those lines?" Ed asked.

Sahara laughed. "Your companion's brother stayed here a good long while trying to work that out."

Something changed in Scar's eyes. "My brother asked the same question?"

"He did." Sahara looked at Ed. "At first he was almost exactly like you."

Ed took his hand off the pillar. "Did he say the exchange didn't add up?"

Sahara searched her memory for a moment. "I think he said something like that."

Scar looked down at the fields. "And then?"

"He kept looking."

"At what?"

"The stone at first. Then the water and the fields."

Scar's gaze went to the fields. "Why?"

"Because that was when his questions started to change, little by little."

Scar pressed on. "How did they change?"

Sahara didn't answer. "I'll show you tomorrow."

Scar's brow twitched. "Why is it always tomorrow?"

* * *

Instead of answering, Sahara led them up to the top of the village as the sun went down.

From there, they could see all of Karsa. Lines ran between houses and fields, channels and cisterns, walls and pillars.

Ed was silent for a long time. In the daytime, each part had looked separate. But from up high, it was different.

"...The whole village."

"Can you see it?" Sahara asked.

"It's connected."

"To some extent."

"Is all of this the same thing as earlier?"

"Not all of it."

"Then how far does it go?"

Sahara laughed. "Here we go again."

Ed didn't answer. What she had said earlier was still in his head.

*It's all already here.*

He looked out over Karsa. In alchemy, the transmutation circle set the boundary of the calculation. But here...

How far did he have to calculate? To the basin? To the channels? To the cliffs?

"...Where's the boundary?" Ed muttered.

Sahara looked at him. "What boundary?"

"Nothing." Ed shook his head. He couldn't put it into words yet.

"How long has this method existed?" Scar asked Sahara.

"I don't know."

"Who created it?"

"I don't know that either. There are old records, but there's no telling where it began."

Ed looked frustrated.

Sahara pointed west. "People used to come from over there." Then east. "And from over there too."

"Trade?"

"Do you think only goods came along?" Sahara said. "Medicine came. Horses came. People came. And ideas came too."

Ed listened quietly. "Then this method might also—"

"I don't know," Sahara cut him off. "You want to know who first taught it to whom?"

"Of course."

"Why?"

"If you know the beginning, you can work out how it developed—"

"How do you know there was only one beginning?"

Ed stopped.

Sahara said nothing more. Instead, she started walking back down.

* * *

When night fell, they went into the room where the old records were kept.

Sahara lit a lamp. The shelves were piled with worn ledgers, wooden tablets, and records written on leather.

Ed's eyes lit up. "Can I look at all of these?"

"No."

"Why not?!"

"What you need first."

Sahara pulled out a few volumes. They were records of people who had passed through Karsa long ago. Merchants. Healers. Craftsmen. Pilgrims. People from Ishval. People from the east.

Ed turned the pages of a ledger. "If this many people came and went, why isn't there a road now?"

The room went quiet.

Sahara looked at Ed. "Because we erased it."

Ed's hand stopped. "I knew it."

"Why?" Scar asked.

This time it was Nadir who answered. "At first it was a good road, one that brought goods back and forth."

Sahara went on. "But people didn't bring only goods along a road. They set taxes. They fixed prices. They asked whose land it was. They even tried to decide who had to give permission."

"Were they people from the west?" Scar asked.

"Good people and bad people came from both the west and the east." Sahara shook her head. "It isn't a story about one side."

"So you closed the road?" Ed said.

"At first."

"And then?"

"When we closed it, they came to open it again."

"So we got rid of it," Nadir said.

Ed thought of the traces they had seen in the desert. Filled-in wells. Broken marker stones. A stone road torn up.

"Even the traces."

"Yes."

"So no one could find their way here."

Sahara nodded.

Ed looked down at the ledger. A road let in more than goods. It let in claims of ownership, too.

"That doesn't mean everyone agreed to erase the road," Sahara said.

Ed looked up. "Even inside Karsa?"

"People live here. How could everyone think the same way?"

"There were people who wanted to open it again?"

"There still are."

Ed turned a page.

Scar did not wait. "My brother."

Sahara looked at him. "We let him in."

"Why?"

"Because he asked questions."

"You let people in just for asking questions?" Ed said.

"No." Sahara smiled. "Plenty of people ask questions after they've already decided the answer."

Scar looked at her. "My brother was not one of them?"

"At first I didn't know."

"But?"

"He stayed a long time." Sahara looked him straight in the eye. "And his questions changed."

Just as Scar was about to ask again, Ed stopped at one corner of a ledger. "Wait."

Scar closed his eyes. "What is it this time?"

"This." Ed brought the lamp closer.

The writing had faded badly. It was nothing like the period when Scar's brother had come. It was a far older record. There was no name. Only a few short lines.

*A traveler from the west.*

*Stayed for quite some time.*

And then—

Ed read slowly. "'Spent a long time studying the waterways and the structure of the stone.'"

His hand stopped. There was one more line below it.

"'Kept asking what was used for the movement.'"

Ed went quiet.

Scar looked over the record. "He is like you."

"...Yeah."

"Who is it?"

"There's no name."

"When did he live?"

Ed checked the pages before and after. "I don't know. Only that it's really old."

Sahara looked at the record. "So that one survived too."

Ed looked up at once. "You know about it?"

"He lived long before I was born. How would I know?"

"Are there other records?"

"We'll have to look."

Ed looked down at the ledger again. Scar's brother and a traveler from the west long ago had both asked about movement, the same as he had. Ed thought back to the question he had asked in front of the stone pillar that afternoon.

He didn't take his eyes off the ledger.

"Are you calculating again?" Scar asked.

"No." Ed thought for a moment. "This time I've got more questions."

"Is that a good thing?"

The corners of Ed's mouth lifted. "Obviously."

Scar lay down with the look of a man who did not understand.

Ed went back to the record. Outside the window, water flowed and wind passed between the cliffs. It was movement that would have been there hundreds of years ago too.

It occurred to Ed that the people of Karsa didn't even have a name for their own method. If it wasn't alchemy, and it wasn't alkahestry, then what was it?

He touched one line of the old record with his finger.

*A traveler from the west.*

"...Who are you?"

The only answer was the sound of running water.
