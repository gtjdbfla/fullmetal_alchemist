# Chapter 23: A Good Material

Crimstone did not break down easily even in fire.

At the survey station on the outskirts of Ishval, a technician took out a cooled sample. Graus watched.

"The same again?"

"Yes, sir."

The geologist beside him turned a page. "As far as we've confirmed so far, there is nothing alchemically unusual about it."

"Alchemically?"

"It was found in an old prayer hall, so Central asked us to check."

Graus looked at the dark red stone. "And?"

"It's just a mineral."

The technician added, "A good material, though."

Graus looked at him. "Good?"

"It holds up to heat and pressure, and it's stable when worked. Its properties as an alloy need more study."

"Can the military use it?"

The technician paused for a moment. "There's no reason only the military would use it. It has potential for high-temperature equipment and industrial parts too."

"Minerals that appear to be the same type have been found in Amestris before," the geologist said.

"Common?"

"No."

"Value?"

"That depends on the deposits."

Graus looked at the map on the desk. "And in Ishval?"

The geologist came over to the map. "That's the problem."

Red marks were dotted here and there. Near reconstruction sites and wells, collapsed buildings and old prayer halls. The geologist connected several of them with his finger.

"There are many places we haven't drilled yet, so we can't be certain."

"Tell me only what has been confirmed."

"If the red layers found so far are connected to one another," he said, "it may be on a scale that's hard to ignore economically."

Graus looked down at the map without a word. "You said there is some in Xing as well?"

"There are records of a similar mineral being traded."

"Do they use it as a material there too?"

"It seems to be treated mainly as a gemstone. They say it fetches a price because of its color and luster."

Graus looked at the sample. In Amestris, a material. In Xing, a gem. In Ishval, a stone whose fate had not yet been decided.

* * *

"They say it's a good material," Adin said.

Scar was carrying bricks. "I heard."

"Rare, and good for a lot of things."

"That too."

"So that means it's worth something, right?"

It wasn't only Adin gathered in front of the building under reconstruction. Zahir and a few residents were there too. Since the survey results had gotten out, the same kind of conversation happened several times a day.

"If there's a mine, there'll be jobs," Adin said.

Zahir shot back at once. "Whose jobs?"

"Ours, obviously."

"Who builds the mine?"

"We can decide that going forward, can't we?"

"Who decides?"

Adin scowled. "This again?"

"Because it matters."

"If we do nothing, nothing changes."

"If we do just anything, we may not be able to undo what changes."

"Then how long are we going to just stare at rocks?"

Just as Zahir was about to answer, Adin turned to Scar. "What do you think?"

Scar set down a brick. "About what?"

"Development."

"I do not know."

Adin sighed. "Why does everyone keep saying they don't know?"

Scar picked up the next brick. "I know you want work." Then he looked at Zahir. "I know you fear the land will be taken."

"Then which side is right?"

"Both of you are speaking for yourselves."

"So?"

Scar carried the brick. "What comes after that is not mine to decide."

"In the end, the government will decide," Zahir said.

Scar's hand stopped for a moment. "Not yet."

"Until when?"

"I do not know."

Adin scratched his head. "You're really no help."

"No."

Scar walked on. Adin watched his back, then said to Zahir, "How can he be so confident about being no help?"

Zahir snorted. Scar didn't look back.

* * *

That night, Graus wrote his report.

Residents' opinions were divided. Some were in favor of the survey but against mining; some said it was fine as long as the sacred sites were left out. The proportions would change depending on how the question was asked. He wrote that there was no basis for regarding any one side as representing all the residents. There had been friction between the military and residents, but no organized armed action.

Miles looked at whether the people here could live through tomorrow. Graus looked at how far the situation could go. Today, their two lines of sight were pointed at the same place.

On the last line, his pen stopped.

*If it is stable, report that it is stable.*

Graus signed without changing the sentence.

* * *

A few days later, in Central.

Brandt read to the last page and set the report down. "And Scar?"

"He is taking part in the reconstruction work. No hostile action."

"Is he dangerous?"

"Judging by his past actions alone, he is a dangerous man. At present, he is not."

Brandt nodded. "This is what I wanted. Not bringing back the conclusion people want to hear."

Graus stopped at the door.

"Keep doing it this way," Brandt said, and tapped the report. "Not what the Fuhrer wants to hear, and not what I want to hear either."

"Understood."

When he came out into the hallway, Graus looked back once toward the Fuhrer's office.

* * *

The same day. Northwestern Xing. A trading house in Lansi.

"He went down as far as Haojin?" Zhenha Bai asked.

"As far as Mulanhe," the merchant across from him answered.

Zhenha set down the teacup he was holding. "The Emperor himself?"

"Yes."

"With whom?"

"Xiaolin An and Chenluo An." The merchant added, "They say the Fullmetal Alchemist was with them too."

Zhenha's eyebrow moved slightly. "Why him?"

"There's talk that he had something to do with how the southern road story first came up."

"Anything confirmed?"

"No."

"Then leave it out."

The merchant lowered his head.

Zhenha opened the ledger on his desk. Medicinal herbs coming into Lansi. Hides. Grain. Goods coming across from the west. Warehouse fees. Cart freight. Brokerage commissions. Among the numbers, the names of Bai merchant houses appeared again and again.

"If a southern road opens, how much would we lose?"

"There isn't even a route yet."

"Then estimate it."

"That isn't easy."

"If it were easy, would I be asking you?"

The merchant closed his mouth.

Zhenha turned a page. "And the An side?"

"No particular movement."

Zhenha looked up. "Is that what I asked?"

The merchant straightened. "The volume of herb trading in Haojin is about the same as last season. Livestock numbers in the western pastures have gone up a little. There was one well dispute on the Mulanhe side, and it's already been settled."

"Carts coming up to Lansi?"

"Slightly fewer than last month."

"Reason."

"The road was in bad shape."

Only then did Zhenha nod.

He hadn't started asking about such things today. To the Bai, the An were not enemies. But they weren't neighbors you could ignore either. For the Bai, who had built up their strength around Lansi and the northwestern trade road, the An in the south had long been an uncertain presence. Herbs and hides came from An land, but because of roads and water, they were not yet Lansi's competitors.

Zhenha had always kept checking on that "not yet."

"How many people do we have trading with them?"

"Which side do you mean?"

"Haojin, Mulanhe. Down to the pastures below them."

"Six who trade directly with the merchant house. More if you count the middlemen."

"People we've used for a long time?"

"Yes."

"And we've been hearing about the An side through them all along."

"That's right."

Zhenha tapped the ledger with his finger. He didn't need a new network of information. There was already a road. A road goods came up along. A road money went down along. And talk traveled along that road too.

"The reaction to the southern road?"

"Divided."

"In favor?"

"Mostly young merchants and haulers on the Haojin side. Xiaolin An is one of them."

"Against?"

"The people who hold the pastures and the well rights are wary. Chenluo An is the best known."

Zhenha thought for a moment. "Do you know why they're against it?"

"Roughly."

"Not roughly. Find out."

"Yes."

"Without putting new people on it."

The merchant looked at him.

"Use the people we already trade with."

"How far should I look into it?"

"Who's for it. Who's against it. Whose land it is, and who uses the wells. What the imperial court is looking into."

The merchant didn't write any of it down. He didn't need to. He nodded. "Understood."

Zhenha started to close the ledger, then stopped. "And."

"Yes."

"Among those who are against it, if there are people who want to speak up but can't, help them."

The merchant's eyes came up. "When you say help..."

"Fund them, if needed."

"Goods too?"

"If it can be turned into money, it doesn't matter."

"How far should the support go?"

Zhenha thought for a moment. "Far enough that the southern road doesn't roll along as if it's already decided."

"You mean block it?"

"If possible," Zhenha answered. "At least delay it."

The merchant was silent for a moment. "There could be trouble on the An side."

"That's why I'm telling you to use people we already know." Zhenha's voice dropped. "I'm not talking about starting a war."

"Yes."

"I'm not telling anyone to kill anyone either."

"Yes."

"Just don't let it turn into a road being settled because the Emperor took one look around."

"Understood."

The merchant gathered his ledger. Just as he was about to open the door, Zhenha called him again. "And Chenluo."

"Yes."

"Don't approach him directly."

The merchant turned. "Why not?"

"That man opposes it out of his own convictions," Zhenha said. "There's no need to make him look like one of ours."

"Understood."

"And."

"Yes."

"Don't tell Lord Luhan."

The merchant hesitated a moment, then answered. "...Yes."

The door closed.

Zhenha looked out the window. Carts were pulling up in front of the Lansi warehouses. Loads were taken off. Other loads were put on. Back to the west. Back to the south.

The Bai were wary of the An not because they hated them. It was because their roads were different. Until now, most goods going west had passed through Lansi. The An's herbs. Their hides. Their people.

But if a new road opened in the south—for the first time, there would be a choice that didn't have to go through Lansi.

Zhenha didn't like that.

* * *

That evening, a familiar cart passed out through the gates of Lansi.

The load was the same as always. The road and the trading network had been there before. The only thing that had changed was the request riding along with them.
