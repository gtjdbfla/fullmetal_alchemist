# Chapter 33: The One Who Isn't Emperor

**Lansi, Xing.**

After Kairun died, the kind of people coming into Lansi changed.

At first it was merchants. Then came people who said they were there to protect the merchants. The Bai merchant houses sent guards. The guards of Zhenha's merchant house were posted only inside the warehouse district, under orders not to step outside it.

"We've come to protect our people."

A few days later, similar people came in from the An side too.

"We've come to protect our people."

On a wall in the An shopping street, words someone had written in ink were bleeding into the plaster.

*Would Xing still function without an emperor?*

People said they were the last words Kairun spoke before he died. No one erased them.

Ling couldn't send either group home. Lansi had always been a merchant city. It was common for guards with swords or spears to come through the gate with a caravan. Saying no only this time would become a problem in itself.

Instead, he gave orders.

*No collective armed action.*

*No armed gatherings above a set number of people.*

*No alkahestry for combat purposes within the city.*

The weapons both sides had sealed earlier stayed in the warehouses.

"People who want to fight are still going to fight," Ed said.

Ling stamped a document. "I know."

"Then?"

"I can't lock them up before they fight."

Ed closed his mouth. He was right. Ed just didn't like it.

* * *

On the fourth day after the merchant's death, the dead Bai merchant's funeral was held. The killer still hadn't been caught.

That afternoon, Ed was near the market with Furong.

"These days, rumors are faster than information," Furong said. "Information has to be confirmed."

"And rumors?"

"Rumors don't."

Ed frowned.

Just then, smoke rose in the distance. They both turned their heads at the same time. It was the Bai shopping street.

A man came running, shouting, "The An set a fire!"

"Did you see it?" Ed asked at once.

The man faltered. "What?"

"Did you see the An set the fire?!"

"Everyone's saying—"

"Then you didn't see it!"

But it was already too late. A few young Bai men ran toward the An shops on the other side. Imperial guards blocked the road. Curses flew. Someone shoved. A fist flew.

And a sword was drawn.

Even Ed didn't see who was first. Metal clashed. Once. Twice. People screamed and scattered.

An An man fell.

For a moment it went quiet. It was after they'd seen blood.

Someone shouted, "The Bai killed an An!"

This time too many people heard it.

Ed's face went hard. "Furong."

"Yes."

"Go to Ling."

"And you, Mr. Elric?"

Ed was already running. "I've got to stop that first!"

* * *

In one corner of the An shopping street, Xiaolin was sitting on the ground, clutching a sack of medicinal herbs.

"...No." Her lips were trembling. "This isn't why I said it was good."

Ed looked at her. "Xiaolin!"

Xiaolin looked up. Her eyes were wet. "All I talked about was the price of medicine. That if there was a road, Haojin's herbs could go south too. That was all."

Her voice broke.

Instead of answering, Ed took her by the arm and pulled her up.

Xiaolin caught Ed's sleeve. "Help me. I can't do it alone."

"...Okay. Go inside and stay there. Get people together and get the kids out first."

Xiaolin wiped her eyes. She couldn't let go of the sack.

* * *

The An fighters appeared first. They were people who had come in as guards for the merchant houses. Just as they were about to draw their swords, Chenluo blocked their way.

"Put the swords down."

"The Bai went first—"

"Whoever went first." Chenluo's voice was strangely quiet. "If we draw now, we become the first."

People with weapons came out from the Bai side too. It wasn't a real army. There were no commanders. That made it all the messier.

A spear came flying. Lan Fan twisted aside. When she yanked the shaft, the man holding it pitched forward. She bent his wrist, took the spear, and flipped the blade around.

*Thud.* The shaft struck another man in the stomach.

"Drop your weapons!"

A sword came at her instead of an answer. Lan Fan blocked it with the spear shaft.

Ling plunged in among the people too. He didn't draw his sword. He struck wrists with his scabbard and broke another man's balance with his elbow.

"Both sides, stop!"

"Your Majesty, behind you!"

Ling ducked. A blade passed over his head.

"Who swung a sword at the Emperor?!"

"Does that matter right now?!" Lan Fan shouted.

"It matters a little!"

* * *

The moment Ed stepped into the alley—

*Tak. Tak.*

Small metal markers stuck into the stone pavement. Ed's eyes moved. "Alkahestrist!"

An An fighter had stepped back. Transmutation light spread between the markers where they'd landed.

"Ling! Right!"

*Crack!* The stone pavement rose where Ling had just been. A low wall formed. The An fighters ducked behind it.

"Alkahestry is banned inside the city!" Ling shouted.

Ed answered as he ran. "You think they care about that right now?!"

This time there was a clink of metal from the other side. *Tak. Tak.*

Ed turned his head. "There's another one!"

It was the Bai side. Another alkahestrist had marked a position some distance away. Transmutation light. The ground under the stone wall the An had raised split open. The wall tilted. People scattered.

"Lan Fan!"

"Yes!"

"Get the alkahestrists away from here first!"

Lan Fan ran.

Ed stopped for an instant. He looked at the ground. Scattered metal markers. Transmutations happening at a distance. Read the Dragon's Pulse, and cause a change somewhere else. Alkahestry.

But—

He thought of the Lansi government office. A single circle in the dust. Floor to pillar. Pillar to wall. Wall to ceiling.

"...It's different."

Ling knocked an opponent's sword aside with his scabbard and shouted, "What is?!"

"Kairun!"

"*Now?!*"

"It's different from what they're doing!"

A chunk of stone came flying. Ed ducked.

"Explain later!"

"That was the plan!"

The Bai alkahestrist threw another marker. Ed looked at the place it landed before he looked at the people. One. Two.

"Lan Fan! Left, second one!"

Lan Fan changed direction. The tip of her foot kicked away a metal marker. The transmutation light broke off.

"Got it!" Ed grinned.

In that instant, the An alkahestrist moved his hand again. Ed ran. The man took out a marker. Before he could throw it, Ed caught his wrist. An elbow came flying. Ed blocked it.

"Ugh!"

He swept the man's leg as he went. The two of them rolled across the ground.

In the old days, he would have clapped his hands. Put them to the ground and finished it. Not now. Ed twisted the man's arm and pinned him down.

"You think I can't fight just because I can't use alchemy?!"

Ling shouted from a distance, "That's not something an alchemist should say!"

"*Former* alchemist!"

"That's even weirder!"

"Shut up!"

Ed kicked the man's marker far away.

* * *

The fighting didn't end in one alley. Rumors ran ahead.

An An man was dead. A Bai shop had burned. The An had set the fire. The Bai had drawn their swords first. At some point, no one was asking anymore what was a confirmed fact.

Shouting came from the east too. Ed climbed up onto some steps and swept his eyes over the street.

"Ling! The east alley!"

"I see it!"

"Not that one, behind it!"

Ling turned his head again. Three Bai fighters were moving in the direction An residents were fleeing.

"Lan Fan!"

"Yes!"

Lan Fan leaped onto the roof.

Transmutation light spread on the other side. Ed shouted at once, "Clear the right! The ground's coming up!"

The imperial soldiers fell back. Right after, the stone pavement rose.

Ling glanced at Ed. "Keep watching!"

"I am!"

He couldn't use alchemy. He couldn't read energy the way Ling did. But he understood transmutation. Where a person was looking. Where they put the marker. What they were trying to change. That much he could see.

"Above!" Ed shouted.

A cracked wall was shaking. A child was sitting on the ground beneath it.

Ed ran.

"Ed!"

The wall came down. Ed wrapped his arms around the child and rolled. A chunk of stone fell right behind them. Dust spread. The child burst into tears.

"If you can cry, you're fine. Run!"

The child didn't move.

"I said run!"

Only then did the child run. At the end of the street, the child threw himself into the arms of a familiar face. It was the young man who repaired cart wheels at the way station north of Lansi. Ed remembered that face. *It's both.* That was what he'd said.

"This little guy's the grandson of the old man from the relay village," the man said, lifting the child. "Last time I said it was both. Right now there's only one side—the side running away."

Ed looked at the collapsed wall. In the old days, he would have fixed it. He couldn't now. So he looked at the people again. There were still things he could do.

* * *

He got a moment to catch his breath. Ed leaned against a wall.

The markers used by the An and Bai alkahestrists were scattered across the road. Their shapes were a little different. The ways they were used weren't the same either. But both of them read the earth.

He thought of Karsa. They made roads. Alkahestry read the roads already in the earth. Kairun didn't look only at the earth.

Ed snorted.

Before he knew it, Furong was at his side. "Why are you laughing?"

"No reason."

"There's no such thing as 'no reason' for a man laughing in the middle of a battlefield."

Ed looked at the markers on the ground. "What I knew wasn't everything."

"About what?"

"Alchemy."

Furong looked at Ed. "You only realized that now?"

"That's not what I meant!"

Ed flipped one of the markers over with the tip of his foot. Being similar didn't mean being the same. Karsa. Xingese alkahestry. Kairun. They might have touched each other. They might have exchanged something. But there was no need to call one of them the beginning of the rest.

"Interesting."

Furong frowned. "What is?"

Ed stood up. "The world."

"You got hit on the head earlier, didn't you?"

"I didn't!"

"I saw you get hit."

"Shut up!"

The bell rang again. Ed's expression changed instantly. The two of them ran.

* * *

By sunset, fights were breaking out in three districts at once. When the imperial army blocked one side, another alley opened up. When one alkahestrist raised a stone wall, an alkahestrist on the other side changed the road.

Ling kept running back and forth between the two sides. Lan Fan took swords and spears away and brought people down.

Luhan was different. He didn't leave the district where the Bai had gathered. He struck an An fighter's wrist with his scabbard and twisted his body to slip past a spear.

"Get the wounded to the back!"

Young Bai men carried away those who had fallen.

Just then, an imperial soldier came running. "Your Majesty! An residents are surrounded in the south!"

Ling spoke at once. "Send half the troops here to the south!"

Luhan turned around. "Your Majesty."

"What?"

"There are wounded Bai here too."

"I know."

"If you pull out the troops—"

"Civilians are trapped in the south."

"Bai have died too."

Ling's face hardened. "An have died too."

Luhan closed his mouth.

"Because we have to protect both," Ling said. Then he pressed down on each word. "The An. And the Bai. They're both my people."

Luhan couldn't answer. Ling wasn't wrong. That was why there was nothing more to say.

* * *

Only after night fell did the numbers come in.

Six dead. Three An. Three Bai. More than forty wounded.

Ling ordered that the wounded on both sides be treated without distinction. The alkahestrists who'd been caught were isolated regardless of clan. Everyone who had drawn a sword, on both sides, was arrested. There were more hardliners on the An side.

The sealed weapons warehouses stayed closed.

Who had set fire to the Bai shop was still unknown. Who had killed the Bai merchant was still unknown too. Whether the two incidents were connected was unknown.

They knew one thing. Now people had actually died.

In the front courtyard of the government office, Chenluo silently watched An people being bound and led away. Some of them were faces that had followed Kairun. He never once looked at Ling.

* * *

Late at night, Luhan was walking down a corridor with Zhenha.

"His Majesty didn't do anything wrong," Luhan said.

Zhenha didn't answer.

"The An are His Majesty's people too."

"That's right."

"He can't protect only the Bai."

"That's right."

Luhan stopped walking. "Then who protects the Bai?"

"We hold on. As we are now."

"While we were holding on, people died."

Zhenha didn't answer.

A bell rang in the distance. Another clash.

Luhan looked down at the sword in his hand. Words he'd heard since he was a child came back to him.

*Protect the Bai.*

*Have power.*

*The greatest power lies with the emperor.*

He used to hate them. After Ling Yao became emperor, he had thought those words weren't needed anymore. Ling was a good emperor. He still thought so even now.

But today, to protect the An, Ling had pulled troops out of the place where the Bai were. It wasn't wrong. An emperor had to do that.

Luhan closed his eyes.

*If I were the emperor.*

This time, he didn't deny it right away. A little later, another thought followed.

*Do I have to be the emperor?*

Luhan didn't answer. He raised his sword. And walked back toward where the bell was ringing.
