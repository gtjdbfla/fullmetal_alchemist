# Chapter 8: Records from the East

"Is it here?"

"It is not."

Ed stood in front of Miles's desk. Miles didn't even look up from his papers.

"Seriously?"

"I told you the same thing yesterday."

"Yesterday was yesterday."

"It still has not come today."

Ed dragged a chair over and sat. "How long does one analysis take?"

Miles finally looked up. "You are the one who requested a detailed analysis."

"So do it fast and in detail."

"Those two do not usually go together."

"Central's got plenty of equipment."

"Wait your turn."

"I've been waiting for days!"

Miles went back to his papers. "Learning to wait might not be a bad thing for you."

"There was an interim report, though."

"What is it?"

"The analysis is still in progress. The preliminary finding is that it does not appear to have any power related to alchemy."

Ed's hand stopped. "...None?"

"It is not final."

Ed was silent for a moment. "Forget it."

He stood. Then Miles said, "Something else did arrive."

Ed turned around at once. "What?"

Miles pulled an envelope from under the papers. The moment Ed saw the front, his expression changed. Familiar handwriting.

"Al?"

"From Xing."

Ed snatched the envelope. "Why'd it come to you?"

"Since he did not know where you were, he apparently sent it to the Ishval Reconstruction Headquarters."

"And how does he know I'm here?"

Miles looked at Ed. "I imagine you wrote to him."

"Oh."

Ed tore open the envelope. Miles said, "You are smart, but you stall in odd places."

"Shut up."

* * *

The letter was long again. The first page was what he'd expected.

> Dear Brother,
>
> I got your letter.
>
> So you went to Ishval after all.
>
> Is Scar doing well?

Ed asked Scar, who was beside him, "Al wants to know if you're doing well."

"Tell him I am."

The next line.

> May says to say hello to Scar.

"May too."

"Understood."

The next.

> And Ling.

Ed started to fold the letter. Scar said, "What?"

"It's not an important part."

He unfolded it again.

> Ling is as busy as ever.
>
> I don't know why an emperor keeps showing up where I am to eat.
>
> This time he ate two of my buns.

Ed clicked his tongue. "Doesn't that emperor have a country to run?"

Miles asked, "When does the important part come?"

"Wait."

"That is not something you should be saying."

Ed ignored him and turned to the next page. The handwriting got a little more cramped.

> I told May the story you sent me.
>
> The part where Scar's brother wrote that Ishvala is connected to alkahestry.

Ed's eyes stopped. He sat back down in the chair.

> I asked if she knew anything about Ishvalan religion, but she doesn't know anything.
>
> She's never even heard of Ishval, except that the country exists.
>
> I also asked about Ishvala = the Truth? I gave her a quick explanation of what Amestris calls the Truth and the Gate.
>
> May just shot back.
>
> "No one in alkahestry has ever opened a gate. Not that I know of. And one shouldn't."

Ed's hand froze. He remembered the question written in his notebook. *What about Xing?* It was the first answer to come back. Something about *one shouldn't* strangely snagged.

> So I looked from the other direction. Not from Ishval, but from the alkahestry side. At how alkahestry came to be.
>
> First, the story of the Sage of the West, which you know.

Ed paused.

The Sage of the West. A tale handed down in Xing since long ago. Hundreds of years back, the Sage had brought new knowledge to Xing, and it had combined with the medicine that already existed there and influenced alkahestry's development. It was the same story he'd thought of when he first read Al's letter. But back then, Ed's interest had been elsewhere. Scar's brother. Ancient Ishval. How the two had come to be tied to alkahestry.

He kept reading.

> But the records are vaguer than I thought.

"Vaguer?"

> Some records say the Sage of the West created alkahestry, and others say he only brought new knowledge.
>
> But if you look at even older medical records, it seems similar ideas existed before he arrived.

Ed's eyes narrowed.

> Of course, it wasn't the same as alkahestry today.
>
> May said we need to keep that distinction clear.
>
> But there are expressions that treat the land and the human body not as fixed things, but as something constantly in motion.

The next page.

> And I found one odd sentence.

Beneath it was a short line.

> **The earth flows.**

Ed's hand stopped. Scar noticed the change. "What is it?"

Ed didn't answer. He read on.

> I don't know if it means exactly the same thing as what alkahestry calls the Dragon's Pulse today.
>
> Maybe it doesn't.
>
> The record is far older, and the context it was written in is different.

Ed said quietly, "Good job."

Miles asked, "At what?"

"He's not jumping to conclusions."

"Who?"

"Al."

Ed looked at the letter again. He remembered the sentence he'd read and passed over in the records hall.

> The earth does not stand still.
>
> Life rises from the earth and returns to the earth.

Ed touched his lip. They were similar. Which was why he had to be more careful. A few days ago, he himself had tied Ishvala and the Truth together on the strength of a resemblance. He hadn't confirmed that the hypothesis was wrong. But it didn't look as simple as it had at first.

He read the next line.

> May says we shouldn't assume expressions like these all began in one place.
>
> Even before the Sage of the West arrived, Xing already had knowledge of medicine and nature.
>
> I think it's true the Sage brought something new, but we'll need to dig further to know exactly what he brought and what was already there.

Ed set the letter down for a moment. "...So one person didn't make it all."

Scar asked, "Make what?"

"Alkahestry."

Ed handed him the letter. "At least, that's what the records Al found seem to say."

Scar read slowly. Ed looked out the window. The Sage of the West. Xing's old medicine. The flow of the earth. The records of ancient Ishval. Similar phrases existed in several places. The question was why they were so similar.

Ed recalled a line from the letter again.

> The earth flows.

The prayer: "The flow may be borrowed." Xing's Dragon's Pulse. Different names, different eras, but seeing the same thing. The flow. And the place where those who'd borrowed the flow were said to have gone.

"...Karsa."

Ed muttered. Scar looked up. "We need to find where Karsa is."

* * *

That afternoon, Ed asked the old Ishvalans at the reconstruction site about Karsa. None of them knew it. Scar shook his head too.

"It is a name I have never heard before."

"If it's the far east, it might be on an old map."

"Let's ask Miles if he has any old maps."

* * *

"We do not," Miles answered quickly.

Ed pounded the desk. "Look first, then say so."

"It is not on the maps in use today."

"Not those."

"How old?"

"I don't know."

Miles looked at Ed. "Do you know that is the hardest request there is?"

Ed held out a paper with the name written on it. "There's a name like this. Karsa."

Miles studied the name for a long time. "Where did you get it?"

"The prayers and Scar's brother's notebook."

"What kind of place is it?"

"That's why I came, because I don't know."

Miles thought for a moment. "During the reconstruction, we collected old land records and ancient documents."

"Ancient documents too?"

"Some."

"Let me see."

"Wait."

"*Again?*"

"They have to be brought from storage."

Ed sighed. "All I do lately is wait."

Miles stood. "The world does not move at your speed."

"That's why it's so frustrating."

* * *

The documents arrived the next day. More precisely, they were closer to tattered scrolls and a bundle of worn land records than documents. They didn't match the present borders. More than half the town names had vanished.

Ed spread them out on the floor. Scar. Miles. And the old man who managed the records.

"This is present-day Ishval," Miles said, pointing to a spot. "And this is east."

Ed noticed a thin line on the old document. "What's this?"

Miles leaned in. "It looks like a road."

The old man shook his head. "Even when I was a child, there was no such road."

"Then it's older."

Ed followed the line. East of Ishval. Desert. Small marks here and there. "Are these wells?"

"They look like it," Miles said.

The line continued east. But it broke off near the end of the document. Ed brought his face close. There was small writing near it. Almost erased.

"What does this say?"

Miles tried to read it. "Ka..."

The old man squinted. Scar came closer too. Ed carefully brushed the dust off with his finger. Faint letters.

**Karsa.**

Ed said, "...Karsa."

This time his voice was certain. Scar opened his brother's notebook and put a finger on *Karsa. East. The end.*

Ed looked back and forth between the notebook and the old document. "It's the same place."

Scar said nothing for a long time. "...So it really existed."

For the first time, the name had been set down on the ground.

* * *

That evening, Ed sat in front of Scar's house, writing a reply to Al.

> I got your letter.

He thought for a moment.

> I found something similar to the records you found, here too.

He tried to write the next line, and stopped. At first he'd meant to write:

> They seem to be connected.

Ed looked at the sentence. Then he erased it. Instead, he wrote:

> It's worth checking out.

After a bit of thought, he added one more.

> Nothing's confirmed yet.

Scar, sitting beside him, said, "You have become very cautious."

Ed looked up. "Shut up."

"It was a compliment."

"It's creepy when you say it."

Ed folded the letter. Then he said, "I'm going to Karsa."

Scar didn't answer right away. "Your brother wrote down that name, and there's an old road. I've got to check."

"I know."

Scar looked east, where it was getting dark. He said nothing for a long time. Then he said, "I will go too."

Ed turned. "Why would you?"

Scar paused as if choosing his answer. "It is the road my brother walked."

"..."

"I intend to follow it."

It was a short answer. Ed didn't ask any more. There seemed to be more to it, but now wasn't the time to press.

Ed stood. When he'd come to Ishval, he'd wanted to know only one thing: how had Scar's brother learned alkahestry? He still didn't have that answer. Instead, where the brother had gone was slowly coming into view. From Ishval. To the east. Along a road that no longer existed. And at the edge of the map, a single remaining name.

Karsa.

Ed looked east into the dark. Xing was still far. Before that, there was one more place to go.
