# Chapter 20: How to Become an Emperor

The next morning, Ed stopped the moment he opened the dining room door.

"...What the—"

Empty plates were stacked on the table. Behind them, Ling raised a hand. "Morning."

"How long have you been eating?"

"Just a little while."

Ed counted the plates. "*This* is a little while?"

Across from him, Luhan sat drinking tea. He had one plate.

Ed looked from one to the other. "You said you were both raised to become emperor."

"So?"

"Guess the curriculum was pretty different."

Ling stopped mid-tear on a piece of bread. "What's that supposed to mean?"

"I believe he's talking about portions," Luhan said quietly.

"Not you too!"

"I didn't say anything."

"You just did!"

Ed sat down next to Luhan. "Did you two talk last night?"

"A little," Ling answered.

"A little?"

Luhan set down his teacup. "I waited while His Majesty ate."

"Hey!"

Ed snorted. "Then I guess you'll have to talk today."

Ling put the last piece of bread in his mouth. "That's why I called you here."

* * *

The meeting room at the Lansi government office. The same map as the day before was spread on the table. The current northern trade road. The vanished old road through Karsa. And a southern line that didn't exist yet.

"Do the Bai oppose the southern trade road?" Ling asked.

"That's a hard question to answer."

That was Luhan's first answer.

Ed raised an eyebrow. "Why?"

"Because the Bai aren't one person either."

Ling laughed. "Did you hear what I said yesterday?"

"No."

"But you're saying the exact same thing."

"Because it's obvious."

Luhan looked at the map. "If a road opens in the south, it'll be an opportunity for the An."

Ed looked a little surprised. "Didn't you come here to oppose it?"

"I came to tell you the Bai could lose out."

"Isn't that the same thing?"

"It's different." Luhan pointed to the northern road. "There are people who make their living from this road." His finger touched Lansi and several points north of it in turn. "People who guard the caravans. People who haul freight. People who rent out horses. People who run inns."

"The Bai?"

"Some are Bai, and some aren't."

"Do you think they'll all disappear if a southern road opens?" Ling asked.

"No." Luhan shook his head. "Lansi is a big city. The northern road won't disappear either."

"Then?"

"It'll shrink, little by little."

It was the same thing Furong had said. Ed looked at the northern road on the map.

"Opening a road takes a day," Luhan said. "But people leaving because of that road can happen over years."

"So don't build it?"

"No." The answer came quickly this time too. "I'm saying we need to know first."

"Know what?"

"Who gains." Luhan's finger moved south. "And who loses."

Ling looked at him for a moment. "And after that?"

"Then Your Majesty has to decide."

Ed looked at Ling. Ling's expression didn't change.

"You really think so?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

This time, Luhan looked at Ling as if puzzled. "Because you're the Emperor."

* * *

In the afternoon, Xiaolin and Chenluo came back to the government office. It was a meeting Luhan had asked for.

Chenluo's face hardened the moment he saw him. "Why is a Bai here?"

"I came to hear the same story," Luhan answered.

"The southern road doesn't pass through Bai land."

"I know."

"Then why?"

"Because the northern road does."

Chenluo's eyes narrowed. "So you came to block it."

"If I'd come to block it, I'd have spoken to His Majesty directly. I'd have no reason to persuade you."

Ling raised a hand. "I didn't call you here to fight."

"We're not fighting yet," Xiaolin said.

"*That's* not fighting?" Ed muttered.

"It's better than yesterday," Ling answered quietly.

The meeting began. Chenluo had brought more concrete demands than the day before.

"Before you consider a road, some things have to be settled first."

"What things?" Ling asked.

"Land," Chenluo said. "Whether outsiders can buy up the land around the road." The next finger. "Wells. How much of the existing wells new merchants and caravans can use." The next. "Pasture. Whether the land we use now can be taken under the excuse of building relay stations or warehouses."

"If you block all of that, who's going to come?" Xiaolin said.

"I didn't say block all of it."

"Then how much?"

"That's what I'm saying we decide first!"

Before their voices could rise, Luhan said, "Chenluo is right."

The room went quiet. Even Chenluo forgot what he was going to say for a moment.

Ed looked at Luhan. "You're Bai, right?"

"I am."

"So?"

"If you're building a road through An land, is it strange to settle the An's rights first?"

"No."

"Then there's no problem." Luhan looked at Chenluo. "I think rights to land and water have to be settled before the road."

"Why are you taking our side?" Chenluo asked.

"I'm not taking your side," Luhan said. "If today the Emperor can guarantee the An's land and water—" His finger moved to the northern road. "—then tomorrow he can guarantee the Bai's as well."

"The Emperor guarantees it," Ling said quietly.

"Yes."

"You think that's the best way?"

Luhan looked at Ling. "If Your Majesty listens fully and then decides."

"And the next emperor?"

A short silence.

"The next emperor would have to do the same," Luhan said.

"And if he doesn't?"

Luhan thought for a moment. "Then we must not make someone like that emperor."

"And who decides who 'someone like that' is?" Ed asked.

Luhan looked at Ed.

"The people who pick the next emperor are going to be the people at the top anyway, right? What if they're wrong?"

"That's exactly why His Majesty is listening like this now."

"That's about *this* emperor."

Luhan didn't answer.

Ed looked at Ling's face. Ling didn't ask anything further.

* * *

During a break in the meeting, Ed went out into the hallway. Ling was nowhere to be seen.

"Where'd he go?"

Lan Fan pointed down the hall. "The archive."

"All of a sudden?"

"His Majesty said there was something he needed to check."

Ed opened the door. Ling was sitting in front of a pile of dust-covered documents.

"What are you doing?"

"Looking for something."

"I can see that. I'm asking what you're looking for!"

Ling turned a page. Land registrations. Well rights. Imperial approvals. Rulings from local offices.

When Ed reached for a paper lying beside him, Ling grabbed it first. "Don't look at other people's stuff."

"Since when are imperial records yours?"

"I'm the one looking at them right now, so they're mine."

"What kind of logic is that?!"

Ling glared at Ed for a moment, then said, "I'm looking at how emperors handled land and well disputes in the past."

"What does that have to do with the trade road?"

"Let's just go back to the meeting."

Ling took one document and went out first. Ed followed, grumbling.

On the table, one of the records Ling had been reading was left lying open. It was an imperial ruling on the right to use an old well. It had been changed by one emperor's decree. And decades later, changed back by another emperor's decree.

* * *

The meeting started again. This time, Ling asked the questions.

"Let's suppose a southern road is built."

Xiaolin leaned forward. "Starting when—"

"I said suppose."

"...Yes."

"We'd restrict outsiders from buying land."

"By how much?" Chenluo asked.

"I don't know yet."

"And the wells?"

"Same."

"Relay stations?"

"Not yet either." Ling raised his hand. "So I'm not deciding anything right now."

Xiaolin looked frustrated. "Then when will you decide?"

"After we ask people."

"Ask who?"

"We have to settle that first," Ling said. "Whether we only ask the people who own land. Whether we ask the people who work that land too. What we do when the young people and the old people disagree. Whether it's all right to leave out villages the road doesn't pass through."

Xiaolin's face stiffened a little. "That will take a long time."

"I know."

"It could take months."

"It could."

"And in the meantime, people keep leaving."

Ling was quiet for a moment. "I know that too."

This time Chenluo spoke. "That's why, in the end, Your Majesty has to decide."

Luhan nodded as well. "I think so too."

Ling looked from one to the other. "That's the first time you two have agreed."

Chenluo's expression turned complicated. Luhan didn't smile.

"We can't wait until everyone's opinion is the same," Luhan said. "Listening is necessary. But in the end, someone has to decide."

"And that's the emperor?"

"Yes."

Ling leaned back in his chair. "A good emperor listens, judges, and decides."

"That's what I was taught the role of an emperor is."

Ling's expression changed slightly. "Me too."

For the first time, Luhan hesitated.

Ling smiled. "I was taught the same thing."

The air between them softened a little.

Ed watched them. They really were alike. Their age. Being raised to become emperor. Being taught they had to protect their clans. And yet, strangely, even when they said the same words, they were looking at slightly different places.

* * *

Before sunset, the four of them walked to the northern gate. A caravan leaving Lansi was tying down its loads.

"The people who'll make a living on a new road, and the people who make a living on the current one," Luhan said. "We have to look at both."

"And if the two want different things?" Ling asked.

"Then you listen fully, and Your Majesty decides."

Ling was quiet for a moment. "Maybe some problems are ones I have to decide. And maybe some are ones I shouldn't."

Luhan looked as if he didn't understand. "Are there things an emperor shouldn't decide?"

"That's what I'm trying to find out."

* * *

That night, on his way back to the government office, Ed noticed Ling had vanished again.

"Again?"

"The archive," Lan Fan said.

"His hobbies got weird after he became emperor."

"They were weird before that."

"That's true."

They both stopped at the same time.

"..."

"..."

Ed spoke first. "Did you just insult the Emperor?"

"No."

"You did."

"You misheard."

Lan Fan kept walking. Ed snorted.

The light was still on in the archive window. Ling was reading old records again. Who could own land. Who could use the wells. Which clan managed which road. And whether those decisions had stayed the same under the next emperor.

Ling put his finger on the end of one document. The emperor had changed. The decree had changed.

He looked at that sentence for a long time. Then he wrote briefly on a sheet beside him.

*The Emperor guarantees it.*

A moment later, he added one more line below it.

*And the next emperor?*
