# Chapter 26: Footsteps

The day after Kairun died, a fourth version of the story was already circulating in Lansi.

The first was that the An clan had tried to kill the emperor. The second, that the emperor had imprisoned the An people. The third, that the Bai clan had been directing the An from behind the scenes.

And the fourth—

"They say Ling Yao's trying to destroy Xing."

Ed stopped. Two merchants beside the road were speaking quite seriously.

"The emperor?"

"They say he wants to abolish all the clans."

"Why?"

"To take everything for himself, I suppose."

"But the emperor's already the emperor."

"Exactly. He wants even more."

Ed watched them briefly.

"...They'll make up anything."

He walked on. He wasn't particularly interested. Ling was alive. Kairun was dead. Who had supported whom, and which clan sided with which, were matters for Ling to handle.

Ed had something unfinished. There had been one transmutation circle. Yet the transmutation hadn't ended.

That was the problem.

* * *

As Ed entered the administrative offices, the plate in front of Ling sat cold, barely touched.

"Not eating?"

"No appetite."

"The emperor?"

"Emperors have appetites too."

Ed looked at the plate in silence for a moment.

"Eat. Who's going to investigate if you collapse?"

Ling picked up a piece of bread. He couldn't get a single bite down and set it back.

"We found it."

He held out a sheet of paper. Ed took it.

"Found what?"

"Kairun's hometown."

Ed's eyes went straight to the page. An territory. Farther west than Haojin. A region now scattered with small villages.

Ed went to the map on the wall.

"Here?"

"Yes."

His finger moved west. Haojin. An land farther west. Desert. And then—Karsa.

Ed unfolded an old map as well. It showed the former An territory, stretching much farther west than it did now.

"...It's close."

"To what?"

"Karsa."

Ling came over. "Are you saying Kairun went there?"

"I don't know."

"Then?"

"I'm going to find out."

Ling folded his arms. "I'm trying to find out who told him to kill me."

"You do that."

"And you?"

Ed remembered yesterday's conference room. Floor. Pillar. Wall. Ceiling. A single transmutation circle.

"I'm going to find out what he did."

"Rather than why he tried to kill me?"

"You're looking into why."

Ling stared at Ed in disbelief. Ed folded the paper and put it in his pocket.

"I'll be back."

"Now?"

"Yeah."

"You really don't listen to anyone, do you?"

"Do I have to obey everything just because an emperor says it?"

Ling's expression tightened strangely. Ed stopped too. A brief silence.

"...I take that back."

"Why?"

"You heard something like that yesterday."

Ling gave a small laugh.

"Go on, then."

* * *

Even on a borrowed horse, reaching Haojin took two full days. Finding Xiaolin there wasn't difficult.

"Xiaolin!"

Sorting bundles of medicinal herbs, Xiaolin looked up.

"...Ed?"

Xiaolin's eyes widened. The welcoming expression stiffened a beat later.

"I heard something happened in Lansi. Were you there?"

"I was."

"Is it true? Someone from the An clan attacked His Majesty—"

"A man named Kairun. He's dead."

Xiaolin drew in a sharp breath.

"Then what about the An? Is everyone under suspicion? What about Chenluo?"

Ed looked at Xiaolin for a moment.

"Chenluo's in Lansi. Just until the investigation's over."

"Investigation..."

"Ling said Kairun did it, not the An clan."

Ed shrugged.

"He told the guards himself to leave the other An people alone. Said he'd reconsider if the investigation showed otherwise."

"...Really?"

"Really. He's surprisingly good at that sort of thing."

Xiaolin let out a long breath.

"That's a relief."

"Glad I found you."

"Why are you here?"

Ed held out the paper.

"Know this place?"

Xiaolin looked at it. "Yes."

"Let's go."

"Where?"

"Here."

"I'm busy."

"You're busy tomorrow too anyway."

"That's your problem."

"Do you know the way?"

"Yes."

"Then we're set."

Ed turned first.

"No, we're not!" Xiaolin shouted behind him.

* * *

It took another day to reach Kairun's hometown. The area where he had grown up was quieter than Haojin. Low houses. Dry pastures. An old well. Narrow channels.

But it was too quiet for a place where people lived.

Several houses stood empty, their roofs half collapsed. One of the two wells had run dry, and people with water buckets were already lined up at the other, early in the morning.

The children had no shoes.

"They grow herbs," Xiaolin said quietly. "But hauling them to Haojin costs more than they sell for, so they just let them dry out."

"Most of the young people have left. Only the old and the children are left."

At first, nothing seemed remarkable. Until Ed stopped in front of one of the channels.

"Why's that like that?"

Xiaolin looked. "What?"

"The channel."

It curved sharply in the middle. To the eye, running it straight would have been much easier.

Ed asked a man working nearby, "Why'd you build it this way?"

"Because the water goes this way."

"But the ground slopes over there."

"Normally, yes."

The man pointed in the other direction. "When it rains hard, it drains that way."

"Why?"

"That's the water's path."

Ed stopped. "...The water's path?"

"The path the water takes."

As though there were nothing strange about it, the man returned to work.

A low stone wall stood at an angle that followed the path of the wind.

"The wind's path," Xiaolin said offhandedly.

Water's path. Wind's path. The direction force escaped. It wasn't alkahestry. It wasn't a special technique. Here, this was simply how people saw it.

Ed looked down at the channel. He remembered Karsa. Walls. Channels. Ground. Pillars. The many lines made upon them.

**Paths.**

"Did it come over from Karsa?"

"What?"

Ed immediately shook his head. "...No."

"No what?"

"I was looking for a starting point again."

Xiaolin looked at him without understanding. Ed walked on.

Being similar didn't mean having the same origin. Karsa and the old An territory had been close. People would have traveled between them. Merchants. Herders. People searching for wells. And ideas, too.

There was no reason to say one side had come first.

Then Xiaolin said, "Oh, if you're looking at old things, there's somewhere we can go."

"Where?"

"Some old ruins nearby."

Ed turned at once. "Let's go."

"What about food?"

"Later."

"I knew I shouldn't have asked."

* * *

What remained was less a building than a few walls. The roof had collapsed long ago, and half the floor was buried in earth.

"How old is it?"

"I don't know. Very old."

"That's supposed to tell me something?"

"I'm a guide, not a historian."

Ed approached the wall. Faint pictures remained. A well. People. Channels. Figures digging into the earth. Some had nearly vanished.

He stopped at one section of wall.

"..."

"What?"

A man stood slightly apart from the others. His clothes were different. Long hair, still picked out in a light color. Traces of the same pigment remained around his eyes.

No beard. Young.

And—

Xiaolin spoke first. "He looks like you."

"Shut up."

The answer came quickly, but Ed didn't move. He leaned closer to the wall. The jawline was a little different. The frame was bigger too. Yet the face was strangely familiar.

The clues he had considered before passed through his mind again. A man from the west. Hundreds of years ago. Golden hair and eyes. Xerxes. Someone who had stayed in Karsa, then gone east.

"...No way."

Ed crouched. Below the mural, a stone tablet lay half buried in earth. He brushed the dirt away with his hands.

Letters emerged. Old writing, some of it worn away. Several names followed one another. Each figure in the mural seemed to have a name.

Ed ran a finger over the surface. He had never seen these characters before. The lines resembled no script he knew.

"It looks like ancient An writing."

Xiaolin looked over his shoulder.

"I can't read all of it either. Just a few characters."

Ed moved his finger from line to line. Unreadable characters continued. Then he stopped.

"..."

That name alone was written in a different hand. Not in the lines of the ancient An script, but in letters Ed recognized.

He read it once. Then again. The letters didn't change.

**Van Hohenheim.**

His hand went rigid.

"What does it say?"

No answer.

He remembered the record in Karsa.

**He did not claim to know everything in this place.**

**He took what he had learned and headed east.**

Ed looked up at the man in the mural again. This time, it was no longer a hypothesis.

"...It really was him."

"What was?"

Ed kept his mouth closed for a moment.

"My father."

"...Your father?"

"Yeah."

Xiaolin looked back and forth between Ed and the mural.

"You really do look alike."

"Enough about that."

"The hair too."

"I said enough."

"As for the height—"

"Got a death wish?"

Xiaolin shut up. Then looked up at the mural.

"But why is your father in a mural this old?"

"It's complicated."

Ed returned to the tablet. Below the name was another short record in An writing. Xiaolin slowly read out the characters that were recognizable. Not all of it could be read. But the surviving portions were enough.

A man from the west. He spoke of what he knew. And asked about this place's water, wind, and earth.

Ed's gaze rose to the mural. Before, it had looked like a simple gathering of people. Looking again, he saw something different.

In one scene, Hohenheim was explaining something. In another, a local was pointing at a channel. Hohenheim crouched in front of it. In a picture a little farther along, someone pointed at a wall and a windbreak.

Hohenheim was listening.

"...The Sage of the West."

"What?" Xiaolin asked.

"That's what Xing called him."

Ed's gaze traveled along the wall.

"But he was asking an awful lot of questions."

"Are sages not allowed to ask questions?"

"That's not what I mean."

Ed looked down at the tablet. It fit oddly well with the Hohenheim he had known. A man who rarely spoke about himself, but listened to others for a long time.

"He wasn't just teaching."

"Then?"

"He was learning," Ed said. "Telling people what he knew."

He looked at the channel in the mural.

"And listening to what they knew."

Xiaolin shrugged. "So they just learned from each other."

Ed stopped at those words.

"...Yeah."

It was so simple that he had been missing it. Karsa had possessed its methods before Hohenheim. So had the An. Xing had medicine and ideas about chi, and knowledge of how to read the earth's Dragon's Pulse.

Hohenheim hadn't created all of it. He had passed among them. Left what he had. Taken what others had. And hundreds of years had passed.

"Alkahestry too," Ed murmured.

"What?"

"It wasn't made by one person."

"Where did alkahestry come from all of a sudden?"

"I used to think my father created it."

Ed stared at the wall.

"I wondered if he'd learned something in Karsa and brought it to Xing."

But that wasn't it. At least, it wasn't that simple.

Things that already existed had met. Changed each other. Passed on to someone else. For hundreds of years.

"I shouldn't have been looking only for who came first," Ed said.

Xiaolin smiled. "Didn't you figure that out before?"

Ed turned. "...I was confirming it."

"Right."

"Really."

"Right, right."

"Why aren't you listening?"

"Because I'm hungry."

* * *

It was that afternoon that Ed heard Kairun's story properly.

He had grown up here. Left home young; there was no living to be made in this village. Traveled through many regions, learning formal alkahestry along the way. There was no story of him visiting Karsa. Nor any clear record of who had first taught him.

When he returned several years later, he was an accomplished alkahestrist.

"He read the water's path and brought it back to the dry well," an old man from his village said.

"But he couldn't bring people back up. Even with the water back, there was no way to make a living, and the children kept leaving. Even he couldn't beat poverty."

The man looked at the well for a while, then added.

"He did say some strange things."

Ed leaned forward. "Like what?"

"Alkahestrists say they look at the flow of the earth, don't they?"

"The Dragon's Pulse."

"That's it."

The man thought for a moment.

"Kairun asked why they looked only at the earth."

Ed's gaze stopped.

"Those exact words?"

"Something like them."

"Anything else?"

"Well..."

The man smiled.

"Everyone here talks about water and wind."

Ed didn't answer. In that instant, he saw the conference room again. Kairun hadn't been watching only Lan Fan.

Floor. Pillar. Wall. Ceiling. Then back to the floor.

One circle. Yet the transmutation had continued.

"...That was it."

"What was?" Xiaolin asked.

Ed stood.

"Karsa made paths. In advance. Paths for the force to travel along."

"Karsa again?"

"Alkahestry reads the paths in the earth."

The Dragon's Pulse.

"But Kairun wasn't just looking at the earth."

"If not just the earth, then what?"

"At least the direction a building carries its loads. He saw that."

The attack in Lansi's conference room played out again. Transmutation beginning in the floor and spreading to the pillars, the walls, the ceiling.

The man from the village had said it: talk of water and wind was something anyone here might say.

"I don't know if he saw as far as the water or the wind. But he was someone who read the things that were already moving. That much, I'd bet."

"There weren't multiple circles. A transmutation started once and didn't stop there."

"How?"

"That I don't know yet."

Xiaolin sighed. "So you don't know after all."

"I can see the principle."

"You said you didn't know the principle."

"I said I don't know exactly how he did it!"

Having said it, Ed fell quiet himself.

It wasn't a new force. It was the same world. The scope of what he saw was different.

"The laws weren't different," he murmured. "The scope was."

"Then did Kairun invent it?" Xiaolin asked.

"I don't know that. Where's the proof he was the first?"

Hohenheim had seen the ideas of this region and learned from them. Countless others must have passed through afterward.

"He might have been exceptionally good at it."

Beyond that, he didn't know yet.

* * *

As the sun began to sink, Ed asked one final question.

"Who did Kairun spend time with after he came back?"

Now that he understood the technique, it was time to find out where and how Kairun had refined it.

Xiaolin pointed toward Haojin.

"Recently, he was right in the middle of the people over there. A lot of them followed him."

"Who?"

"The people against the road."

"Chenluo?"

"Chenluo knows Kairun, sure. But he's different," Xiaolin said. "He objects because of his land and wells."

"And them?"

"They don't like the imperial court interfering in An affairs at all."

Ed recalled Kairun's final words.

**Whether our land is ours. Does the emperor decide that too?**

"Hard-liners."

"Yes."

"Ling can look into that."

Ed turned away without much interest. Then Xiaolin stopped.

"Huh?"

"What?"

"Those."

Boxes were stacked nearby. Cloth. Dried medicinal herbs. Food. Travel supplies.

Ed looked. "What about them?"

"They're from Lansi."

"Then someone bought them in Lansi."

"I suppose."

Ed took two steps. Xiaolin was still looking at the boxes.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"What is it?"

"There's more than I'd expected."

Ed came back. "Is it strange for Lansi goods to be in Haojin?"

"Not at all."

Xiaolin shook their head. "They go back and forth all the time."

"Then what's the problem?"

"But they bought all this?"

Ed narrowed his eyes. "Why?"

"They haven't done much trading lately."

Ed looked at the boxes again.

"Could be on credit."

"Could be."

"Someone might have given it to them."

"That too."

Ed thought for a moment. He had seen where Kairun had grown up. The ideas he had grown up among. Found traces of his father passing through the same land centuries earlier. He thought he understood a little of what Kairun had been looking at.

And now an entirely different question had appeared.

"...This."

Ed tapped a box.

"Who paid for it?"

"I don't know."

Belatedly, he remembered the story he had heard in Lansi the morning he left.

**The Bai clan had been directing the An from behind the scenes.**

He had thought it was nonsense. It might still be nonsense.

Ed looked at the boxes, then said, "Let's go back to Lansi."

Xiaolin's eyes widened.

"Now?"

"Yeah."

"What about food?"

Ed stopped. His stomach growled.

"...After we eat."

Xiaolin nodded.

"That one's certain."

"What is?"

"More than anything else we've found today."

"Shut up."
