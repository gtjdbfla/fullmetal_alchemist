# Chapter 24: Two Futures

It was the sixth day since they'd come back from Mulanhe.

"That won't work."

*Thump.* The official set the papers down.

Ling's eyebrow twitched. "Why not?"

"If you ban outsiders from buying land entirely, even existing transactions become a problem."

"Then just around the new road."

"There is no road yet."

"Which is why, before there is one—"

"How can you set rules when you don't know where it'll be?"

Ling closed his mouth.

Across from him, Ed propped his chin on his hand. "Lots of things even an emperor can't do, huh."

"Shut up."

Lan Fan held out the next document. "Well rights."

"Again?"

Land. Wells. Pasture. Purchases by outsiders. Sacred sites and cemeteries. They were only looking into a single new road—and there were more things outside the road than the road itself.

* * *

In the afternoon, Xiaolin and Chenluo came back to Lansi. This time a merchant from Haojin, a herder from Mulanhe, and people who used the pastures came with them.

Within ten minutes the meeting was loud.

"What's wrong with outsiders buying land?"

"You only say that because you don't have any!"

"They say business will grow if there's a road."

"And will the water grow when business does?"

Ling rapped on the table. "Hold on."

The noise died down.

Ling picked up the paper in front of him. "Let me go over what's come up so far."

*Existing ownership and use rights to land shall not be changed arbitrarily.*

*If land is needed for roads and relay stations, the people of the area shall be consulted first.*

*Existing well users shall be protected.*

*Sacred sites and cemeteries shall be excluded from the route.*

*Land purchases by outsiders shall be recorded and restricted if necessary.*

An old herder who had come up from Mulanhe raised his hand. "Who does the recording?"

"The government office."

"By the time the office knows, it'll already be sold. Out where we are, rumors travel faster than documents."

Ling was quiet for a moment. "...I'll put someone in charge of records in Mulanhe too."

"And who chooses that person?"

Ling opened his mouth, then looked at Lan Fan. Lan Fan shook her head.

"I can't decide that right now."

Ling added one more line to the paper.

*And the people of the An shall take part in reviewing the actual route.*

Xiaolin spoke first. "If it's that much, I don't see a reason to oppose reviewing the road."

Chenluo looked down at the document. "Let me ask one thing."

"What?"

"Is this something Your Majesty guarantees?"

"For now, yes."

"Will the next emperor keep it?"

Ling's answer stopped. "That... I can't guarantee."

"Then it isn't finished yet."

"Still, we have to start with what we can do now," Xiaolin said.

"I'm not saying we shouldn't." Chenluo looked at Ling. "Your Majesty making a good decision..." A pause. "...and us holding that right are different things."

Ling nodded slowly. "I know."

This time he didn't add anything after it.

* * *

That evening, a cart that had left Lansi headed south. It belonged to a merchant who always traveled between Haojin and Lansi. The load wasn't much different from usual. There was just one more small sheet of paper in it.

*Survey of land ownership and use.*

*Survey of well users.*

*Review of records of land transactions by outsiders.*

*Review of protective regulations at the imperial level.*

It wasn't an imperial document. Someone had simply written up what they'd heard in Lansi.

The cart rolled on the next day. And the day after that. And so the talk that came out of Lansi went south.

* * *

Three days later. Haojin.

"They're going to look at land and wells just to build one road?" A middleman read the paper. "They say the Emperor's looking into it himself."

"Our land too?"

"That's what it says."

"Should we show it to Chenluo?"

The merchant shook his head. "Leave him out of it."

"Why?"

"He's the kind of man who says his own piece."

"Then?"

"Show it to the people who need to see it."

The paper moved on again. Some people read it as it was. Some told others what they'd heard.

The imperial court is surveying land. Surveying wells. Controlling transactions by outsiders. Making new rules.

Taken one at a time, none of it was wrong. But the farther south it went, the less you heard one word that had been on the original paper.

*Protection.*

* * *

In the Lansi market, Lien caught hold of Ed. "Mr. Elric, there's a strange story going around."

"What story?"

"That the Emperor is surveying the An's land so he can 'reorganize' it."

"He said protect! The name of the regulation even starts with protect—"

"That's too long. People shorten things."

"Who said that?"

"Everyone says they heard it. Lots of people heard it, but nobody said it." She added, "Yesterday it was 'reorganize.' This morning at the market, it was 'control.'"

Ed shut his mouth.

"Can't you correct it?"

"Correct it to who?"

Lien gestured at the market. There was no telling who had passed the words along, or how far they'd gone.

Ed remembered the question he had asked Furong. Once words went out, they didn't come back.

* * *

Two more days later. An old warehouse on the outskirts of Mulanhe.

It was where people gathered who hated the very idea of the imperial court meddling in An affairs. People called them the hardliners. People who used the pastures. Small merchants. Haulers who went back and forth to Haojin.

And in the middle of them sat the young man who had questioned Ling a few days earlier.

Kairun.

No matter who was speaking, everyone's eyes went to him now and then. Everyone knew that in this group, his word was the last one.

"It's a story from Lansi." The paper was spread on the floor. "They say the Emperor is surveying the land."

"He already did that."

"This time they say he's making rules."

"What rules?" Kairun asked.

"Who can buy land. Who uses the wells. Where the road will go."

Kairun's eyes stopped on the paper.

He had seen it himself. The Emperor had come all the way down to Mulanhe. He had looked at the well. He had looked at the pastures. He had asked who used the land. At the time, it had been called a survey. But now, back in Lansi, the Emperor was making rules.

"Who decides?"

Someone answered. "The imperial court, I suppose."

"Of course," Kairun said shortly.

"They say this time it's to protect our land."

Kairun looked up. "From whom?"

"What?"

"Who is protecting our land from whom?"

No one answered.

In a corner of the warehouse sat a few small sacks. Goods that had come in from the Lansi side through the usual merchants. Silver coins. Cloth. Old ornaments that would fetch money if sold.

Someone picked up one of the ornaments. There was a worn pattern on one side of it.

"Wonder how much this'd go for."

"Take it to Lansi and it'll sell."

The ornament went back into the sack. Kairun didn't even look.

* * *

Meanwhile, the meetings continued in Lansi. After days of revision, the protection plan was finally laid on the table.

"I don't think this is the end of it," Ling said.

Xiaolin read the document to the end. "But it's enough to start."

"The southern road too?" Chenluo asked.

"It doesn't mean we're building the road," Ling said. "It means we can start a survey."

"Who does the survey?"

"I won't send only people from the imperial court. We'll take people from the areas affected too."

"And if those people are against it?"

Ling thought for a moment. "Then we'll have to look at it again."

"Even if Your Majesty thinks it's necessary?"

"Yes."

"Then we might not be able to do anything," Xiaolin said.

"Maybe not." Ling looked down at the document. "But me wanting a road..." A pause. "...and the people who live there being the ones who own that road are different things."

Xiaolin looked at the document again. Haojin's herbs getting a fair price. The young people who'd left maybe coming back. The An having more choices than they had now. It wasn't perfect. But to her, this document was—a first step forward.

* * *

The protection plan didn't reach the south right away. Imperial documents went through procedures, and copies had to leave Lansi and pass through Haojin again. In the meantime, the rumors went first.

And a few days later, the formal text reached Mulanhe too.

*Provisional Regulations for the Protection of Land and Water Resources in the Western An Region.*

Some people were relieved. Some said it wasn't enough. Some worried about the word "provisional."

Kairun read the document too.

The man beside him said, "See?"

"See what?"

"They say the Emperor will protect us."

Kairun said nothing for a long while.

"So isn't that enough?"

"Before, it was the emperor who took it away."

The man's expression stiffened.

"This time, it's the emperor who protects it."

"That's different."

"How?"

"This time it's good for us."

"And next time?" Kairun asked.

"What?"

"What if the next emperor decides differently?"

The man couldn't answer.

Kairun looked at the title of the document again. "Provisional regulations." His voice was low. "Is our land being ours provisional too?"

"That's not what it means."

"Then who decided it?"

There was no answer.

Kairun folded the document. "It's the same in the end."

"What is?"

"Whether our land is ours," he said. "The emperor decides that."

* * *

That night, people gathered in a small house in Mulanhe.

Kairun was washing his hands beside the water jar by the door. While the others packed, he didn't say a word.

"Let's go to Lansi," someone said.

"And do what?"

"Tell the Emperor directly."

"No matter how much we talk here, one sheet of paper comes down from Lansi and that's the end of it."

"We have to tell him to keep his hands off our land."

Not everyone thought the same way. People who opposed the road itself. People who wanted the protection plan made stronger. People who hated the very idea of an emperor deciding the An's rights. But they agreed on one thing. They would go and say it directly.

A few packed the swords they used for long journeys. Someone loaded a club onto a cart. No one meant to start a fight. But no one meant to go all the way to the city where the Emperor was empty-handed either.

The door opened, and Chenluo came in. "Leave the swords."

The room went quiet.

"We're going to talk. So leave them."

"It's because talking doesn't work that we're in this mess," someone muttered, but Chenluo didn't respond.

Someone looked at Kairun. "You're not bringing a sword?"

"Don't need one."

"I know," Chenluo said, glancing at Kairun. "I've heard you're rough with your words but you never lay a hand on anyone."

"Even all the way to Lansi?"

"Yeah."

Kairun turned to Chenluo. "Are you going too?"

"I'm going."

Chenluo asked in return, "You're going to talk to the Emperor directly?"

Kairun didn't answer for a moment. "Yes."

No one asked what he meant to say.

* * *

The same night. Lansi.

Ed opened his notebook at his lodgings. He wrote down what he'd been hearing for the past few days.

*The imperial court is surveying land and wells.*

True.

*It may restrict land purchases by outsiders.*

Also true.

Below that, he wrote:

*The Emperor is trying to lay hands on the An's land again.*

Ed's pen stopped. "...It's hard to say this one's completely wrong either."

That Ling was trying to protect the An was also a fact. Some people wanted that protection. Some people hated it.

After a long while, Ed wrote one more line.

*Then where did it change?*

He closed the notebook.

At that very hour, on the southern road several days from Lansi, small lights were beginning to move.

Their destination was Lansi.
