# Chapter 4: The God Beyond the Gate

The next morning, Ed sat at one end of a table in the records hall, writing a letter to Al.

> Dear Al,
>
> I made it to Ishval. Scar and Miles are doing fine.
>
> I saw the notebook Scar's brother left. It's like a diary, but a few lines stuck out.
>
> Ishvala = the Truth?
>
> That's what it said. You know what the Truth is.
>
> It also said Ishvala is connected to alkahestry, but I'm not sure about that.
>
> I'm going to keep checking.
>
> Make sure you eat.

Ed folded the letter and slid it into an envelope. Mailing it could wait. Verifying came first.

There were more books on the table than yesterday. More precisely, Ed had pulled out more. Old prayers and religious records lay piled up, and in the middle of them, Ed's notebook lay open.

*What is Ishvala?*

Beneath it.

*The Truth.*

Every time he saw those two words, he felt a little better. He had a hypothesis. Now he only had to confirm it.

"Next."

He unfolded an old sheet of paper.

> The world is one, and all life is within it.

He marked it. "One."

Next.

> One who does not know oneself cannot know the world.

"Self."

Marked again. Next.

> Before the door, a human being comes face to face with himself.

Ed's hand stopped. "...This one too."

Marked.

The more he looked, the more he found. The world. One. The self. The door. God. The wording differed slightly each time, but it was close to what he'd heard from the Truth. Yesterday's conclusion seemed to be getting more solid.

But then—

A few pages later, Ed's hand slowed.

"...That's weird."

Something was missing. He couldn't tell what. He reopened the records he'd read earlier. The door. The world. The self. God. They were all there.

And yet.

There was something that should always come along when he thought of the Truth. He stared at the table for a long time. In the end, he couldn't find it.

"What is it?"

* * *

Toward sunset, Ed went to the reconstruction site.

Scar was rebuilding a collapsed garden wall. Ed crouched beside him.

"Hey."

Scar set a stone. "What."

"Don't the old Ishvalan records have stories like—a person who saw god lost an arm?"

Scar's hand stopped. "No."

"Or a leg."

"No."

"Or went blind."

"No."

Ed's eyes narrowed. "Really?"

Scar looked at him. "Why should there be?"

"Because it's the price."

"For what?"

"For seeing the Gate."

Scar studied Ed for a moment. "Why would one have to pay?"

Ed spoke up immediately. "Because that's obvious. To gain something, you need to give something of equal—"

He stopped. Scar waited. Ed blinked.

"...Well."

"Go on."

"Never mind."

Ed stood. "I'll check again tomorrow."

"Check what?"

"The price."

Scar went back to stacking stones. "I told you there are no such records."

"Maybe you just don't know about them."

"That is possible."

He admitted it too easily. It left Ed speechless.

"...Right. That's why I'm checking."

* * *

The next day, Ed asked the keeper the moment he walked into the records hall.

"Do you have any records about sacrifice?"

The old man looked at him. "What kind of sacrifice?"

"Offering something to god."

"We have rites."

"Not those. I mean stories about seeing god, or getting something, and losing something in return."

The old man thought for a moment. "Nothing comes to mind."

"What about the word 'price'?"

"The price of what?"

Ed shut his mouth. Same question again.

"I'll just... start with the oldest ones."

That day, Ed changed how he read. He wasn't looking for what Ishvala was. Instead, he looked for what people gave up. Sacrifice. Exchange. Price. Punishment. Being deprived.

But it didn't come up. There were records about offerings. There were later religious rituals. There were prayers for washing away sin. But none of it was what Ed was looking for.

What had happened before the Truth. The structure in which, because one has seen something, something is taken. It wasn't there.

Long after noon, Ed read one old prayer. It was short.

> A river does not lose its water.

His eyes moved.

> The water changes form and flows on.

The next line.

> There is nothing to give back to the earth.

Ed's eyebrows rose. And finally.

> For nothing ever left it.

Ed didn't move for a while. He read it again from the beginning.

> A river does not lose its water.
>
> The water changes form and flows on.
>
> There is nothing to give back to the earth.
>
> For nothing ever left it.

"...What is this?"

It wasn't completely alien to alchemy. Matter is not destroyed. Only its form changes. But the way of saying it was different.

Without realizing it, Ed muttered, "Still, if you get something, then that much—"

He stopped.

**If you get something.**

The phrase snagged in him. He looked at the prayer again. There was no mention of anyone getting anything. Or of anyone giving anything. It just flowed. It changed form. And it had been in there all along.

Ed leaned back in his chair. "...Wait."

* * *

That afternoon, Ed went down to the underground prayer hall again. This time Scar came too.

"What now?"

"There's something I need to check."

"You said that yesterday."

"That's why I'm still checking."

Ed sat in front of the circular pattern on the floor. When he'd first seen it, he'd thought it was a transmutation circle. Then he'd thought it was a Gate tied to the Truth. This time, he looked at the lines. The lines that spread from the circle didn't end in one place. People. Earth. Trees. Animals. People again. The figures were linked by lines.

"This symbol." Ed pointed to one spot. "What does it mean?"

Scar looked down. "It is not exactly the same, but similar symbols appear in old records."

"Meaning?"

"Flow."

Ed shut his mouth. That word again.

"No offerings or anything here?"

"No."

"Nothing given to a god?"

"Not that I know of."

Ed stood and went to the relief on the wall. The one he'd seen yesterday. The person. The rectangle that looked like a door. The humanlike being beyond it.

> One who sees Ishvala sees oneself.

This time, he looked for something else. Arms. Legs. Eyes. Any trace of a body part missing. There was none. The figure had been whole before going in, and whole after coming out.

Of course, it was only a relief. It might not have depicted everything.

Ed crossed his arms. "Maybe the price just wasn't recorded."

Scar said, "That is possible."

"...Why aren't you arguing with me today?"

"You are doing it yourself."

"Who said anyone was arguing?"

Scar didn't answer. Ed looked at the relief again.

Until yesterday, it had looked so clear. Door. God. Self. Truth. But today, he saw what wasn't there.

* * *

After sunset, back at Scar's house, Ed opened the records he'd copied during the day. This time he checked even the parts he'd skipped over on the first read. The sections where old Ishvalan records had been compiled. Ed's hand stopped.

"There's no word for sacrifice."

Scar looked up. "What is missing?"

"No word for exchange either."

Ed checked page after page. Nothing. Not anywhere.

He turned the next page. A section where old sentences had been copied down.

> The flow may be borrowed.

Beneath it.

> But one must never think of it as one's own.

Ed's eyes narrowed. "Borrowed."

The next page. A later religious record.

> Humans must not carelessly alter Ishvala's creation.

Ed laid the two sentences side by side. The old record.

> The flow may be borrowed.

The later record.

> Humans must not carelessly alter Ishvala's creation.

They seemed similar, and yet they weren't.

"Maybe it was the other way around."

Scar looked at him. "What was?"

"I thought the Ishvalans forbade changing the world from the start."

"Many people teach it that way even now."

"But the old record's a little different." Ed tapped the first sentence. "It's not saying don't change it."

The next sentence. "It's saying don't think of it as yours."

Scar was silent. Ed sank further into thought.

"If using the flow wasn't itself the sin..."

Back to the old prayer.

> There is nothing to give back to the earth.
>
> For nothing ever left it.

Ed thought of the phrase he'd used so naturally. Equivalent Exchange. To gain something, you must give something equal. He had thought of it as a law of the world. Ever since he was little. While learning alchemy. And even in front of the Truth.

He said slowly, "It wasn't missing."

"What was not?"

"The records about the price."

Ed looked down at the records. "They weren't left out." After a pause. "These people just didn't think that way to begin with."

Scar's eyes narrowed slightly. "Think what?"

"That you sacrifice something to get something else."

Ed traced a prayer with his finger. "At least in these records."

That was as far as he could be sure. Still, Ed added one thing. "That doesn't mean it's free."

"Water doesn't just appear from nowhere, and the earth doesn't vanish. They change form and they move."

Scar listened quietly.

"But I don't think these people thought of it as a trade." Ed spread both hands. "You take something from the earth," one hand, "and give something back in return." The other. "That's not it." He moved his hands together. "They thought what was already there just keeps flowing."

The room went quiet. Ed looked at the records. If so, there was now a problem. A very big one.

* * *

Ed opened his notebook.

*What is Ishvala?*

Beneath it.

*The Truth.*

He lifted his pen and held it over those two words for a long time.

Scar asked, "Will you cross it out?"

Ed thought for a moment. "No."

"Why not?"

"There's no proof it's wrong."

"There is not enough proof it is right."

"That was true yesterday, too."

Ed didn't strike a line through *The Truth*. Instead, he wrote a new question beneath it.

*Then what about the price?*

After a moment's thought, he added one more.

*Why was no one deprived of anything?*

He looked at that sentence for a long time. He had opened the Gate. He had lost an arm and a leg. Al had lost his body. Izumi had lost her organs. Mustang had lost his sight. All of them were Amestrian alchemists.

Ed looked up. "Scar."

"What."

"Ever hear of anyone in Xing who saw the Truth?"

Scar thought. "I have never heard such a thing from May."

"Anyone who lost a body part?"

Scar answered with annoyance. "I do not know."

"Anyone who used alkahestry and saw the Gate?"

"I said I do not know."

Ed looked back at his notebook.

*What about Xing?*

He wrote it briefly. Then he turned to a new page. The pen touched the paper.

*Why did only Amestrian alchemists see the Truth?*

He looked at the sentence. This time, no answer came.

Ed set the question aside and went back to skimming the prayers. At the very end, in small writing, there was an added line.

> Those who borrowed the flow went to Karsa, at the eastern edge.

Ed's hand stopped. He remembered the strange word in the notebook's margin.

> Karsa. East. The end.

"...It wasn't a person's name."

Scar looked up. "What are you saying?"

"Karsa. It's a place."

Ed pointed to the prayer. "It's where the people who borrowed the flow went. Your brother wrote it down after seeing this."

"...Is there such a place?"

"That, I don't know yet."

The corner of Ed's mouth lifted. "I'll have to look for it."

* * *

A little later, Ed paused while tidying the materials on the table. Something was snagging in his head.

The thing he'd been looking at since yesterday. The flow. The prayer hall. The lines. The crossings. And—

"Wait."

Scar looked up. "What now?"

"That stone."

"Which stone?"

"The one in the prayer hall floor."

Dark red. Small stones set at regular intervals along the lines. Ed stood. "Why were they set exactly where the lines meet?"

Scar didn't answer. Thoughts raced through Ed's head.

The old records said the flow could be borrowed. The lines in the prayer hall looked like they represented the flow. And at each crossing of those lines there was a red stone.

He had been leaning toward there being no price. But Ed still wasn't convinced. Things you borrow come with a price. He'd learned that all his life.

"Maybe..."

Ed muttered. "What if that's the price of the flow?"

Scar said, "Here we go again."

"No, think about it."

"It is late."

"So?"

"It is night."

"We can take torches."

"We will go tomorrow."

"We could look right now."

"Tomorrow."

Ed pouted. "You got boring when you got older."

"I have always been this way."

"That's true."

On one side of the table lay Scar's brother's notebook. Ed had already copied down everything worth copying. He pushed it toward Scar.

"I'll leave the notebook with you tonight."

"You finally return it."

"I told you I was borrowing it."

"I never lent it."

Ed sat back down. Three questions remained in his notebook.

*What is Ishvala?*

*Then what about the price?*

And—

*Why did only Amestrian alchemists see the Truth?*

Yesterday, he'd thought he'd found the answer to the first question. Today, he hadn't.

What lay beyond the Gate, he still didn't know. This time, though, he planned to start by checking the stones set into the floor.
