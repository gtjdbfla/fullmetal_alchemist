# Chapter 28: A War Without Fire

The day after word of Zhenha got out, Lansi grew louder.

"They say it's true the Bai put up the money."

"The imperial court confirmed it."

"Then the assassination was the Bai too."

"I heard it wasn't."

"They gave money and it wasn't them? Then what was it?"

"The An tried to kill the Emperor first."

"They said it was one man."

"Who believes that?"

In the middle of the market, Ed scowled. It was worse than yesterday.

The Bai had supported the An opposition. True. That the support had been for assassinating the Emperor. Not confirmed. Kairun had tried to kill the Emperor. True. All of the An had taken part in the assassination. False.

People rarely kept facts and guesses apart.

Ed grabbed the shoulder of the man who'd been talking loudest. "What's confirmed is that they gave money. That's it."

"If they gave money, it's the same thing."

"It's *not* the same!"

People turned to look. Ed shut his mouth. He'd given the same explanation yesterday.

Just then, something shattered on the western street. Shouting broke out right after. People began to surge in that direction.

Ed looked that way for a moment. Then he ran.

* * *

In front of a Bai merchant's shop on the western street, grain sacks had burst open. Bai and An merchants had split into two sides and were glaring at each other.

"You're the ones who've been shoveling goods to the An!"

"We didn't know either!"

Ed squeezed in among the people and snatched a stone someone had picked up. "What's throwing that going to do? If you crack someone's head open, does the money Zhenha gave disappear?"

It got him nowhere. Every time he pushed one person back, someone else came in.

With alchemy, it would have been easy. Make the ground surge up and split the two sides apart, and that would be the end of it. Now he had to block them one at a time with his own body.

Footsteps sounded behind him.

"Everyone, back off!"

It was the Lansi garrison. People flinched.

Lan Fan came in between the soldiers.

Ed spoke, breathing hard. "You're late."

"By order of His Majesty the Emperor."

"What?"

"Guards will be posted around the An and Bai merchant houses."

A shout went up from the Bai side at once. "You're protecting the An?"

The other side didn't stay quiet either. "You're just trying to watch us!"

"The same number of troops will be posted on both sides," Lan Fan said.

"Why are we being treated the same as them?!"

"From this point on, anyone who joins the disturbance will be arrested, the same as anyone else."

It went quiet.

Ed looked at Lan Fan. "Ling told you to?"

"Yes."

"That was fast."

"You are slow."

"I was holding them off by myself until a minute ago!"

Lan Fan didn't answer.

"Hey!"

* * *

Reports had been piling up at the government office since morning.

"Three An merchant houses have stopped doing business today."

"Two Bai merchant houses have closed their warehouses too."

"An inn on the west side says it won't take any more An guests."

"Some An haulers say they won't carry goods for Bai merchant houses—"

"Hold on." Ling raised his hand. The reports stopped.

A map of Lansi was spread on the table. Ling pointed to several places on it. "Post guards around the An and Bai merchant houses, the same number on each side."

One official asked, "Some places carry different levels of risk."

"I know."

"And still the same number?"

"Right now, a difference of even one person will look like taking sides."

The official closed his mouth.

Ling pointed to another spot. "Check the inns where a lot of people from both sides are staying."

"Should we separate them?"

"Only the ones who want it."

"Wouldn't it be safer to do it by force?"

"Then by tomorrow morning there'll be a rumor that the Emperor locked up the An."

Ed, who had been leaning by the door, said, "Could be tonight."

Ling pointed at Ed. "Write that down."

"Write what down?!"

An official held back a laugh.

Ling looked back at the map. "For anyone who wants to move, arrange other lodgings. The imperial household will pay."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"And keep the investigation into Zhenha limited to the merchant houses connected to him."

Zhenha looked at Ling.

"Don't investigate all the Bai," Ling said.

Luhan's eyes came up. "What about Kairun's side?"

"The same," Ling answered at once. "I won't investigate all the An because of Kairun." He added, "But the people who moved with Kairun and took Zhenha's money stay locked up until the investigation's done."

An official bowed his head. "Twelve An hardliners."

That bothered Ed. The side that took the money gets locked up, and the side that gave it gets its ledgers checked first. It was the right order. That was exactly why it bothered him.

A moment of silence passed.

"Your Majesty," another official said carefully. "Might it be better to suspend the talks on the southern trade road for now?"

Ling looked at him. "Why?"

"Feelings are running high on both sides right now. If the talks continue, it may only—"

"And if we suspend them?"

"Your Majesty?"

"Kairun tried to kill me, so I won't talk to the An." Ling's finger touched the An region. "Zhenha put up money, so I won't talk to the Bai either." This time, Lansi. "If we take them out one at a time like that, who's left to talk to?"

No one answered.

"Keep the talks going," Ling said.

Ed looked up.

Ling added one thing. "But we won't decide anything."

"What?"

"For now, I'm only going to listen."

Ed looked at Ling for a moment. "That's not bad."

Ling grinned. "Right?"

"Your face makes me want to take it back."

"Why?!"

* * *

Only Zhenha was left in the meeting room.

"Why did you send money to the An opposition without telling me?" Ling asked.

"Because Your Majesty is not the Bai's Majesty."

"So?"

"Your Majesty can protect the Bai. But you will not protect them first."

Ling couldn't answer.

"That is why there are people like me."

Zhenha bowed his head and left.

* * *

After the meeting, behind the government office, Chenluo stopped Ling.

"Twelve people."

"I know."

"Twelve people are locked up because they took Zhenha's money."

"Once the investigation's done—"

"And the side that gave the money?"

Ling couldn't answer.

"I heard it's a ledger investigation. The side that took the money goes to jail, and the side that gave it gets its ledgers checked?"

"Both under the same standard—"

"The same standard doesn't make the same result." Chenluo's voice was low. "The ones locked up are An. They followed Kairun, and they still don't know why Kairun went alone. I don't know either."

"I'll let out the ones who can be let out. Give me a few days."

"A few days," Chenluo repeated. "I suppose we'll watch who else dies in those few days."

He bowed his head and turned away. Ed felt that the back walking away was a little different from the Chenluo of yesterday.

* * *

Before noon was over, there was more trouble. This time it was at an inn where An merchants were staying.

By the time Ed and Lan Fan got there, a dozen or so young Bai men were blocking the entrance.

"Give us proof Kairun acted alone!"

Ed pushed in among them. "There's no proof like that."

"Then that means someone was behind it!"

"There's no proof of that either! We don't *know*."

"Then what are we supposed to believe?!"

"Believe what's confirmed!" Ed held up his fingers one at a time. "Zhenha put up the money." One. "Kairun attacked Ling." Two. "There's no proof the two are directly connected." Three. "That's what's confirmed right now."

"So you're saying Kairun did it alone?!"

"That's—"

*Crash.*

An inn window shattered. A bottle flew inside.

"Hey!"

The door flew open. An people came rushing out. Someone shouted, "Not this again!"

People on the Bai side stepped forward too.

Lan Fan moved to Ed's side. "Get back!"

No one listened.

One man pulled out a short club.

Ed grimaced. "He didn't have that earlier."

"Is that what matters right now?"

"It matters!"

The club swung. Lan Fan knocked his wrist aside. At the same moment, two men charged from the other side.

"Ed!"

"I see them!"

Ed slipped past the first man's arm. The second came in from the side. Ed twisted and shoved with his shoulder. The two of them tangled together.

"Right!" Ed shouted.

Without even looking, Lan Fan knocked an attacker's arm away with her elbow.

"Two!"

"I saw."

"If you saw, say so!"

"You are slow."

"You said that earlier too!"

In an instant, the front of the inn was a tangle. You couldn't even tell who was An and who was Bai.

Ed pushed one man away as he charged. Dodged a fist. Caught an arm. Threw him. As gently as he could. That was actually the harder part.

Just then the inn door opened again. A child came running out, crying.

Ed's expression changed. "Wait!"

He threw himself toward the child. A flying piece of wood grazed his back. Ed wrapped himself around the child and rolled across the ground.

"You okay?"

The child nodded, still crying.

"Go back inside. Close the door and stay in there."

The moment he sent the child back, soldiers poured in from both ends of the alley.

"By order of His Majesty the Emperor!"

The garrison began separating people.

"Put down your weapons!"

"They attacked first!"

"We got hit first!"

The commander kept repeating the same thing. "Both sides, back off!"

"Why us too?!"

"*Both* sides!"

Ed stepped back, breathing hard.

Lan Fan stood beside him. "Are you hurt?"

"No."

"Your back."

"It just grazed me."

Lan Fan checked for a moment. "You will not die."

"Who said I was dying?"

"I confirmed it."

"Why do you talk like that?"

Lan Fan was already turning away. Ed followed with a look of disbelief.

A few steps later, Ed asked, "Are you okay?"

"About what?"

"Kairun."

Lan Fan walked on without answering. Then: "I do not think I disobeyed my orders."

A moment later. "But his face stays with me."

Ed didn't ask anything more.

Even in the middle of it all, the soldiers were pushing both sides back the same distance. Who started it could wait. Stopping it came first. Exactly as Ling had ordered that morning.

Ed watched it for a moment. At least for now, it was the right order.

* * *

"I did good, right?"

When Ed got back to the government office that evening, that was the first thing Ling asked.

"You did good. Increasing the guards, splitting up the inns, not forcing it."

"Of course."

"But one thing bugged me." Ed dropped into a chair. His back stung a little. "An An merchant told me that because the guards are the same number, it feels more like being watched."

Ling's smile vanished. "...I missed that."

"Then fix it."

"I'll ask those people myself tomorrow morning."

Ed put down his cup. "And just because you got attacked doesn't mean everything Kairun said was wrong."

Ling's face stiffened a little. "I know."

"Really?"

"I do think about things too."

"Even as emperor?"

"What do you think I am?"

"An emperor who eats a ton."

"That's true."

Ed snorted. Then he spoke again. "Today, somebody had to decide fast."

"Yeah."

"People are fighting, and you can't get everyone together for a meeting about how many guards to put on which side."

"Then it'd get decided tomorrow."

"And everyone would've fought it out before that."

Ling nodded.

Ed went on. "But the southern road is different."

Ling didn't answer.

"What happens to An land. Who uses the wells. Where the road goes." Ed looked at Ling. "Just because your order saved people today doesn't mean you have to decide all that by yourself."

A silence passed.

Ling looked out the window. Guards were standing on every street.

"Still, today I had to decide."

"Right."

"If I hadn't, more people would've gotten hurt."

"Probably."

"Then..." Ling paused for a moment. "How far does it go?"

"How far does what go?"

"How much am I supposed to decide?"

Ed looked at Ling. The answer didn't come quickly. In the old days, he might have started with what he thought. But this wasn't a transmutation formula. It wasn't a problem where you found the one right answer and wrote it in.

"I don't know."

Ling turned to look at him. "You don't know?"

"Nope."

"After all that talking?"

"I'm talking *because* I don't know."

Ling closed his mouth.

Ed folded his arms. "I can tell you what I think."

"Yeah."

"But how far you're supposed to decide..." He paused. "...isn't that something you should settle with the people it affects?"

Ling said nothing for a while. Then he smiled a little. "Then I'll have to decide how to decide that first."

Ed narrowed his eyes. "You again?"

"Ah." Ling stopped.

Ed laughed. "Caught you."

"Shut up."

This time it was Ling who'd said it first.

* * *

Around sunset, Xiaolin came up to Lansi. She hadn't sold a single sack of herbs.

"When the Haojin merchants heard about the unrest in Lansi, they all stopped trading. A week's worth of herbs is drying out as it is."

"How is that your fault?"

"I'm the one who talked about the road."

Xiaolin set down the sack. "I just wanted to find a way for all of us to live well. The An and the Bai."

Ed couldn't answer. He was the one who'd brought up the road first.

"...Fine. I'll talk to a merchant I know."

"Furong?"

"That guy'll want money, though."

Even so, Ed got up.

* * *

That night, there were no fires in Lansi. No shops burned. No one died.

The imperial court announced that this was fortunate. It really was fortunate.

But the An merchants began sleeping together with other An. The Bai merchants moved to inns close to each other. Haulers who had carried goods together until yesterday went back over their business partners. Who had taken money from whom. Which merchant house was close to which clan. Who you'd look like you were siding with if you traded with them.

There were more guards on the streets. People said it was safer now. And they drifted a little further from each other.

Ed opened the window of his lodgings. The noise of the day was gone. Guards stood at regular intervals.

Across the street, an An merchant closed his shop door. A little farther off, a Bai merchant brought the rest of his goods inside. The two of them looked at each other. Neither said a word. Soon their doors closed.

* * *

Late that night, a letter slid under Ed's door. It was Al's handwriting.

> Brother. May found more material than I expected. Enough to send in boxes. I've got a feeling you're getting into trouble again, so I'm hurrying.

Ed held the letter for a long time. "...Who's getting into trouble?"

His mouth grumbled, but he folded the letter and tucked it into his notebook.

Ed rested his arms on the windowsill. Ling's order today had been right. At least for today. No one else had gotten hurt. The child hadn't been hit. No shop had caught fire.

But the question Ling had asked that evening stayed in his head.

**How much am I supposed to decide?**

Ed didn't know the answer. It wasn't an answer he could decide alone to begin with.

No one was fighting in the streets. No swords had been drawn. There was no fire.

But on the road between the two shops, no one was walking.

Ed looked at that empty road for a while.

There was no war.

But to call it peace,

it was a strange sort of night.
