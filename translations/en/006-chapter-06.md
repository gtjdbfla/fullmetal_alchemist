# Chapter 6: Good Intentions

The rumor outran the analysis.

It started at the construction sites.

"I heard they found a new mine."

"Not a mine, apparently."

"Then what?"

"Some really valuable mineral came up."

By lunchtime, it had changed.

"They say it's a stone you almost never find, even in Amestris."

"I heard the military uses it?"

"Is it worth money?"

By evening it had grown bigger.

"They say the military even sent people."

Ed looked up from his bread. "Who showed up?"

The worker sitting across from him said, "They say someone came from Central to investigate."

"Nobody came."

"What?"

"Nobody came, I said."

"Really?" The worker turned to the man beside him. "He says no."

The man shrugged. "That's what I heard."

Ed set his bread down. "So this is why rumors are scary."

Scar said, "You were the one going around looking for stones."

"How's that my fault?"

"People would have seen you."

"I was investigating."

"And the people who saw you talked."

Ed shut his mouth. Come to think of it, that wasn't wrong.

* * *

In Miles's office, a small box containing red stones sat on the desk. It was the sample to be sent to Central.

Ed asked, "Where did it leak from?"

"I do not know."

"Construction crews?"

"Likely."

"The military side?"

"It could be."

Miles pulled an old report from beneath some papers. "A name appears in the military warehouse records. Crimstone."

"Crimstone?"

"It resists heat and pressure, so it was used in weapons. Few places produce it, and the output is extremely small."

Ed looked at the stone in the box. "You're saying it's the same thing?"

"Similar. Not confirmed."

"Then the military might go after it."

"Can't you stop that?"

Miles looked up from his papers. "Dozens of people saw the red layer while digging."

"..."

"Do you intend to tell them to keep quiet for the rest of their lives?"

"That's not what I'm saying."

"Then it will spread eventually." Miles closed the sample box. "What matters is establishing the facts faster than the rumors."

"What about Central?"

"We will send the sample. With a request for detailed analysis."

"And the geological survey?"

"We will request that too."

Ed pressed a hand to the desk. "Then Central sends people right away?"

"It is still only a request."

"If it's approved?"

"Then we will have to discuss it."

"With who?"

Miles looked at Ed. "Deciding that is part of it."

Ed didn't answer.

* * *

That afternoon, people gathered at the town hall. It was supposed to be a meeting on the reconstruction schedule. The water pipes. The roads. The school building. Materials arriving next month. But before the meeting even began, a question came up.

"Is the story about a mine true?"

The murmuring crowd went quiet.

Miles stood at the front. Beside him were a few Ishvalan elders and reconstruction officials. Ed leaned against the back wall. He hadn't planned on speaking. But Miles looked at him.

"You should explain."

"Why me?"

"You were one of the first to confirm it."

"I'm not a mineral expert."

"Then say only what you know."

Everyone's eyes turned to Ed. He sighed and stepped forward.

"First." He thought for a moment. "A mine hasn't been found."

A few people looked at one another.

"Then what is that stone?"

"We still don't know exactly."

"They say it's valuable."

"It might be."

The murmuring grew louder. Ed raised a hand. "Hear me out."

It quieted.

"It looks like a mineral that turns up in tiny amounts elsewhere in Amestris. If it's the same one, it can be used in special alloys, and yes, it's expensive."

The expressions around the room changed.

"So it's worth money, isn't it?"

"I said we don't know yet," Ed said immediately. "We need a detailed analysis to tell if it's the same mineral, and we don't know how much is down here."

"But it's right there on the ground."

"You see it in one place. How would anyone know how much is underground?" Ed pointed at the floor. "There's water under here, but that doesn't make all of Ishval a lake."

A few people nodded. Another hand went up.

"If it's really valuable, can we mine it ourselves?"

Ed stopped. "That's..."

This time a question flew in from another direction.

"Isn't the military going to take it?"

"No decision like that has—"

"It came out of our ground. Why would the military decide?"

"Who said the military would?"

"Then who decides?"

The hall went silent. Ed closed his mouth. The crowd's gaze turned to Miles, naturally.

Miles didn't answer right away. It was only a few seconds. But to Ed, it felt strangely long.

"Nothing has been decided," Miles said.

"Then what happens next?"

"We request analysis and a geological survey from Central."

"Doesn't that mean Central decides?"

"Requesting a survey and deciding on development are different matters."

Someone said quietly, "It probably started as a survey last time too."

The mood shifted. Ed looked toward the voice.

It was a man leaning against the wall. Mid-thirties. Thin build. Red eyes. His voice wasn't loud, but strangely it carried.

Zahir.

He was a man of few words. But when he spoke, the hall went quiet, and the residents watched his reaction first. Even Miles checked his face first when he asked for the residents' opinions.

Right now, he was one of the most influential people in Ishval.

The young man beside him frowned. "So you're saying we should do nothing?"

People looked at him. Ed didn't recognize him, either. Late twenties, maybe. Dirt on his work clothes. Ed thought he'd seen him a few times that day, at the road construction site.

"Adin," someone said.

Adin said, "If that stone is worth money, we should at least have it surveyed."

An older man said, "You want us to let them dig up our land again?"

"Has anyone said they'd dig?"

"The military is doing the survey."

"The military is helping with the reconstruction, too."

"So you're telling us to trust the military?"

Adin's face hardened. "That's not what I'm saying."

"Then what?"

"If that stone is really valuable—" Adin pointed around them. A house that still had no roof. The water system under construction. A school without windows. "We could use it, too."

"And if we get robbed because of that money?"

"Then we should think about how not to get robbed."

"Adin."

"Sacred land?"

Adin's voice rose. "And how long are the kids supposed to keep walking two hours for water?"

The hall went silent. Adin didn't stop.

"Just because our parents died, do we have to live poor, too?"

No one could answer right away. Adin said nothing more. He let out one deep breath and sat down.

Ed looked at him. It was strange. It was hard to say who was wrong.

* * *

After the meeting, Ed walked with Scar.

"What do you think?"

"About what?"

"That stone."

Scar kept walking. "I do not know yet."

"I figured you'd be against developing it."

Scar looked at him. "Why?"

"It was used in the prayer hall. And you said before it wasn't a stone you dig up."

"That is a story of the past."

Ed paused. Scar stopped, too.

"So you think it's fine to develop it?"

"I did not say that."

"Then you're against it?"

"Not that either."

Ed's face crumpled. "Which is it?"

"I do not know."

"That's your answer?"

"Yes."

Scar started walking again. "We do not know what the stone is, or how much there is."

"True."

"And what people want is not one thing."

Ed thought back to the meeting. Adin. The older people. The one who'd asked if a mine would mean money. The ones afraid the military would take it.

Without meaning to, he said, "I thought you'd be against it, no matter what."

"Why?"

This time, Ed couldn't answer. An Ishvalan. Faith. A sacred stone. Those were things he'd connected in his head on his own.

"...No reason."

* * *

That evening, Miles came to Scar's house with papers in his hand.

Ed asked right away. "Analysis results?"

"No."

"Then what?"

"Confirmation that Central received the sample."

Ed's face went flat. "You carried that all the way over here?"

"I expected you would come looking tomorrow morning."

"What about the analysis?"

"They will start now, I suppose."

"How long will it take?"

"I do not know."

Ed sighed. Miles set a sheet on the table. "However, the geological survey request has been passed to the relevant department."

Ed read it. *Review of geological survey for the reconstruction area. Possibility of rare mineral production.* And—

**Foundation for economic self-sufficiency.**

Ed's eyes stopped on that phrase. "Economic self-sufficiency."

"Reconstruction costs money," Miles said. "We cannot sustain it on Central's budget forever."

"If there's really a lot of the mineral?"

"Jobs could be created."

"More roads, too."

"Possibly."

"And schools."

"It could happen."

Ed looked at the paper again. It wasn't a bad story. If anything, it was a good one. Ishvalans could work in Ishval. Reconstruction wouldn't have to depend only on aid. Roads would appear. Schools would appear. The town would come back to life.

"Seems all right, doesn't it?" Ed said.

Miles answered, "It could be."

That phrase again.

* * *

Night fell. Unable to sleep, Ed went outside. The town under reconstruction still didn't have many streetlamps. In the distance, a few work lights glowed.

As he walked, he heard the voices of some young people.

"If a mine really opens, could I work here?"

"If they pay properly."

"I was thinking of heading west."

"Why?"

"There's no work here."

A little laughter.

"If it's real, I won't have to leave."

Ed slowed. A little farther on, he heard other voices. Older people.

"They say the military is going to survey it."

"In the end they'll tell us to hand over the land."

"They say it's different this time."

"They had their reasons back then, too."

"Still, the current Fuhrer—"

"And if the Fuhrer changes?"

Ed walked past. It was the same stone. To some, a reason not to have to leave. To others, a reason they might be robbed all over again. Neither side seemed to be lying.

* * *

The next day, Miles took Ed and Scar to the edge of town. It was an area a little way from where the red layer had first been exposed in a big way.

"We have received a request to exclude this area from the survey."

Ed looked around. "Why?"

Scar said, "There is an old cemetery."

In the distance, low mounds of stone were visible.

"Oh." Ed understood at once.

Miles said, "The survey itself has not been approved yet, but we are marking it in advance."

Ed looked at the map. It wasn't far from the direction where the red stone had been found.

"What if there's some under here too?"

Miles didn't answer. Ed turned to Scar. "You still wouldn't dig?"

Scar looked at the cemetery. "Knowing what is there and digging it out are different things."

Ed thought for a moment. "So only the survey?"

"That, too, must be decided by the people here."

Miles said, "It should be."

Scar looked at him. "The one who starts a survey will not end up making the final decision too, I trust."

Miles was quiet for a moment. "It should not."

This time, the same phrase sounded a little heavier.

* * *

When they returned to town, there was a stranger there. Not an Ishvalan. Not a soldier. A well-tailored coat. Boots dusty from the road. A horse beside him with a load of cargo. He was talking to a worker.

Ed asked, "Who's that?"

Miles's expression hardened. "I do not know."

The man noticed them and approached with a smile. "Excuse me."

"What is this about?"

"I'm a merchant who travels the eastern routes."

Miles asked, "Why are you in Ishval?"

"Looking for new trading partners."

"Trading what?"

"That depends on the circumstances."

The man's eyes flicked briefly toward the construction site. "I hear reconstruction is in full swing."

Ed looked at him. "That's all you heard?"

The man laughed. "In my line of work, you have to keep an ear out for rumors."

With that, he walked off toward town. Ed watched him go.

"That was fast."

Miles said, "Indeed."

Central's analysis hadn't even come back. No one knew how much was there. They didn't even know whether it was a mine. And yet someone who could smell money had already arrived.

* * *

That night, at Scar's house, Ed opened his notebook. The first pages held the questions he'd written over the past few days.

*What is Ishvala?*

*The Truth.*

Beneath it.

*Then what about the price?*

*Why was no one deprived of anything?*

And on a new page.

*Why did only Amestrian alchemists see the Truth?*

Ed looked at those lines for a long time. When he first came to Ishval, what he'd been curious about was the past. Scar's brother. Ancient Ishval. Alkahestry. Ishvala. But the moment a stone came out of the ground, the present had suddenly barged in.

Ed wrote a new line.

*What are we going to do with this stone?*

He thought for a moment. He remembered the voices he'd heard that day. The one who said they wouldn't have to leave if a mine opened. The one who said they'd be robbed of their land again. Adin, saying they should at least survey it. The people saying not to touch the cemetery. Central. The military. The merchant. And Miles. Scar. Himself.

Ed picked up the pen again.

*Who.*

He stared at the two words for a long time. Then he drew a line through the previous sentence. And wrote it again.

*Who decides what to do with this stone?*

Scar asked from across the table, "Is there an answer?"

Ed looked down at his notebook. "No."

"I see."

Ed looked up. "But it's weird."

"What is?"

"Everyone's saying good things."

Jobs. Schools. Roads. Reconstruction. Economic self-sufficiency. Tradition. The cemetery. Faith. Never being robbed again. Nobody had said anything about doing something bad. Ed looked at his notebook again. That was what made it harder.

He turned a page.

*Red stone = connected to alchemy?*

At first, he'd believed that. It had been set in the prayer hall, at every point where the lines met. But now all he heard was talk of mines and money.

Ed flicked the question mark with the tip of his pen.

"...Is it even connected at all?"
