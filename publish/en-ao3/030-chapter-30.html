Lansi, Xing.

“Disarm?” Luhan asked.

Ling pushed the report across the table. “More precisely, I’m asking you to disband the watch.”

“Isn’t that the same thing?”

“Close.”

“Then why do you put it differently?”

“Because it’s not exactly the same.”

Luhan looked down at the report.

The northwest of Lansi. In the district where Bai merchant houses and homes were clustered, young men had started standing guard on their own. At first there had been five. Last night there were more than twenty. They had even brought out old spears and swords kept in the warehouses. They hadn’t attacked anyone yet.

The problem was the other side. Similar movements were showing up among the An too.

Two days earlier, Ling had asked the An merchants himself and changed how the guards were posted. Not the same number on each side, but guards standing where the merchants wanted them, as many as they wanted.

Even so, both sides had brought out their spears.

“If one side picks up weapons, the other side picks them up too,” Ling said.

“The Bai were attacked.”

“The An say the same thing.”

“Because of Zhenha, all the Bai are under suspicion.”

“I know.”

Zhenha lowered his head without a word.

“But if you make them put down their weapons, who protects them?”

Ling answered at once. “I do.”

Luhan’s eyes came up.

Ling pointed at the map. “I’m going to increase the garrison. In the Bai district and the An district both.”

“You’re telling us to trust the imperial troops.”

“Yeah.”

It was far too simple an answer. Luhan closed his mouth.

Zhenha, beside him, spoke. “Your Majesty. If you tell the Bai to lay down their weapons now, there will be strong resistance.”

“That’s why I don’t want to give the order myself.”

“Then?”

Ling looked at Luhan. “I’d like you to talk to them.”

Luhan’s expression stiffened. “Me?”

“It’s different for the Bai to hear it from me and to hear it from you.”

“Couldn’t Your Majesty just order it?”

Ling looked at Luhan for a moment. “I could.” Then he said, “But I don’t want to.”

Luhan said nothing.

Ling shrugged. “That’s what I keep getting yelled at for these days.”

Ed, by the door, said, “You’ve improved.”

“Shut up.”

Luhan looked back and forth between them. “And if I can’t persuade them?”

Ling’s expression grew a little more serious. “Then I’ll have to think again.”

“Without ordering it?”

“Including that.”

Ling didn’t hide it. If he had to, he would give the order. But he wasn’t going to do it from the start.

Luhan looked at the report again. Bai names were written on it. Some were families he knew. Some were people he had known since he was a child.

He spoke slowly. “I’ll go.”

“No.”

It was the answer he’d expected.

In the warehouse where the Bai had gathered, swords and spears leaned against one wall. The middle-aged man standing in front of Luhan said, “You want us to believe the Emperor will protect us?”

“The garrison has already been posted.”

“Today.”

Luhan stopped.

“And tomorrow?” the man said.

It was a familiar question. The next emperor? The next order? Next time?

“Right now, Ling Yao is the Emperor,” Luhan said.

“So?”

“His Majesty did not punish all the Bai for what Zhenha did.”

“That’s only natural.”

“Has there always been an emperor who does what’s natural?”

The man closed his mouth.

Luhan went on. “He did not punish all the An for what Kairun did either. He posted the same number of guards on both sides.”

“And if we’re attacked anyway?”

“The garrison will stop it.”

“And if it can’t?”

Luhan couldn’t answer right away.

Someone behind him said, “Holding them ourselves is more certain.”

A few people nodded.

Luhan looked at the weapons standing against the wall. He understood why they had brought them out. Because they were afraid. Because they wanted to protect their families and their warehouses. Because it was unfair that they were being attacked for something Zhenha had done.

Luhan was Bai too. He couldn’t help understanding that feeling. That was what made it harder.

“If you hold those…”

People looked at Luhan.

“…the An will pick up weapons too.”

“They may already have.”

“Then we pick up more,” Luhan said. “And they’ll pick up more again.”

The warehouse went quiet.

“Someone has to put them down first.”

The middle-aged man asked, “Why does it have to be us?”

Luhan didn’t answer for a while. Then he said, “Because we can.”

Even as he said it, he wasn’t sure it was a good answer. But at least for now, he believed it.

“His Majesty said he would protect us.”

“Do you trust the Emperor?”

Luhan thought of Ling. The order that had put the same number of soldiers on both sides. The words don’t investigate all the Bai. And Ling Yao, whom he had known for a long time. A man who had become emperor for the Yao. A man who, even after becoming emperor, tried not to look only at the Yao.

“Yes,” Luhan said. “I trust him.”

The middle-aged man looked at Luhan for a long time. “Can you take responsibility for those words?”

“…”

“If someone dies tonight, the one who said those words is you.”

The warehouse went quiet.

Luhan looked at one of the spears leaning against the wall. “Yes. I’ll take responsibility.”

Just then, the door opened and a young man ran in. “They say the An have opened their warehouse too!”

Every eye in the room went to Luhan.

“Has that been confirmed?”

“…No. People are just saying—”

“Then it’s a rumor,” Luhan said. “Until it’s confirmed, we lay them down.”

Putting the swords away took less time than expected. When one person laid his down, a second followed.

The weapons gathered in the warehouse went back into crates. They weren’t destroyed. They were sealed. One key went to the Bai representative. The other went to an imperial official. Neither side could open it on its own. It was the method Luhan had proposed.

Ling heard the report and nodded. “Good.”

“Do the same on the An side.”

“I’ve already sent people.”

Luhan looked at Ling for a moment. “Your Majesty.”

“Hm?”

“You have to keep your promise.”

The smile left Ling’s face. “I know.”

“I told the people to trust Your Majesty.”

“Yeah.”

“That’s why they laid down their weapons.”

Ling nodded. This time there was no joking. “I’ll keep it.”

Only then did Luhan turn away. His eyes met Ed’s at the door.

“What?”

“What do you mean, what?”

“Why are you looking at me like that?”

“No reason.”

“If you have something to say, say it.”

Ed folded his arms. “You did good.”

Luhan blinked. “…Did you eat something bad?”

“I compliment you and you freak out.”

“Because it’s so sudden.”

“Want me to take it back?”

“No thanks.”

Luhan walked straight out.

Ed watched him go, then said to Ling, “That guy trusts you a lot.”

Ling smiled. “I know.”

“Don’t look so happy about it.”

“Why?”

“Because it’s heavy.”

Ling’s smile faded a little.

It was quiet until evening. The trouble came at night.

Ed was reading in his lodgings. There were three knocks on the door.

“Ed!” It was Ling’s voice.

Ed opened the door. “What?”

“Come out.”

His face was different. Ed followed without asking.

It was the Bai district Luhan had gone to that afternoon. The garrison was already blocking the street.

“Another fight?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

Ling didn’t answer.

People had gathered at the end of an alley. When Ed came closer, they made way.

A person was lying on the ground, covered with a cloth. Luhan was standing beside it. He wasn’t moving.

Ed’s steps slowed. “Who is it?”

“A Bai merchant,” Ling said quietly.

“Why?”

“He was attacked on his way home.”

“By who?”

“We don’t know yet.”

Ed looked around. A broken wooden crate. Scattered goods. Signs of a short struggle at the foot of the wall.

“Where was the garrison?”

“There was a disturbance in another alley.”

“On purpose?”

“I don’t know.” Ling’s answer was quick.

What hadn’t been confirmed, he left unconfirmed.

Ed looked at Luhan again. Luhan was looking only at the person under the cloth. Zhenha stood beside him.

“Did you know him?” Ed asked.

After a long time, Luhan answered. “I met him today.”

Ed stopped. “In the warehouse?”

“Yes.”

Only then did he understand. It was one of the people Luhan had persuaded himself.

“He was against it to the very end.” Luhan’s voice was strangely calm. “He said the sword in his own hand was more certain.”

No one spoke.

“I told him to lay it down.”

Ling opened his mouth. “Luhan.”

“That Your Majesty would protect him.”

“Luhan.”

“I said that.”

Ling took him by the shoulder. “You didn’t kill him.”

Luhan slowly looked at Ling. “Then who did?”

“That’s what we’re trying to find out.”

“That’s not what I mean.”

Ling stopped.

Luhan looked at the body again. “If he’d had his sword, would he have lived?”

“I don’t know,” Ed said.

Luhan turned to him. “He could have fought, at least.”

“Maybe.”

“He could have run.”

“Maybe.”

“But I told him to lay it down.”

Ed looked at Luhan. “If he’d had a sword, maybe someone else would’ve died instead.”

Luhan’s expression hardened.

“That’s possible too,” Ed said.

Luhan said nothing.

“Don’t decide that something nobody knows yet is your fault.”

Luhan didn’t answer. It was true. That was exactly why it was no comfort.

Just then a soldier ran over. “Your Majesty.”

“Report.”

“People are gathering again in the An district.”

Ling’s face hardened. “The news has already gotten there?”

“There’s a rumor going around that the Bai will retaliate.”

Ed clicked his tongue. They didn’t even know who had killed him yet. But the next story was already being made.

Ling spoke at once. “Double the guards in both districts.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

“Tell the Bai side too. No one moves until it’s confirmed.”

“As an order?”

Ling stopped for a moment. He looked at the person lying on the ground. “As an order, this time.”

“Yes, Your Majesty!”

The soldier ran off. Luhan watched him go without moving.

That night, people gathered again. In front of the warehouse that had been sealed that afternoon.

“Open it,” a Bai man said.

The imperial official shook his head. “It is His Majesty’s order.”

“Someone’s dead.”

“I know.”

“That’s why we’re asking you to open it.”

“I can’t.”

Voices rose among the crowd.

Luhan watched the scene from a little way off. The eyes that had looked at him that afternoon were different now. Then, they had seen the heir of the Bai. Now, they were looking at the man who had persuaded them to lay down their weapons.

A woman called out. “Lord Luhan.”

Luhan looked at her.

“Do we really have to wait?”

No answer came.

“You said the imperial court would protect us.”

“…Yes.”

“But someone died.”

Luhan’s hand slowly curled into a fist. “His Majesty is looking for the killer.”

“And if someone else dies before then?”

It was the question he had heard that afternoon. And if it can’t? He hadn’t been able to answer then. He couldn’t now either.

Zhenha came to Luhan’s side. “Let’s go.”

Luhan didn’t move. “I told those people.”

“I know.”

“To trust Ling Yao. I even said I’d take responsibility.”

Zhenha said nothing.

“Was I wrong?”

“We don’t know yet.”

Luhan smiled bitterly. “Everyone keeps saying they don’t know.”

“One can’t claim to know what one doesn’t.”

“And in the meantime, people die.”

Zhenha’s eyes turned to Luhan.

Luhan looked at the warehouse. The sealed weapons. The imperial official. The frightened people. The man who had been under the cloth a little while ago.

“If I were the emperor…”

The words came out first. Zhenha’s expression changed, ever so slightly.

Luhan heard his own words too. For a while, no one said anything.

“…No.” Luhan shook his head. “What does that matter.”

“Lord Luhan,” Zhenha added quietly. “I’ll pretend I didn’t hear that. Right now is the time to hold on.”

“His Majesty didn’t do anything wrong.”

He meant that. Ling had deployed the troops. He had protected both sides equally. He hadn’t punished anyone collectively. Even now, he was moving to prevent further clashes. He was a good emperor. That was what made it so strange.

“Ling Yao is doing well,” Luhan said. “And still, someone died.”

Zhenha didn’t answer.

Luhan opened his hand. The marks of his nails were left in his palm.

“Then what more is there to do?”

Once again, there was no answer.

As dawn neared, Ed was sitting on the steps of the government office. Ling plopped down beside him.

“I’m tired.”

“Isn’t an emperor not supposed to say that?”

“Emperors get tired too.”

“I thought if you ate a ton you didn’t get tired.”

“What does eating have to do with it?”

A laugh slipped out. It faded quickly.

“No killer yet,” Ling said.

“Yeah.”

“No proof it was the An.”

“Yeah.”

“No connection to Zhenha’s side either.”

“I know that too.”

“It could’ve been a robbery.”

“It could’ve.”

Ling rested his arms on his knees. “But nobody’s going to believe that.”

Ed didn’t answer.

“If I add more guards, the Bai will ask why I didn’t do it sooner.”

“Yeah.”

“And the An will think I added troops to catch them.”

“Maybe.”

“And if I do nothing?”

“They’ll say the Emperor abandoned the Bai.”

Ling tipped his head back. “This is such a pain.”

Ed looked over at him. “That’s my line.”

“I’m the Emperor, so I can have it.”

“Don’t use your power for stuff like that.”

Ling snorted. Then he asked, “Did you see Luhan?”

“I saw him.”

“What am I supposed to say to him?”

“I don’t know.”

“Again?”

“This time I really don’t know.”

Ling was silent for a moment.

Ed said, “You said you’d protect them.”

“Yeah.”

“And now there’s someone you couldn’t protect.”

Ling’s face stiffened.

“That’s not going to go away.”

“I know.”

“But that doesn’t mean everything you did was wrong either.”

Ling looked at Ed. Ed looked down the steps. “Or what Luhan did.”

Ling didn’t say anything for a long while. “Then what am I supposed to do?”

Ed sighed. “Why do you keep asking me?”

“Because you’ve always got a lot to say.”

“You want to die?”

Ling laughed. Only for a moment.

Even when the sun came up, the seal on the warehouse hadn’t been broken. The Bai didn’t go home. Neither did Luhan.

He sat against the wall and watched the day break.

There were words he had heard since he was a child.

Protect the Bai.

Raise the name of the Bai.

A Bai must become emperor.

He used to hate those words. Then Ling became emperor. And Luhan thought: That’s enough. As long as there’s a good emperor, it’s enough.

And Ling really was a good emperor.

But a man who had trusted his word and laid down his sword had died.

Luhan closed his eyes.

If I were the emperor.

The same thought came back. This time it stayed a little longer than before.

Luhan opened his eyes. “…No.”

Not yet.

But that thought,

unlike the first time, did not fade away so easily.
