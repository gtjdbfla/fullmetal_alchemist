From the morning on, Edward studied the ground and the grooves of the channels. When he put his palm to the ground, nothing happened. He had lost alchemy, but he could still read rock, slope, moisture, and cracks.

“Are you looking at the Dragon’s Pulse?” Scar asked.

“Karsa talks about flow, the same way Xingese alkahestry does. That doesn’t mean they’re the same. I’m checking.”

“How?”

“…I’m checking that too.”

Sahara was waiting for them.

“Are there more records of people from the east?”

“I was going to show you those today.”

They went into the record room.

What Sahara took out was even older than the ledgers they had been reading last night. It wasn’t even paper. Thin wooden tablets. Leather. Hand copies of broken stone tablets. Many of the records had no clear date.

Ed opened them one by one. A healer from the east. Someone who had traded medicinal herbs. A craftsman who had taught them how to repair wells. Someone who had written down how to set bones.

“Here.” Ed pointed to a line. “’Examines the energy of the earth.’”

Sahara leaned in to look. “So it does.”

“This person came from the east, right?”

“That’s what it says.”

Ed opened the next record. There was more. A record of storing medicinal herbs by telling apart cold ground from warm. A record of examining the terrain and the state of the plants before searching for water. A record of using the energy that flows within the human body to heal.

Ed’s hands moved faster. “This is it.”

“What is?” Scar asked.

“Knowledge from before it became alkahestry.”

Sahara raised an eyebrow. “Before?”

“No, more precisely…” Ed corrected himself. “Knowledge that might connect to the alkahestry they use in Xing now.”

He set yesterday’s record of the traveler from the west beside it. Several hundred years ago. A man from the west. He had studied Karsa’s water and stone. He had asked how far the movement of the earth extended. And in the east, people who talked about the energy of the earth and the energy of the human body had been coming and going since even before that.

Ed moved his finger across the records on the table as if connecting them. “Wait.”

Karsa. The traveler from the west. The east. Xing.

“If this person learned the flow here…”

Scar looked at Ed. “And?”

“…and went east…” Ed’s words came faster. “…and met the knowledge about medicine and energy that was already there—”

“It became alkahestry?” Scar asked.

Ed closed his mouth. A few seconds passed. “…It’s possible, isn’t it?”

Sahara said nothing.

Ed looked at the records again. “The timing could roughly fit too. There’s a legend in Xing about the Sage of the West appearing. If that person brought Western alchemical knowledge—” He pointed at the Karsa record. “—learned the flow of nature here—” The eastern record. “—and it merged with the medicine that already existed…” His finger stopped.

“Alkahestry.”

It was neat. Too neat.

“Are you certain?” Scar asked.

“No.”

“And yet you look excited.”

“What’s wrong with making a hypothesis?!”

Sahara laughed. “Then we’ll have to check whether it’s wrong.”

“Older records,” Ed said at once.

“I thought you’d say that.”

From the very bottom shelf, Sahara took out a small box. Inside were copies of records rather than records themselves. The originals were long gone, and later generations had transcribed what they said.

Ed opened one. He read it. He read it again. His expression changed.

“…Huh?”

Scar came over beside him. “What?”

Ed checked the record’s date. He opened another. Then another. “This.”

“These are records from before the traveler from the west came,” Sahara said.

“How long before?”

“I told you, it isn’t exact.”

“Still.”

“A long time.”

Ed read through it. They had repaired channels. Stopped cliffs from collapsing. Changed the waterways with the seasons. Made roads in the walls and floors. It wasn’t very different from what he could see in Karsa now.

Ed was silent for a moment.

“It seems it does not fit your hypothesis,” Scar said.

“…Yeah.”

“That was fast.”

“What was?”

“Being wrong.”

“Shut up!”

Ed scratched his head.

Karsa’s method had existed before the traveler from the west arrived. He couldn’t have created it. Then what about the possibility that he learned it in Karsa and took it east?

Ed opened the eastern records again. And there he got stuck once more. People from the east had been talking about the energy of the earth since before the traveler from the west appeared in Karsa. Reading the flow of energy. Examining the land. Finding where the water was.

“…It was here too.”

“What was?” Scar asked.

“Already.” Ed leaned back in his chair. “The idea of flow already existed in the east too.”

For a while, no one spoke.

Ed stared at the ceiling. The line he had just drawn in his head broke. A single line starting in Karsa, passing through the traveler from the west, and leading to Xing. It wasn’t that.

“I was doing it again.”

“Doing what?” Scar asked.

“Looking for the starting point.” Ed sat up. “Starting in Karsa, going to that guy, and from him going east.” He picked up a record. “But this was in Karsa before that guy came.” Another record. “And there were already similar ideas in the east.”

“Then they are unrelated?” Scar said.

“That’s not it either,” Ed answered immediately. “People came and went.”

He looked at the records scattered across the table. The west. The east. Ishval. Karsa. Someone had brought medicine. Someone had learned how to handle water. Someone had recorded the terrain. Someone had left their own knowledge behind.

“They might not have the same origin,” Ed said. “But that doesn’t mean they didn’t influence each other.”

“Then what will you look for?” Sahara asked.

Ed thought for a moment. “Not who made it first.” He picked up a record. “What changed when they met.”

Sahara smiled faintly. “That’ll be harder.”

Ed smiled too. “I know.”

After a moment, his eyes went back to the window. He could see the water in the channel.

There was still one thing left. Equivalent Exchange.

“Sahara.”

“What?”

“Can I see that stone from the first day again?”

Sahara looked at Ed for a long moment. “You still haven’t given up.”

“How could I give that up?”

They went back to the clearing. The stone pillar from the first day still stood there.

This time Ed didn’t look only at the pillar. He looked at the whole clearing. The height. The direction of the cliffs. The channels. The ground. Stone in the sunlight and stone in the shade. Cracks in the walls. The direction the wind came from. And the lines people had made.

“Do it once.”

Nadir stood in the same place as on the first day. Water flowed. The pillar moved. Ed didn’t look at the pillar. He looked at everything around it.

“Again.”

A second time.

“Again.”

A third.

“What are you calculating this time?” Scar asked.

“Everything.”

“Everything?”

“That first time, I only looked at the pillar.” Ed pointed at the channel. “Then the water.” The cliff. “Height.” The ground. “The earth.” The wind passed. “Wind.” The rock face catching the sunlight. “Heat.”

Scar looked at Ed. “Can you calculate all of that?”

“No.”

“…”

“That’s the problem.”

Ed crouched and put his hand on the ground. “In alchemy, you look inside the transmutation circle.”

Materials. Structure. Deconstruction. Reconstruction. To gain something, you needed something to match it. That wasn’t wrong. Ed believed that.

No. He knew firsthand how cruel that law was. He and Al had learned it with their bodies.

But Karsa had no transmutation circle. At least not in any form he knew. There was no boundary either.

“…The boundary,” Ed muttered.

It was the question he had asked on his first day in Karsa. Where’s the boundary?

Ed raised his head. He could see all of Karsa. The channel ran on beyond the clearing. That water came from the northern spring and the groundwater. That water came from the rain. The rain—

Ed stopped that thought. This time he looked at the wind. The wind that came in between the cliffs passed through the clearing and out into another valley. The pillar stood on the ground. There was already weight resting on the ground. Heat was moving. Water was moving. Air was moving.

Nothing was standing still.

Ed slowly stood up. “…Oh.”

“Have you worked it out?” Scar asked.

Ed didn’t answer. He went to the pillar and put his hand on it. Then he looked at Sahara.

“It’s not that there’s no price.”

Sahara stayed still. “Then?”

“I just wasn’t seeing it.” Ed pointed at the ground. “I only looked here.” The pillar. The basin. “Just this.” His hand swept wider. The channels. The cliffs. The village. “But you never finished inside this to begin with.”

“What did we do, then?” Sahara asked.

Ed was quiet for a moment. He still didn’t know the exact principle. He didn’t know what force was carried, or how. He couldn’t tell whether the people of Karsa understood every phenomenon scientifically either.

But he knew one thing.

“You used what was already flowing.”

Sahara’s eyes narrowed slightly.

“You didn’t make water. You made it flow. You didn’t suddenly create a force that wasn’t in the stone.” Ed looked around. “You sent movement that already existed in a different direction.”

“Is that Equivalent Exchange?” Scar said.

Ed thought for a moment. “I don’t know.” Then he added at once, “No. More precisely, I can’t calculate it yet the way I know how.”

Scar nodded.

Ed grinned. “But at least it isn’t free.”

“Why do you think so?” Sahara asked.

“Because you can’t do it in a place where there’s nothing,” Ed answered.

For the first time, Sahara laughed out loud. “So you’re starting to see it.”

Ed narrowed his eyes. “You knew, didn’t you?”

“Knew what?”

“What I was looking at wrong.”

“If I’d told you, would you have believed me?”

Ed opened his mouth. Closed it. “…No.”

“I thought so.”

“So did I,” Scar said.

“You stay out of this!”

Ed looked out over Karsa again.

The flow of nature. Becoming a part of it.

Yesterday, he had thought that was far too vague. Even now, he didn’t think it was an exact explanation. But he felt he understood a little of why they said it that way.

An alchemist puts the world in front of himself and tries to change it. The people of Karsa think they are inside that world too. So they ask first. What is already moving? Where is it going? And where should a road be made?

Ed’s eyes sharpened again. “…Then alkahestry.”

“Here we go again?” Scar said.

“Think about it.” Ed pointed at the ground. “In Xing, out of all this, they look at the earth.”

“The Dragon’s Pulse.”

“Yeah. Karsa looks at the water, the wind, and the terrain.” Ed tapped the ground with his finger. “Alkahestry might be what you get when you dig much deeper into just the flow of the earth.”

“Did someone create that alone too?” Sahara asked.

Ed stopped. Then he snorted. “Not falling for it.”

“Too bad.”

“I was just wrong a minute ago.” Ed looked out at the village. “Being similar doesn’t mean having the same origin.”

Karsa. Xing. The land between them. People coming and going for hundreds of years.

“Maybe people in a lot of places were looking at the same thing from the start.”

“Nature is everywhere,” Sahara said. “And where there’s a road, people move.”

Ed nodded. When people move, knowledge moves. And then things that were different at first meet each other and change. Karsa’s method. The medicine of the east. Alkahestry. And—

The traveler from the west who had passed through here hundreds of years ago.

Ed’s expression changed. “That guy.”

“The traveler from the west?” Scar asked.

“Yeah.” Ed looked toward the record room. “He saw this in Karsa.”

“I suppose so.”

“He asked how far the movement of the earth extends too.”

“That is what the record says.”

“And if he went east…” Ed paused. This time he didn’t build the conclusion first. “We shouldn’t only look at what he taught.”

Scar looked at Ed. “Then?”

“We have to look at what he learned too.”

Sahara smiled without a word.

Ed headed back toward the record room.

“Where are you going?” Scar asked from behind him.

“To find it.”

“Find what?”

Ed didn’t look back. “Where he went.” A moment later, he added, “And where he came from.”

That evening, Ed found a new record.

It was short. It was so old that parts of it couldn’t be read. All that remained was the direction in which the traveler from the west had left Karsa.

East.

Below it was a sentence that seemed to have been added by someone later.

He did not claim to know everything of this place.

He took what he had learned and headed east.

Ed looked at those sentences for a long time.

The name Hohenheim didn’t come to mind yet. But it bothered him in a strange way. Several hundred years ago. A man who came from the west, learned in Karsa, and went on east.

Ed didn’t close the record. His notebook lay open beside it. In it was a sentence he had copied from the wall of the prayer hall in Ishval.

The one who stood before the door saw the world.

Ed’s eyes stopped on that line. It was the same sentence as when he had first read it. But now it seemed as if it might read a little differently.

Ed set that thought aside. It wasn’t the story to grab hold of now.

Scar sat beside him, silently reading the records. They were both looking east. But they were searching for different people.

Ed opened the record again. This time it wasn’t to find the starting point. It was to see what had changed as that man passed through.
