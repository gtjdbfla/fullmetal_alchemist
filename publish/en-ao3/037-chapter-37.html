Ishval, Amestris.

Graus didn’t run. When Mustang entered the room, he was still standing behind his desk.

A map of Ishval lay open on it. Red marks. Checkpoints. Sites of clashes. Routes of troop movements.

Mustang looked down at the map.

“Second Lieutenant Bark is dead.”

“I heard.”

“He was aiming at Scar.”

Graus’s expression didn’t change. “I did not order that.”

“I know.”

“The battalion at the southern terminus?”

Graus’s eyes moved.

“It was a joint exercise.”

“The approval line for the exercise was blank, and the telegram that turned it back carried your name.”

“…”

“I’ve never seen an exercise stop on a single sheet of paper.”

Mustang pulled up a chair and sat. “That’s why I’m asking.”

“Asking what?”

“How much of it was on your orders?”

Graus started to speak. Mustang raised a hand.

“No.”

“…”

“Not here.”

Mustang stood. “Say it somewhere it’ll be on the record.”

“…”

“That’ll be better for you too.”

The military police waiting outside came in. Graus didn’t resist. Handcuffs closed around his wrists.

Just before he left the room, Mustang asked, “One thing I’ll ask now.”

Graus looked back.

“Do you think it’s over?”

For a moment, Graus couldn’t answer. Outside, it was quiet.

“…Yes.”

“I think so too,” Mustang said. “So now let’s look at what’s left after the war.”

Ishval remained quiet after Graus was detained. A day passed without gunfire. The following morning brought no new deaths.

At checkpoints where dismantling had stopped, people began removing the barriers again. Residents reopened their shuttered shops. People who had stayed in one district for days returned home.

There was no declaration that the war was over. There were simply fewer people acting as though they were at war.

Bark’s body and gun were handed over to the military investigators.

Hawkeye repeated the same statements. She had confirmed that Bark was targeting Scar. She had confirmed that he was Graus’s subordinate. Beyond that, she didn’t know.

A notebook turned up in Bark’s quarters. The military investigators showed it to Hawkeye first.

It was only a few tables. Target. Effect. Alertness inside the army. Distrust among residents. Response time of the local command.

At the top of the first page, in dry handwriting: Civilians — avoid if possible. Interpretation: mission effect takes priority.

On the last page there was one name.

Scar. High influence. Maximum effect if removed.

Hawkeye looked at that line for a long time.

I would have written it this way too.

That thought stayed with her the longest.

Miles brought back the reports of the first three incidents.

“Comparing the gun and the bullets may tell us something.”

“Even if it does, we must not reverse the order,” Hawkeye said.

“What do you mean?”

“We must not take what we know now and fit every earlier incident to him.”

Miles looked down at the reports. “They never found the shooter in any of the three cases.”

“That is why we suspect him.”

“But are not certain.”

“Correct.”

Miles gave a tired smile. “I hear that a great deal lately.”

“What?”

“’I do not know.’”

Hawkeye thought for a moment. “Perhaps that is a good thing.”

On the afternoon of Graus’s second day in custody, Brandt arrived in Ishval. He had reportedly left Central as soon as he received word of the detention.

Even after being notified of his arrival, Mustang wasn’t welcoming.

“Why are you here?”

“Because I need to be.”

“You could have stayed in Central.”

“The man who sent Graus to Ishval ought not to remain in Central.”

Mustang’s expression hardened.

Brandt pulled up a chair. “I recommended Graus.”

“I know.”

“You were wary.”

“I didn’t go so far as to oppose it.”

“That would be more accurate,” Brandt said. “I took your concerns too lightly.”

Mustang said nothing.

“Graus was capable. He had served in Ishval and achieved results on several fronts afterward.”

Brandt looked at Mustang. “Above all, he was not one of your people.”

“Is that why you chose him?”

“It was one reason.”

Brandt didn’t hide it.

“I wanted to prevent the military from once again operating through one man’s personal connections.”

“My connections.”

“Yes.”

Mustang gave a humorless laugh. “Not an unreasonable concern.”

“Which makes it worse,” Brandt replied. “A valid concern does not guarantee a sound choice.”

“I heard from Miles,” Brandt added. “I knew he led a unit of his own. I did not know it still moved on his name.”

Mustang looked at him. “Would you not have sent him, had you known?”

“I do not know. That may be the worse part.”

Silence passed.

“Exactly what Graus did is still under investigation,” Mustang said.

“I am aware.”

“So don’t start talking as though you’re already guilty.”

“I am speaking of responsibility, not guilt,” Brandt said. “They are different.”

Mustang narrowed his eyes. Brandt looked out of the window. A checkpoint was being dismantled.

“I kept trying to hold your power in check.”

“I know.”

“Because I did not believe a good Fuhrer made a good system.”

“That idea still isn’t wrong.”

Brandt smiled faintly. “It feels strange to hear you say it.”

“Don’t take it as praise.”

“I have no intention of doing so.”

Brandt rose. “I will see Graus.”

Graus was in a temporary holding room. When Brandt entered, he stood.

“Councilman.”

“Sit down.”

Graus didn’t sit. Brandt remained standing too.

“Why did you do it?”

Graus was silent for a moment. “I will speak at the trial.”

“Very well.”

Brandt asked no more. Instead, it was Graus who asked a question.

“Do you regret it?”

“What?”

“Sending me here.”

Brandt thought for a moment. “Yes.”

Graus’s expression changed very slightly. “I see.”

“But I do not intend to stop there.”

“What do you mean?”

“If I conclude only that I chose the wrong man, next time I need only choose a better one,” Brandt said. “That is the easiest conclusion.”

“Is it wrong?”

“It is insufficient.”

Brandt headed for the door.

“Tell the court what you did.”

He stopped at the doorway. “I will examine why a man like you had the power to do it.”

By the next morning, news of Graus’s detention had spread throughout Ishval. The fact that Bark had served under him followed it.

Unconfirmed stories grew between those facts. Graus had killed the first three victims too. Every checkpoint shooting had been staged by the military. Graus had ordered the shelling in the south.

None of it had yet been confirmed. But to the people, it already sounded like one story.

People began gathering outside the temporary military offices that morning. At first, a little over ten. Then thirty. By noon, they were beyond counting.

“Tell us who killed those people!”

“Hand over Graus!”

Miles pulled the soldiers back toward the building.

“Barrels down.”

“Colonel, their numbers keep growing.”

“Show them.”

“Sir?”

“Show your hands and lower your guns.”

The soldiers obeyed.

Scar arrived outside the military offices too. A man caught hold of him.

“They say he’s in there.”

“He is.”

“Then why aren’t you doing anything?”

“What do you intend to do?”

The man shut his mouth.

“Drag him out.”

“And then?”

“Make him answer for his crimes!”

“Who will?”

The man couldn’t answer.

Scar said, “If an angry man decides guilt, it is no different from my past.”

The man’s grip weakened.

At the back of the crowd, Adin stood with his teeth clenched. His fists were tight, but he didn’t move forward. In his pocket was a worn ball he had picked up at the school lot.

Then the door opened. Brandt emerged. The murmuring swelled. Someone recognized him.

“A councilman from Central!”

“He’s the one who sent Graus!”

Miles blocked Brandt’s way.

“It is dangerous to go out now.”

“That is why I am going.”

“Councilman.”

Brandt pointed to the soldiers. “Did you tell them not to point their guns at the crowd?”

“Yes.”

“Then I ought not to hide behind them either.”

Miles frowned. “Those are not the same thing.”

“Let us make them the same thing for today.”

Brandt stepped forward. The voices rose.

“Why did you send Graus?!”

“You’re the ones who started it again!”

Brandt didn’t turn away.

“I was the one who sent Graus.”

The crowd quieted a little.

“I supported his appointment and judged that he could be entrusted with security in Ishval.”

Someone shouted, “Then you’re just the same!”

“I will not evade responsibility for that judgment.”

“Responsibility?”

A young man near the front laughed.

“Another trial, another investigation, and we’re supposed to wait again?”

He stepped forward. Miles raised a hand. The soldiers tensed.

“Stop.”

The man didn’t stop.

“My father died waiting.”

Scar moved toward him. “Stop.”

“Stay out of this.”

The man’s hand went inside his clothes. A soldier reflexively raised his gun.

Miles shouted, “Lower it!”

Too much happened at once. A small knife appeared in the man’s hand. The soldier’s finger stiffened. Scar rushed at the man.

And Brandt stepped between them.

One gunshot.

The man’s knife fell to the ground.

So did Brandt.

“Lower your weapons!”

Miles’s shout cut across the square. Frozen, the soldier let go of his gun. Scar twisted the young man’s arm and pinned him to the ground.

“Do not move.”

The man didn’t resist.

Zahir stepped in front of the crowd and spread his arms.

“Step back. No one wants anyone else hurt.”

The crowd retreated one step at a time. Adin ran toward the fallen Brandt, shouting, “Medic, over here!”

Blood spread through Brandt’s clothes.

Mustang rushed out of the building. “Medic!”

Hawkeye followed.

Brandt was breathing. For now.

Mustang knelt. “Why did you come out?”

Brandt barely opened his eyes.

“I think… I heard that question earlier.”

“Don’t talk.”

“Fuhrer.”

“The medic’s coming.”

Brandt caught Mustang’s arm. There was hardly any strength in his grip.

“That soldier…”

Mustang looked back. The soldier who had fired stood white-faced. He was the soldier who had put a hand on his rifle during the stake incident and been told by Graus, “Don’t reach for your gun first.” Miles remembered that face.

“He’ll be investigated.”

“Do not… let it end with him alone.”

Mustang looked back at Brandt. Brandt drew a labored breath.

“Graus, too.”

Another breath.

“And me.”

Mustang’s face hardened.

Brandt said, “Do not replace the person.”

His voice was almost inaudible.

“Change… the system.”

Mustang couldn’t speak.

The strength left Brandt’s hand. The medic arrived. But before long, the movement stopped.

The square was quiet.

No one shouted.

Night fell.

The soldier who had fired was detained. So was the man who had drawn the knife.

Miles rewrote the first line of his report again and again. He couldn’t put who had been wrong first into a single sentence.

The man drew a knife. The soldier raised his gun. Brandt stepped between them. The gun fired.

All facts. But depending on the order in which he placed them, they became entirely different stories.

In the end, Miles wrote:

Under investigation.

Hawkeye saw the words and said nothing.

Scar sat on the steps outside the military offices. Mustang stood beside him.

“I thought no one would die today.”

“The end of a war does not end death,” Scar said.

“I know.”

“You do not look as though you know.”

Mustang didn’t answer.

After a while, Scar asked, “What did he say at the end?”

Mustang stared out across the square for a long time.

“Not to replace the person.”

“…”

“To change the system.”

Scar lifted his head.

Mustang wasn’t smiling. “He left us such an easy thing to do.”

“He left it because it was difficult,” Scar said.

Mustang looked at him. Scar said no more.

In the distance, the last barrier of a checkpoint under demolition came down.

The war was ending.

But what it left behind would have to be dealt with from here on.
