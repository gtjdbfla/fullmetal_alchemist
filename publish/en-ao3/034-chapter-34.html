Ishval, Amestris.

When Mustang arrived in Ishval, the first thing he heard was gunfire. One shot. A moment later, two.

Hawkeye looked out the window.

“Quite the welcome,” Mustang said.

“I do not think it is a welcome.”

“I know.”

Before the car had even stopped, Miles approached. “Your Excellency.”

“The situation?”

That was the end of the greetings.

Miles spread out a map. “There was fighting at the eastern checkpoint. One soldier and two residents wounded. No fatalities.”

“Cause?”

“Unknown.”

Mustang looked at Miles. “Good.”

“What is?”

“Having a commander who says he doesn’t know when he doesn’t know.”

Miles didn’t smile. “Until a few days ago, that was enough.”

“And now?”

“People die while we do not know.”

Miles added, “And a military train is coming up the southern railway.”

“Whose troops?”

“They say it is a joint mobile exercise. We are confirming who approved it.”

Mustang’s eyes went to the southern end of the map. “Stop it at the terminus. Not a single step toward Ishval.”

“On whose orders?”

“Mine.”

“And Brigadier General Graus?” Hawkeye asked.

“He is at headquarters.”

Mustang got out of the car. “Let’s go see him.”

Graus was looking at a map on the wall. When Mustang came in, he saluted. “Your Excellency.”

The map was crowded with red marks. Markets. Checkpoints. Supply depots. Places where army posts and residential districts met.

Hawkeye scanned the map. “When was this drawn up?”

“Right after I was posted here.”

“Before the clashes started, then.”

“I marked the danger zones.”

Miles looked at the map. It was the map that had been there since Graus first came to Ishval. And a good number of the actual clash sites matched the marks.

“Your predictions were quite accurate,” Mustang said.

“I have been through many wars,” Graus answered evenly. “Where people collide tends to be much the same.”

Mustang looked at the map once more. “Troops?”

“There are only twelve investigation escorts, so I posted them only at key points, to help Colonel Miles’s troops.”

“Additional troops?”

“I have not requested any. If a large force came in, it might be taken as a suppression operation.”

“Agreed.”

“I hear an exercise train is coming up the southern railway.”

“I understand it is part of Central’s training plan.”

“Is that so.”

Graus’s eyes moved, very slightly.

Mustang pointed to one of the checkpoints. “Get rid of this one.”

“It is there to stop weapons coming in.”

“Right now more clashes are happening because of it.”

Miles nodded.

Mustang’s finger moved to the next point. “And separate the army supply route from the residents’ route.”

“A detour will lengthen supply times.”

“Cheaper than people dying.”

Graus asked, “And if the clashes continue anyway, what will you do?”

“Stop them.”

“Even by force?”

“If necessary.”

“Does that include opening fire?”

The room went quiet. Mustang looked at Graus. “Have them tell apart the people holding weapons from the people who aren’t.”

“There are situations where that is hard to tell.”

“That’s why there are commanders in the field.”

“If they misjudge, soldiers die.”

“Right,” Mustang said. “That’s why being a commander is hard.”

The first clash broke out in less than an hour. In the western square.

An army vehicle was surrounded by residents. Someone shouted that the army was carrying away bodies. There was nothing in the vehicle but food. But before anyone could confirm it, stones were thrown.

A soldier raised his gun.

“Lower it,” Miles said.

“But Colonel—”

“Lower your gun.”

On the other side, a young man was about to draw a blade. Someone caught his wrist. It was Scar.

“Let go!”

“If you draw that,” Scar said, “they will raise their guns.”

“The army went first—”

“Then you mean to be the second?”

The man’s hand stopped.

Scar looked at the soldiers. “You lower yours as well.”

Miles raised his hand. The muzzles went down.

Someone among the residents shouted, “Why do we have to step aside?!”

Scar turned around. “Stepping aside and surrendering are different.”

“Are you on the army’s side too?”

“No.” Scar’s voice was low. “But that does not make your anger the will of Ishval.”

Little by little, the road opened. The army vehicle passed through. No one died.

Graus was watching from the edge of the square.

That afternoon, the moment the first blast sounded, Mustang looked up. The second was closer.

“Mortar!” Miles shouted.

There was no telling who was firing it. Whether it had been taken from the army. Whether it had been hidden somewhere. There was no time to find out now.

Hawkeye scanned the sky. “Sir!”

A small dot came over the buildings. There were people beneath it. A woman running with a child in her arms. A man helping a wounded person along. An old man standing frozen in the middle of an alley, not knowing where to go.

Mustang’s right hand came up.

Miles saw that hand. So did Scar. A white glove. A transmutation circle on the back.

Scar’s eyes narrowed.

Snap.

The flames passed over the people. They rose into the sky. And in that instant—

The shell exploded in midair.

The roar shook the alley. People ducked. Fragments rained down onto the rooftops.

“One more!” It was Hawkeye’s voice.

Mustang was already turning. Snap. Flame flashed a little farther off. The second shell didn’t reach the buildings either.

“Miles!”

“Sir!”

“Get the people out first!”

“Everyone, move west!”

The soldiers moved. Scar went in among the residents too. “West! Do not stop!”

A third sound came. This time it was low. Close.

“Right!” Hawkeye shouted.

Mustang’s fingers moved. Snap.

An explosion. The shock shook his coat. A metal fragment flew out of the smoke and bounced across the road.

Mustang looked down at it once. Then he raised his head.

People were looking at him. Ishvalans. Some of them knew that hand. The sound of those fingers snapping. The flames that followed. They had heard the same sound long ago. Back then, the flames had not gone toward the sky.

At the end of the alley, an old Ishvalan man was looking at that hand.

“I know that flame,” the old man murmured.

Mustang’s right hand trembled, very slightly. Hawkeye saw it. She said nothing. Mustang said nothing either.

He raised his right hand again. “I’ll stop the next one too.”

Scar looked at Mustang. A moment later, he said, “Then stop it.”

When the shelling stopped, the soldiers started to move forward.

“Halt,” Mustang said.

“We’ll secure the firing position.”

“Don’t pursue.”

“But, sir—”

“If we push the army in now, the residents will move too.”

Miles changed the orders at once. “Locate the firing position only. Do not engage!”

“Sir!”

A man who had been throwing stones at soldiers a moment ago was helping a wounded soldier by the arm. Another soldier was running with an Ishvalan child in his arms. It was only for a moment.

Graus watched the scene from a distance.

The flames had flared three times. No one had burned. And yet the shelling had stopped. People had moved as the army directed them.

Graus’s gaze rested on Mustang’s right hand.

Evening.

Hawkeye spread out the death records of the first three people. An adult man. An adult woman. A child.

Miles sat down across from her.

“Were the first three incidents the same as the fighting now?” Hawkeye asked.

“No.”

“How are they different?”

Miles thought for a moment. “At first, almost the same thing happened over and over. It was strange.”

“In what way?”

“One person died. The person who fired was never seen. And right after, the army and the residents faced off.”

“All three times?”

“Yes.”

Hawkeye laid the three records side by side. “And after that?”

“A mess,” Miles said. “Fights where no one knows who started it. Mistaken fire. Cases where the army got caught in fights between residents. There were attacks aimed at soldiers too.”

“But the same pattern as at first?”

“Once the war grew bigger, it actually disappeared.”

Hawkeye’s hand stopped. One person. An unseen shooter. A clash right after. Three times. And then it ended.

“What if it did not disappear…”

Miles looked at her.

“…but stopped?”

A moment of silence passed.

Miles asked, “You mean someone started it on purpose?”

“I do not know.” Hawkeye answered at once. “Not yet.”

“Then what are you looking for?”

Hawkeye touched the child’s record. “When it stopped.” And she pulled over the report beside it. “Who knew what at that time.”

The next day there was another clash. This time in a narrow alley. Both sides were armed. If the army pushed in, it would likely turn into a firefight.

Mustang looked at Miles. “Who can talk to those people?”

“Scar would be better than me.”

A voice came from behind them. “Why should I obey your orders?”

Mustang turned around. It was Scar.

“It’s not an order.”

“Then what?”

“A request.”

Scar looked at Mustang for a moment. “What do you want?”

“Get the people out of the alley.”

“And then?”

“We separate the ones holding weapons and send the rest home.”

“Arrests?”

“I don’t think locking everyone up will end it either.”

Scar was silent for a moment. “I will get the people to come out.”

He turned away. A few steps later, he stopped. “Mustang.”

“What?”

“Do not mistake anything just because you saved people today.”

Mustang listened quietly.

“Ishval is still not yours.”

A moment later, Mustang said, “I know.”

Scar walked on.

It took two hours.

The old people and children came out first. Then the people who weren’t holding weapons. Last of all came the people with swords and guns. They were made to lay their weapons on the ground. The soldiers lowered their muzzles too.

Someone cursed. Someone cried. But no one fired.

That afternoon there was a clash in the east too. Miles stopped it. In the evening, people gathered in front of the army supply depot. Mustang went out himself.

Two checkpoints were gone. One supply route was changed. The army and the residents ran into each other less often.

The clashes continued. There were still wounded. But there were fewer shots. There was no large-scale army fire. There was no large-scale retaliation by residents. No front lines formed.

Little by little, the war was losing its shape.

Night.

Graus was looking at the map alone. The door opened. It was Bark.

“I have something to report.”

“Go on.”

“Clashes have decreased in both the west and the south.”

“I know.”

“Army fire has decreased as well.”

“And the residents?”

“The same.”

Graus didn’t take his eyes off the map.

“Scar’s influence is significant.”

“I know.”

“Colonel Miles is following the Fuhrer’s directions as well.”

“I know that too.”

Bark was silent for a moment. “It is faster than expected.”

Graus looked at him. “What is?”

“The speed of stabilization.”

Graus’s face hardened. “It is not over yet.”

“Yes, sir.”

“A quiet day or two does not mean the war is over.”

“That is so.”

Graus looked at the red marks on the map. Mustang had moved as predicted. He had separated people. He had waited. Wherever possible, he had not killed. And yet the rate at which people were dying was going down.

“Keep watching.”

“Sir.”

Bark turned.

“Bark.”

He stopped.

“The order regarding civilians stands.”

“I understand it clearly.”

The door closed.

Graus looked at the map again. Today, not a single new red mark had been added.

He took a copy of a second telegram out of the drawer. It was not for Ishval. It was to go to the southern railway terminus. One battalion, standing by for the second day under the name of a joint mobile exercise. The troops that would go into Ishval the day Mustang failed.

The signature line was blank.

Graus put the copy back in the drawer. Before he closed it, he looked at that blank space for a very long time.

Mustang was standing outside his quarters. Hawkeye came out holding a report.

“No fatalities today.”

“Good news.”

“It is only one day.”

“We cut it down one day at a time.”

Hawkeye stood beside him. A bell rang in the distance. It wasn’t an alarm. It was the bell that marked the night hours.

“What were you thinking during the shelling today?” Hawkeye asked.

Mustang looked at her. “Why all of a sudden?”

“I was just curious.”

Mustang looked down at his right hand for a moment. A white glove. A transmutation circle on the back.

“Before…”

That was as far as he got. Hawkeye waited.

Mustang lowered his hand. “Never mind.”

“Yes, sir.”

Hawkeye didn’t ask any further.

Mustang looked out at the Ishvalan night. The war wasn’t over yet.

But today, the flames had not killed a single person.

Beneath those flames,

people had gone home alive.
