A few days later.

One morning in Lansi, Furong said over a bowl of noodles, “Talk about a southern road is already going around among the An.”

“Who’s building a road?”

“You asked if it was possible.”

“I only asked!”

“And I only said it might be possible. After that, who knows.”

Ed’s face scrunched up.

Furong tapped the southern part of the map. “On a map, it’s possible. In reality, you’d need water, rest stops, and the An’s consent. One road won’t make Lansi disappear. But the work for warehouses, transport, and inns would shrink, little by little.”

The door burst open. Lien came in, out of breath. “Mr. Elric!”

“What?”

“His Majesty the Emperor is coming.”

Ed paused. “…Who?”

“His Majesty the Emperor.”

“Coming where?”

“To Lansi.”

Ed looked at Furong. Furong kept eating his noodles.

“Why?”

“He was already on a tour of the west. Lansi was one of the planned stops.” Lien caught her breath. “But they say he heard about the Redvein Stone and the trade road and extended his stay.”

Ed’s eyebrow twitched. “No way.”

There was a way.

Around noon, Lansi’s central market was far more crowded than usual. Ed had no intention of going to watch the Emperor’s procession. More precisely, he hadn’t planned to.

“That looks delicious.”

Not until he heard a familiar voice from somewhere nearby.

Ed stopped.

In front of a stall with fruit piled up like a mountain stood a young man holding two skewers, a coat of fine cloth thrown over ordinary traveling clothes. A guard in black stood beside him.

Ed’s mouth fell open. The young man spotted Ed too.

“…Ed?”

“…Ling?”

They both shouted at the same time. “What are you doing here?!”

People around them turned to look. The guard pressed a hand to her forehead.

Ling Yao laughed first. “Long time no see!”

“Don’t ‘long time no see’ me! Why is the Emperor eating skewers in a market?!”

“Because I’m hungry.”

“Eat at the palace!”

“The palace is in the capital.”

“Then eat at your lodgings!”

Ling ate the rest of the skewer in one bite. “An emperor has to eat a lot.”

“You always ate a lot!”

Ling’s eyes went wide. “So I was born to be emperor.”

“There’s no such thing!”

Laughter broke out around them.

The guard caught Ling’s sleeve. “Your Majesty.”

“What?”

“We must go.”

“Just one more of those.”

“No.”

“I’m the Emperor, though?”

“That is why the answer is no.”

“He hasn’t changed at all,” Ed muttered.

“Agreed,” the guard said.

Ed turned around. A familiar voice. A familiar blank face.

“Lan Fan?!”

An hour later, Ed was sitting in a reception room at the Lansi government office.

This time, Ling looked like an emperor. At least when there was no food in his mouth.

“Oh, right,” Ling said. “Al’s doing well.”

“Al?”

“Even before I left the capital, he and May were still looking into all kinds of things.”

“Yeah?” Ed’s answer was short.

“Yeah. He’s doing better than I expected.”

“…Is he eating properly?”

“Less than you.”

“That kid does fine wherever you throw him.”

“I don’t think you’re the one who should be saying that.”

“Shut up.”

Ed didn’t ask anything else. If it was Al, he’d be handling things fine on his own. Ling didn’t explain further either.

Instead, he leaned back in his chair. “So. Did you have a good trip?”

“More or less.”

“What did you find?”

Ed thought for a moment. “I found a record in Karsa.”

“Karsa?”

“A small village east of Ishval. The trade road used to pass through there, but now the road’s cut off.” Ed waved his hand as if it were a bother. “It’s a long story. The people there don’t like getting tangled up with the outside.”

“And?”

“Don’t bring that name up when you talk about roads.”

Ling looked at Ed for a moment. “…Got it.”

“…What record?”

“About four hundred years ago, a man from the west passed through there.”

Ling’s eyes widened slightly. “The Sage of the West?”

“Probably. …It might be Hohenheim.”

“That’s your dad.”

“I don’t know yet. You can’t be sure just because a few records match.”

Ling didn’t ask further. “So you followed his trail all the way here?”

“Yeah.” Ed spread a map on the table. “There was a record that he went east from that village. Then I ended up looking at the old roads.” His finger traced the current northern trade road. “These days you come into Lansi this way.” It moved down. “In the old days, you came through that village. So I just asked whether a road could run farther south without going through the village. That’s all.”

Ling looked down at the map. “But among the An, there’s talk that the imperial court is considering a new southern trade road.”

Ed froze. “…What?”

“Some merchants are even wondering which well will be used as a relay point.”

“I never said anything about wells!”

Ling stopped smiling. “I know.”

“How did it even turn into that?”

“There’s something more important than finding that out.” Ling’s finger touched the An region. “What the people who heard it have started doing.”

“What have they done?”

“Nothing much yet. They’re asking.”

“Asking what?”

“Land prices. Where the wells are. Where the road will go.”

Ed’s face hardened.

“So I wanted to see for myself,” Ling said.

“You said you were coming to Lansi anyway.”

“That’s right. I’ve been on the western tour for weeks. This was a planned stop.” Ling folded the map. “But now that I’m here, what I heard in reports on the road and what I’m hearing here are too different.”

“Different how?”

“The reports I got on the way said the An want a new trade road.”

“That’s wrong?”

“That’s what I’m here to find out,” Ling said. “Who are the An?”

Ed closed his mouth.

“If the chieftain wants it, does that mean the An want it?” Ling raised one finger. “What if the merchants want it?” Two. “What if the young people want it?” Three. “What if the people who own the land hate it?” Four.

Ling lowered his hand. “The report just said ‘the An.’”

Ed looked down at the map. Until a few days ago, a road had been a question for him. Could you go there? Could you make a new road where there wasn’t one? But once that question passed to other people, it was turning into something completely different.

A servant spoke from outside the door. “Your Majesty. The two of them have arrived.”

Ling stood up. “Want to listen in?”

“Why would I?”

“You’re the one who talked about roads.”

“I told you, I only asked if it was possible!”

“So listen from the side.”

Ed’s face scrunched up. “I’m starting to really hate that line.”

The first to come in was Xiaolin An. Twenty-eight. She carried medicinal herbs and leather from the An villages in the west to Lansi.

Behind her came Chenluo An. Thirty. He took part in the negotiations between several villages over pastureland and the right to use wells.

The two of them paid their respects to Ling.

“Sit comfortably,” Ling said. “I didn’t call you here to grant anything today.”

“Then what do you mean to ask?” Chenluo said.

“Whether a road would be a good thing.”

Xiaolin spoke first. “It would.”

“It wouldn’t,” Chenluo said immediately.

Ed looked from one to the other.

Ling snorted. “Good. That’s already more accurate than the reports I got on the tour.”

“The young people in our village keep leaving,” Xiaolin said.

“Where to?”

“To Lansi, or farther inland. Even if they stay, there’s not much work.” Xiaolin added, “We have medicinal herbs, and leather. But getting them up to the northern road costs too much to transport.”

“And if there were a southern road?”

“We could sell them. If people come, inns will open, and there’ll be work fixing carts.”

Chenluo cut in. “And then merchants will come.”

Xiaolin looked at him. “Is that a bad thing?”

“When merchants come, money comes,” Chenluo said. “When money comes, people who want to buy land come too.”

“Then we don’t sell.”

“For how long?”

Xiaolin closed her mouth.

Chenluo looked at Ling. “At first they’ll say it’s only a road. Then they’ll need a relay station. Then wells. Then warehouses. Then land.” He asked, “Will that land still be ours then?”

Ling didn’t answer right away.

“Then are you saying we should do nothing?” Xiaolin said.

“I never said that.”

“The young people are leaving.” Xiaolin’s voice rose. “Because there are no choices for making a life here.”

“So you want to hand the choice over to someone else?”

“I never said that either.”

Their voices began to overlap. Ling didn’t stop them. He listened for a while.

Then he asked, “What do the An people think?”

They both looked at Ling at the same time.

“As I said—” Xiaolin began.

“No.” Ling shook his head. “Not what Xiaolin thinks.” He looked at Chenluo. “Not what Chenluo thinks either.”

The room went quiet.

“Did you ask every village?”

Neither of them answered.

“Whether they need a road,” Ling said. “If one is built, how far it’s all right for it to go. Where it must not pass. Who should use the wells. Whether outsiders can buy land.”

“We haven’t properly asked yet,” Chenluo said slowly.

“Why not?”

Chenluo hesitated. “Because we thought in the end it was something Your Majesty would decide. If we sent up our opinions, the imperial court would judge.”

Ling’s expression stopped.

Ed looked at him too.

“…I see.” Ling’s finger tapped the table. Once. Twice. “I’m the Emperor, so I suppose I could decide.”

No one answered.

Ling looked at Chenluo. “But is being able to decide…” His finger stopped. “…the same as having to decide?”

Neither Chenluo nor Xiaolin could answer.

Ed looked at the line on the map. A moment ago, it had been a road. Now he could see the things beneath that line. Villages. Fields. Wells. And the people who lived there.

Night. Ling looked down at the lights of Lansi from the railing of his lodgings. Ed stood beside him.

“I thought once I became emperor, I’d know the answers.”

“You always said that if you just became emperor, you could protect the Yao.”

“Right. I thought I just had to make good decisions.”

“And now?”

“If those two had drawn swords today, I’d have stopped them right away. But roads? Wells?”

Ed rested his arms on the railing. “Don’t ask me. I’m not the emperor.”

“I’m telling you, I don’t know the answer either.”

Ling laughed, then looked back out at the city. Ed didn’t answer.

There was a knock at the door. Lan Fan came in. “Your Majesty.”

“Hm?”

“A visitor has arrived.”

“At this hour?”

“From the Bai.”

Ling’s expression changed slightly. “Who?”

“Lord Luhan Bai.”

It was a name Ed had never heard. “Who’s that?”

Ling was quiet for a moment. Then he smiled strangely. “Someone like me.”

“There’s another one like you?”

“Why do you have to put it like that?!”

“How is he like you?”

Ling’s smile faded a little. “He was raised to become emperor.”

Ed blinked.

“To protect his own clan,” Ling said.

“…Like you?”

“Yeah.”

A moment later the door opened, and a young man came in. About Ed’s age. Neatly dressed. Quiet eyes.

Luhan Bai stood before Ling and paid his respects. “Your Majesty.”

Ling waved a hand. “Long time no see, Luhan.”

“It’s the first time since the coronation.”

“I was kind of surprised you actually congratulated me back then.”

A faint smile came to Luhan’s lips. “I lost, but the Bai didn’t disappear.”

Ling laughed too. “You haven’t changed.”

Ed looked back and forth between them.

“Let me introduce you,” Ling said. “Edward Elric.”

Luhan’s gaze went to Ed. “The Fullmetal Alchemist.”

“Former.”

“My apologies.”

“…If you apologize that fast, it’s hard to say anything.”

Ling laughed.

“Don’t laugh.”

Luhan looked at Ling again. “I heard about the southern trade road.”

The smile that had been on Ling’s face a moment ago sank away. “It’s already reached the Bai.”

“There’s no reason a story that started in Lansi would reach the Bai late.”

“So that’s why you came?”

“Yes.” Luhan didn’t hesitate. “Because the Bai have things to protect too.”

Ling heard that and was silent for a moment.

Ed looked out the window. The lights of the caravans that had come in from the north were going out one by one.

Among the An, there were people who wanted a new road. There were people who feared that road. And there were people who made their living on the road that already existed. It was the same road. But for some it was an opportunity, and for others a danger.

Ling pointed to a chair. “Sit.”

Luhan took a seat. Ling sat across from him.

“This time,” Ling said, “I’ll listen first.”
