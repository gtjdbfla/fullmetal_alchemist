The next morning, the first thing Edward looked for was last night’s record.

“Sahara took it,” Scar said. “You were crumpling it in your sleep.”

Ed grumbled and went outside.

Karsa’s water passed through the channels into the fields, and whatever was left ran down to the cisterns below. The walls and paths that had looked separate yesterday now connected.

Sahara took the two of them to the northern spring. Water welled up from a crack in the rock.

“This is all of it?”

“There are also the cliffs where the rain seeps in, and the water under the ground. The movement changes with every season.”

Ed took a small cloth bundle out of his bag. It was a sample he had gotten from Miles before leaving Ishval.

“Things like this were set into the floor of the prayer hall in Ishval. At every point where the lines met. Do you use anything like this here?”

Sahara picked up the stone and held it to the light once. “It’s a stone.”

“That’s it?”

“We don’t use stones like that. If it’s from an Ishvalan prayer hall, an Ishvalan would know.”

She handed it back and turned toward the spring. Ed looked down at the stone in his palm for a moment. There was no reaction. His hypothesis that it was the price for the flow didn’t connect to anything here either.

He looked over at the channels. “I get that you gather the water and use it. But yesterday’s stone pillar still doesn’t add up.”

“Then look at another place.”

In front of a field wall that the wind had knocked down, a man pressed his fingers to a groove in the ground. As the wind passed through the gaps in the wall and water flowed in from the channel, a few stones moved slowly. The man lifted the last stone into place with his own hands.

“That’s it?”

“That’s it.”

“Yesterday you moved a whole pillar.”

“Yesterday it was a place where that could be done.”

Ed’s eyes narrowed. Conditions were different from place to place, then.

Next was a narrow path cut halfway up a cliff.

Sahara stopped. “Yesterday you asked for a name.”

“Yeah.”

“And I said there wasn’t one.”

“I still think that makes no sense.”

“Why do you need a name?”

“To tell things apart.”

“From what?”

“From other methods. Alchemy, alkahestry.”

Sahara nodded. “I suppose your people would need one.”

“And yours?”

“We never needed one.”

“Then how do you even explain it?” Ed said, exasperated.

Sahara thought for a moment. “Explain it.” She looked down at her feet. “Nature moves.”

“I know that.”

“No.” She looked at Ed. “I think you only think you know it.”

Ed’s eyebrows shot up. “What?”

Scar took a step back.

Ed turned around. “Why are you backing away?”

“Do not mind me.”

Sahara went on calmly. “Water flows.”

“Right.”

“The wind moves.”

“Right.”

“The earth moves.”

“I know that too.”

“Heat moves.”

“I know!”

“Then what about people?”

Ed stopped. “What?”

“Do people stand outside all that?” Sahara opened her hands. “We use the water. We walk on the earth. The wind hits us. We give off heat. We live inside it.” She pointed a finger at Ed’s chest. “So why do you think you alone are standing outside?”

Ed didn’t answer.

Sahara pointed down the cliff. Karsa lay below them. Channels. Fields. Stone walls. Houses. People.

“We look at the flow.”

Ed’s eyes moved. “…The flow?”

“Where the water wants to go. Where the wind passes. Where the earth gives way and where it holds. Where the heat escapes.” Sahara said, “And where we are.”

“So you use that flow?” Ed asked.

“Use.” Sahara turned the word over as if tasting it. “I suppose you could say that.”

“Then what would you say?”

“That we go into it.”

“What?”

“You read the flow of nature,” Sahara said slowly, “and become a part of it.”

Ed was quiet for a moment. Then he scowled. “That’s way too vague.”

“I agree,” Scar said.

“Right?”

“It is the first correct thing you have said.”

“What?!”

Sahara laughed. “Maybe that’s why it has no name.”

Ed looked down at Karsa again. “But you still need the lines.”

“Sometimes.”

“And the channels.”

“Sometimes.”

“Then the things on the walls and the ground when you attacked us yesterday?”

“Roads that were prepared long ago.”

Something changed in Ed’s eyes. “Roads.”

“The way you make places where water can flow easily,” Sahara said, “we make places where other things can pass easily too.”

“Other things?”

“Movement.”

“What kind of movement?” Ed asked at once.

Sahara laughed. “If I knew all of it, I’d have given it a name like you.”

“Oh, come on!”

Ed scratched his head. But this time, his face wasn’t only angry.

The flow.

The things he had been seeing since yesterday began to shift places in his head. The wall that had stopped when the channel was cut. The stones elsewhere that had moved after the wind. Strength that differed from place to place. A structure spread across the entire village.

“So…” Ed said slowly. “You don’t create the power.”

Sahara waited.

“You find what’s already moving…” Ed moved his hands. “…and make a road it can travel along?”

“If you understand it that way, that’s enough for now.”

Ed closed his mouth.

“Does your calculation work now?” Scar asked.

“No.” The answer came instantly. “Not at all.” Ed looked down at Karsa. “If anything, it’s worse.”

“Why?”

“Because that’s not enough to explain it.” Ed folded down his fingers one at a time. “Water.” One. “Wind.” Two. “Differences in height.” Three. “The force that was resting in the stone.” Four.

He paused. “…There’s more I don’t know.”

“Is that not obvious?” Scar said.

Ed glared at him. “I know. That’s why I’m excited.”

Scar shook his head.

“Your companion’s brother was like that too,” Sahara said.

Scar looked at her at once. “My brother?”

“For the first few days.” Sahara smiled. “He was quieter than this one, though.”

“Obviously,” Ed said. “What did he ask?”

“Things like you.” Sahara searched her memory. “What moves. What makes it move. How far it connects. And…” She paused for a moment. “Where the price is.”

The smile left Ed’s face. “…Really?”

“Really.”

“Did my brother find the answer?” Scar asked.

Sahara didn’t answer right away. “I don’t know if you could call it an answer.”

“What do you mean?”

“His question changed.”

That again.

Scar’s voice dropped. “How did it change?”

This time Sahara answered. “At first he asked how to move stone.” She pointed at the fields below. “Later he asked whether this could hold together land that was crumbling.”

Scar’s expression stiffened.

Sahara looked at him. “He asked whether it could be done in Ishval too.”

Scar said nothing. Ed went quiet as well.

“From then on,” Sahara said, “your brother spent less time looking at our method itself…” She paused. “…and more time looking at what it could be used for.”

Scar looked down at the fields without a word. “…I see.”

That was all.

Sahara said nothing more.

After a while, Ed asked carefully, “So did your brother learn this here and take it home?”

Scar looked at Ed.

Sahara shook her head. “It isn’t that simple.”

“Why not?”

“He watched what we did. He asked questions. He wrote things down.”

“That’s learning.”

“He already knew a great deal,” Sahara said. “He knew things we didn’t. And he didn’t know things we did.”

Ed narrowed his eyes. “So you learned from each other?”

“Something like that.”

“Learned what?”

“For that, we’d have to look at the records.”

Ed’s face lit up. “Records!”

Scar sighed.

In the afternoon, they went back to the record room. This time Sahara took down several old ledgers herself.

Scar searched for traces of his brother. Ed searched for the traveler from the west he had found last night. Neither of them spoke for a while.

Scar found something first. “Here.”

Ed looked up. It was a short record left by Scar’s brother. Karsa’s channels. The ground. The way they stored water. And notes on whether it could be applied in other regions.

Ed read it and said, “It’s really him.”

Scar was looking at the last line.

In places with little water, before thinking about how to make water, think first about how not to lose the water you have.

Scar’s finger stopped on that sentence. He quietly copied the page.

Ed said nothing.

A while later, it was Ed’s turn. “Found it.”

It was a ledger from the same period as last night’s record. A traveler from the west. No name. Stayed quite a long time. And a few of the things he had asked had been written down.

Ed read them. “’What is needed for stone to move.’” The next line. “’Can the same thing be done where water does not reach.’” The next. “’Does the earth itself have movement.’”

His hand stopped.

“What is it?” Scar asked.

“This.” Ed pointed at the last line.

How far does the movement of the earth extend?

The two of them looked down at the record for a moment.

“This guy too…” Ed muttered.

“He is like you,” Scar said.

“I thought of it first.”

“You did not say it.”

“I was about to!”

Sahara looked over their shoulders at the record. “It says he kept asking for a long time.”

Ed looked up. “Are there any other records?”

“We’ll look.”

“A name?”

“Not yet.”

“Where he came from?”

“The west.”

“I know that.”

“There might be a record that says somewhere farther west than that.”

Ed turned the pages again. “When was this?”

Sahara checked the chronology beside her. “It isn’t exact.”

“Roughly.”

“It’s not a matter of a few generations.”

Ed’s hand slowed. “Then?”

“It must be several hundred years.”

Ed looked up. “Several hundred years?”

“Yes.”

That snagged on something. But for now, that was all.

The west. Several hundred years ago. A traveler who had spent a long time studying Karsa’s method. None of it was enough to settle anything.

Ed looked at the record again. “Did this person learn it here?”

“Learn what?” Sahara said.

“The flow.” Ed said the word out loud. “The way nature moves. If he learned it here and then went east…”

“What would change?” Scar asked.

Ed started to answer, then stopped.

Xingese alkahestry. The flow of the earth. The Dragon’s Pulse. Transmutation that happened at a distance.

A line was trying to draw itself in his head. Karsa. The traveler from the west. The east.

Ed opened his mouth. Then he closed it. “…Not yet.”

Scar looked at him. “What?”

“It’s not enough.” Ed held up the record. “If I connect things using only this, I could be wrong again.”

Sahara laughed. “So you do know how to hold back a little.”

“It’s not a little.”

“Very little,” Scar said.

“Why are you joining in too?!”

Ed turned back to the records.

Until evening, the three of them went through the old ledgers. People from the west. People from the east. People from Ishval. People who brought medicine. People who left behind ways to handle water. People who learned something and left. People who left something behind.

The records were more complicated than he had expected. There was no single line that divided who had taught and who had learned.

Only after the sun had fully set did Ed lift his head from the books. Through the window, he could see Karsa’s lights. Water was still flowing in the channels.

Ed looked down at his palm.

When he could still use alchemy, he had always drawn a boundary. Transmutation circle. Materials. Result. Price. He had looked for the answer inside it.

But in Karsa, he couldn’t see that boundary. The lines were already laid across the land.

“Is there no price?” Ed said, half to himself.

“Are you still on that?” Scar asked beside him.

“Yeah.”

“Do you think they are lying?”

“No.” Ed shook his head. “That’s the bigger problem.”

“Why?”

Ed looked out the window. Water. Wind. Earth. Stone. People.

“If those people are really right…” He paused for a moment. “…then I have to go back and look again at the things I took for granted.”

“It would not be the first time,” Scar said.

Ed’s brow twitched. “…Okay, that’s true.”

Scar went back to his brother’s record. Ed looked at the old record of the traveler from the west.

One man had tried to bring the knowledge of this place back to Ishval. The other had been looking at the same flow several hundred years ago. And now Ed was sitting in the same place, wondering about the same things.

The three of them had never met. They had lived in different times. You couldn’t say they were searching for the same thing. And yet the first questions they had asked were strangely alike.

What moves.

Where does it come from.

How far does it extend.

Ed read the last sentence again.

How far does the movement of the earth extend?

And for the first time, he thought of a word that had come to him from Xing.

The Dragon’s Pulse.

Ed’s eyes narrowed. “…No way.”

Scar looked up. “What is it?”

Ed looked at the record for a while. Then he shook his head. “Not yet.”

This time, that really was all.
