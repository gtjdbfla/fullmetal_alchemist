“I found it.”

It was the morning after they got back to Lansi. Furong set his teacup down as he said it.

The moment Ed sat down across from him, he held out his hand. Beside him, Xiaolin was stifling a yawn. She had been following him around ever since they came up together from Haojin.

“What?”

“Money.”

“Already?”

“I don’t think that’s something the person who came to me last night and told me to find out by this morning gets to say.”

“And you found it.”

“Which is why I should be paid.”

“Let’s talk about that later.”

“You always say that.”

Furong set a few sheets of paper on the table. Ed picked up the first one at once. “Where?”

“First, let me correct one thing.”

“What?”

“There’s nothing strange at all about Lansi goods being in Haojin.”

“I know.”

“Herbs, hides, textiles, food. They go back and forth all the time.”

“I know that too.”

“You didn’t know it yesterday.”

“I know it now.”

Furong looked at Ed for a moment. “How very convenient for you.”

“So what about the money?”

Furong sighed. “I checked some of the goods Miss Xiaolin thought were odd. Some were bought directly, some on credit, and some were gifts.”

“Gifts?”

“Closer to support, to be precise.”

Ed’s hand stopped. “From who?”

“A few Lansi merchants.”

“Why would they do that?”

“To find that out, you have to follow the money one step further.”

Furong slid the second sheet over. Behind the names of several merchants, one merchant house appeared again and again. And above it, one more name.

Ed read it. “…Zhenha Bai.”

Beside him, Xiaolin caught her breath. “The Bai… in our village?”

“…That old man who was with Luhan?”

“Yes.” Furong nodded.

A Bai merchant. A man who had handled Lansi’s trading network for a long time. And the side most likely to lose out if a southern trade road opened.

Ed put the paper down. A rumor he’d heard a few days ago came back to him.

The Bai were moving the An from behind the scenes.

“…So it was true?”

Furong tilted his head. “What was?”

Ed went quiet for a moment. He remembered the name carved into the stone slab a few days ago.

Van Hohenheim.

That time, the evidence in front of his eyes had turned out to be right. This time it looked right too. That was exactly why he couldn’t rush.

Ed looked at the paper again. Zhenha Bai.

“No.”

“Pardon?”

“Not yet.”

Furong smiled faintly. “I like that answer.”

“Why?”

“It sounds like it’ll cost less.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

“Impatient customers are the most expensive.”

Ling wasn’t surprised when he heard the report. “Zhenha.”

“You know him?”

“He’s one of Luhan’s people.”

Beside him, Luhan’s face hardened. “…Elder Zhenha isn’t the kind of man who would do that.”

“Then let’s ask him ourselves,” Ling answered.

Luhan looked at Furong. “Are you certain?”

Furong bowed his head. “The only thing that’s certain is that the money leads this far.”

The transaction records were spread across the table. Lansi merchants. A middle merchant house. Bai-affiliated business partners. And Zhenha.

Luhan turned the records over one page at a time. “He sent money to the An.”

“And goods,” Ed said. “To those hardliners.”

“Any money that went directly to Kairun?” Ling asked.

Furong answered at once. “Not so far. A lot of money went to the hardliners. Kairun was at the center of them. That’s why it looks that way all the more.”

“Anyone connected to the assassination?”

“None.”

“Weapons?”

“Not confirmed.”

Ling leaned back in his chair. “Then we just have to ask him directly.”

Ed looked at him. “You’re calling him in?”

“Yeah.”

“What if he runs?”

“That’s why I’ve already sent people.”

Ed looked at Ling for a moment. “Being emperor’s convenient.”

“Jealous?”

“Not at all.”

Zhenha didn’t run. Instead, he walked into the government office himself.

The man, close to sixty, was neatly dressed, as befitted a merchant. He stood before Ling and paid his respects. Luhan didn’t look at Zhenha.

“Your Majesty.”

Ling returned the greeting, then asked straight out, “Did you send money to the An opposition?”

“I did.”

Ed blinked. That was too fast. Ling paused for a moment too. “…You admit it right away.”

“You’ve even found the ledgers. If I said no, would you believe me?”

“No.”

“Then it’s better to save time.”

Ed thought to himself: I don’t like this guy.

“Why?” Ling asked.

“Because of the southern trade road.” Zhenha was calm. “If the road opens, the An’s trade will grow. Goods from the west will be able to go directly through Haojin and the An.”

“And Lansi?”

“It will matter less than it does now.”

“And the Bai too.”

“That’s right.”

“So you used the An?” Luhan asked.

Zhenha looked at Luhan. “I used them.”

Luhan’s expression stiffened.

Zhenha went on. “But I didn’t create opposition that wasn’t already there.”

“Is that an excuse?”

“It’s a fact.”

“Give people money and their voices get louder.”

“I suppose so.”

“Give them goods and they can hold out longer.”

“That’s right.”

Zhenha didn’t deny any of it. That made it all the more unpleasant.

“I didn’t tell them the southern trade road would be bad for the An. There were already people who thought so.”

“So you gave them money instead,” Ed cut in.

“That’s right.”

“And goods.”

“That’s right.”

“Information too?”

For the first time, Zhenha looked at Ed. “What information do you mean?”

“The imperial survey schedule. How the road review’s going. When Ling’s in Lansi.”

Ling turned to look at Ed. Ed paused for a moment too. “…The last one, I was just asking.”

“I passed along information about the trade road,” Zhenha said. “How far the imperial review had progressed, which areas were being surveyed. That sort of thing.”

“Why?”

“If you’re going to oppose something, you need to know about it.”

“So they’d oppose it well?”

“That’s right.”

The room went quiet.

It was too clear. Money. Goods. Information. Even a goal. Delaying the southern trade road. Killing the plan, if possible. And among the An hardliners who received that support was Kairun.

Ed looked at Zhenha. Now there was just one thing to confirm.

“You know Kairun?”

Zhenha’s eyebrow moved. “Who?”

Ed stopped. “Kairun.”

“I’ve never heard the name.”

“An An man. He came here a few days ago.”

Zhenha thought for a moment. “You mean the man who attacked His Majesty?”

“Yeah.”

“That was his name? Kairun?”

This time Ed didn’t say anything.

“Did you ever order him to assassinate me?” Ling asked.

For the first time, Zhenha’s expression changed. “Never.”

“Did you know about it?”

“No.”

“Did you ever tell anyone to attack the imperial court?”

“Never.”

“Did you send weapons?”

“No.”

“Then what did you want?”

Zhenha was silent for a moment. “Opposition.”

“How far?”

“For the southern trade road to be delayed.”

“And?”

“If possible, for the plan itself to be reconsidered.”

“And?”

“Nothing beyond that.”

Luhan asked sharply, “If the Emperor dies, the road stops too.”

“I suppose it would.”

“Isn’t that to your advantage?”

“No.” Zhenha’s answer was fast. “If the Emperor were assassinated, the army might go into An territory. Fighting between the clans might begin. Trade in Lansi would be the first thing to stop.”

Luhan closed his mouth. It was a merchant’s calculation. Realistic to the point of cruelty.

Ling looked at Furong. “Any direct connection?”

“None so far.”

“And the metal ornament from Kairun’s belongings?”

Furong took a small object out of his robe. An old metal ornament. A faint mark remained on it.

Zhenha looked at it once and said, “An old merchant house mark.”

“Bai?”

“Yes.”

“Is it yours?”

“No.”

“Then why did Kairun have it?”

“I don’t know.”

Furong added, “It’s a mark that’s hardly used anymore. It wouldn’t be strange for it to turn up on secondhand goods or old trade items.”

Ed looked down at the ornament. Until yesterday, it would have looked like fairly strong evidence. Bai money. Bai goods. Bai information. A Bai mark found among Kairun’s belongings. And an attempt to assassinate the Emperor. All of it was true.

And yet—

“…We collected only true things.”

Ling looked at Ed. Ed put the ornament down.

“But the answer’s wrong.”

“What do you mean?” Zhenha asked.

“That you told Kairun to kill the Emperor.”

“I—”

“I know,” Ed cut him off. “At least from everything that’s come out so far, that line isn’t there.”

“Then you think Kairun was alone?” Ling asked quietly.

Ed didn’t answer right away. “Not yet on that either.”

Ling grinned. “You’re really being careful this time.”

“I was always like this.”

“A few days ago you found your dad and got all excited—”

“Don’t bring that up.”

“Your father?” Luhan turned.

Ed glared at Ling. “I’m killing you later.”

“That’s an assassination threat against the Emperor.”

“You’re so annoying!”

For the first time, Zhenha looked a little flustered.

Even after the questioning was over, Zhenha couldn’t leave right away. The ledgers and transaction records had to be checked further.

Before he went out, Luhan said, “Let me ask one more thing.”

Zhenha stopped.

“Why did you do that for the Bai?”

“I’ve told you.”

“Not the trade road.” Luhan’s voice was low. “Why was it you who decided?”

Zhenha looked at Luhan for a moment. “Because someone has to.”

Luhan’s eyes wavered.

“It is a matter of protecting the interests of the Bai.”

“So it’s all right to use another clan?”

“I don’t think it’s good.”

“But you did it.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Zhenha said, very evenly, “I am the one who protects the interests of the Bai.”

Luhan closed his mouth. A moment later, he said, “You taught me to have power. You never told me to use it like this.”

“Power always ends up being used,” Zhenha said. “Choosing how to use it is my work. Deciding how far to go is yours, Lord Luhan.”

“I don’t want to protect the Bai like that.”

Zhenha bowed his head. “Then you’ll have to find another way.”

And he went out. On his way out, Zhenha’s gaze rested on Luhan for a moment. Luhan didn’t meet it.

Ed saw it, but he didn’t step in. It wasn’t his problem. Not yet, at least.

Chenluo stayed in Lansi until that evening. Ed went to find him.

Before Ed could open his mouth, Chenluo asked first. “Kairun. Is he getting a funeral?”

“Ling says to send him home. With people to go with him.”

“…I see.”

Chenluo slipped the white stone he’d been holding into his sleeve.

“I’ve got something to ask,” Ed said.

“What?”

“The people Kairun hung around with. They got support from the Bai.”

Chenluo’s face hardened. “How much?”

“Money. Goods. Information.”

“…”

“And you?”

“I didn’t take any.”

“You sure?”

Chenluo glared at Ed. “Why would I lie?”

“I’m just checking.”

“The Emperor’s people came and asked too.”

“Then that’s that.”

Chenluo said nothing for a while. Then he asked, “Then…”

“Hm?”

“Did they make my opinions too?”

Ed narrowed his eyes. “What?”

“Opposing the southern road.” Chenluo looked at the ground. “Saying the imperial court shouldn’t survey An land however it likes.” His voice dropped. “Kairun said similar things.”

Ed thought for a moment. “Who told you that?”

“What?”

“About your land and your wells.”

“No one.”

“Did the Bai give you money and tell you to think that way?”

“No.”

“So you thought that way to begin with?”

Chenluo looked at Ed. “…Yes.”

“Then it’s your opinion.”

“But those people—”

“The money they gave doesn’t disappear,” Ed said. “Neither does them using you. But don’t hand them the reason you opposed it too.”

“…”

“That’s yours.”

After a long while, Chenluo nodded.

On the way back to his lodgings, Ed passed through the market he’d walked by that morning. This time a different story was going around.

“So it was the Bai after all.”

“They say they gave the An money.”

“Then the Bai must have been behind trying to kill the Emperor too.”

“Must have.”

Ed stopped walking. Until yesterday, he would have just walked past. Not today.

“That’s not right.”

The two of them turned. “What isn’t?”

“It’s not that far yet.”

“Didn’t they give money?”

“They did.”

“Goods too?”

“Those too.”

“Then it’s right.”

Ed opened his mouth, then shut it. Explaining would take a long time. A very long time.

“…Never mind.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Ed walked on. Rumors were fast. Faster than facts.

He stopped and took the paper Furong had given him out of his coat. On one side, Zhenha’s name. Below it: money, goods, information. On the other side, Kairun.

He put his finger between the two names, then lifted it again without drawing a line. He folded the paper, put it away, and kept walking.

Just then, someone came running toward him. An imperial messenger. He brushed past Ed and ran toward the government office. Another horse came in right behind him.

Ed looked up. The air in Lansi was changing again.

This time, ahead of the rumors.
