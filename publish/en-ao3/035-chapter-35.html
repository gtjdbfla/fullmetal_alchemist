Lansi, Xing.

There were three more clashes overnight. No one died in them. But one Bai man injured the day before died at dawn.

From early that morning, Ling gave the clinic and the guards the same order. An or Bai, save them first. Work out who started it afterward.

Ed stopped halfway through a report.

“Take this out.”

“What?”

“The fire at the Bai shop.”

Ling looked at the document. “Possible An retaliation.”

“Possible. It’s not a fact.”

Ed pointed to another line. “This too. The first Bai merchant who died.”

“Why that one?”

“You don’t know who killed him.”

Ling studied the report for a moment. People were already building a story. A Bai merchant had died. A Bai shop had burned. An An man had died. And fighting had continued all night. Listed as facts, every one was true. That made it more dangerous.

“Leave out what we don’t know,” Ling said.

“No.”

Ed shook his head. “Leaving it out is wrong too. It happened.”

“Then?”

“Write that we don’t know.”

Ling looked at Ed for a moment, then picked up his brush. Beside both incidents, he wrote the same words.

Under investigation.

An hour later Lien pinned that sheet to the market wall. What was confirmed and what was not were split into two columns. People spent the whole morning arguing in front of it.

Before noon, a bell rang in the Bai residential district.

“Again?!”

Ed ran first. Dozens of people had already gathered in the square.

“Hand over the murderer!”

“The An did it!”

“There’s no evidence!”

The imperial soldier’s voice vanished in the crowd. By the time Ling and Lan Fan arrived, a stone had already flown.

Glass shattered. A shop window broke. Someone was shoved to the front. From the alley opposite, An people came running.

“Ling!” Ed shouted.

“I know!”

Ling stepped forward. “Stop!”

His voice rang across the square. For a moment—a very brief moment—they actually stopped.

A Bai man shouted, “Your Majesty, do you know who killed him?”

“No.”

“Wasn’t it the An?”

“That hasn’t been established.”

“Then how long are we supposed to wait?”

“We can’t turn what we don’t know into a fact!”

Another stone flew. This time, it hit a person. Someone screamed.

That was all it took. The crowd moved.

“Damn it!”

Ed plunged into the crowd. A piece of metal struck the ground. His eyes shifted. Another one. A small metal marker flew from a Bai man’s hand.

The ground rose.

“Alkahestrist!”

Ed shoved the person beside him away. A stone wall rose just behind them. Another piece of metal rolled from the opposite side, this time from the An. The ground split, forming a ridge between people.

Ed ducked. Markers. Distance. And the earth between them.

The Dragon’s Pulse.

It was different from Kairun. They marked specific points. Through the earth’s flow, they transmuted at a distance. It wasn’t one transmutation continuing along every surrounding path, as Kairun’s had.

“I knew it,” Ed muttered. “It’s not the same.”

“What isn’t?!” Ling shouted behind him.

“Later!”

Ed lunged at a man reaching for a metal marker and caught his wrist.

“Let go!”

“No!”

A fist came at him. Ed ducked and drove against the man’s waist. They rolled across the ground. The metal piece fell. Ed kicked it away.

“You think I can’t beat someone up just because I can’t use alchemy?!”

“You’re proud of that?!”

“Right now I am!”

The clashes weren’t confined to one place. A report came in: An people had been surrounded in the northern alley.

“Send half the troops north!” Ling ordered.

Luhan turned. He stood in front of the Bai people. There was no blood on his scabbard. On the ground, people he had felled with the back of his blade and its hilt were groaning.

“Your Majesty. There are many wounded here too. If you withdraw the troops, the Bai will be in danger.”

“An civilians are trapped in the north. We have to protect both.”

Ling cut him off.

“They’re both my people.”

Luhan’s mouth closed.

It wasn’t wrong. That made it stranger. Ling wasn’t wrong. When the An were in danger, he sent soldiers. When the Bai were hurt, he opened clinics. He said he didn’t know who had killed the Bai merchant. He also stopped the claim that the An had burned the Bai shop, because there was no evidence.

He was fair. As an emperor.

Luhan looked behind him. The Bai were there. Frightened faces. Angry faces. Faces looking at him.

Ling could not choose only them. Because he was the emperor.

At that moment, words he had heard for years came back to him. Protect the Bai. Gain power. The emperor has the greatest power of all.

Luhan gripped his sword.

If I were emperor.

Until a few days ago, he had denied that thought.

Must I become emperor?

That question, too, was behind him now. Luhan lifted his head.

I must become emperor.

At that same moment, on the main road of the An quarter.

Xiaolin stood in front of the column.

“Chenluo. Stop.”

“Move.”

“You said you object because of your land. Whose land is it for now?”

Chenluo did not slow down.

“Your road talk is your business. This is ours.”

Xiaolin’s face hardened.

The people pushed her aside. By the time she got up, the column was already passing.

She didn’t even brush the dust from her sleeve. She ran to find Ed.

“Ed! Chenluo’s—”

She was too out of breath to finish.

“Chenluo’s what?”

“He’s gathering people and marching on the administrative hall. He says he’s going to get the people who were locked up out. Three An died yesterday!”

“Stop him.”

“I can’t. He won’t listen to me. If it’s you—”

Xiaolin was gripping Ed’s sleeve.

“…Where?”

The main road to the administrative hall ran past the entrance to the Bai quarter. If they met there, it was over.

Ed ran, teeth clenched.

An people were packing the street. Some held swords. Some held nothing. Chenluo was at the front.

“Chenluo!”

“Move.”

“Where are you going?!”

“To get the people who were locked up.”

Chenluo didn’t slow down.

“Kairun’s dead, the people who came with him are locked up, and three An have died. And the one who paid the money is only having his ledgers checked.”

“What happens when you march in there with swords?!”

“We’ll see when we get there.”

Ed tried to block the way, but the crowd shoved him aside. Chenluo didn’t even look at him.

“…Damn it.”

Ed turned and sprinted for the administrative hall.

By sunset, the fighting had spread to three districts.

Ed came running up to Ling, out of breath.

“Chenluo’s bringing people! The main road!”

Ling sent half the troops toward the main road. Ling pushed the imperial troops between the two sides.

“Don’t cut first!”

“Push them back!”

“Separate the streets!”

The soldiers locked shields, forcing the An and Bai apart.

Then the An column appeared at the end of the main road. Chenluo was at its head.

At the entrance to the Bai quarter, merchant guards stood in a single line, a wall against the column. Behind that line stood Zhenha.

“Hold position only,” Zhenha told them quietly. “Do not move first. If they draw swords, you simply stand there.”

Then the Bai warriors moved. One. Two. And at their head was Luhan.

Luhan walked past the edge of the guards’ line.

“Lord Luhan. Do not cross that line.”

“You taught me. To take power.”

“I never taught you to use it.”

Luhan didn’t stop. Zhenha didn’t follow. The guards’ line didn’t move either.

“Luhan!”

Ling’s voice reached him. Imperial soldiers blocked the way. Luhan stopped, then slowly knelt.

“Your Majesty.”

“What is it?”

Luhan’s face twisted. “I must become emperor.”

Ling said nothing.

“To protect the Bai.”

Luhan closed his eyes. “There is no other way.”

“Have I abandoned the Bai?”

“No.”

“Have I failed to protect them?”

“No.”

“Then why?”

Luhan opened his eyes. “Because Your Majesty cannot protect only the Bai.”

“…”

“The An are your people too.”

“Yes.”

“I am the heir to the Bai.”

Luhan rose. “Your Majesty became emperor for the Yao clan, too.”

Ling’s expression hardened.

“Is this what you want?”

For a moment, Luhan couldn’t answer.

“…It is what I must do.”

Kneeling had been his last courtesy.

His sword came free.

“Luhan,” Ling said quietly.

Luhan advanced. An imperial soldier thrust out a spear. Luhan twisted his sword. The back of the blade struck the shaft, and the hilt drove against the soldier’s chin. The soldier fell. Another rushed him. Luhan shoved him aside with his body.

He didn’t cut them. But he didn’t stop.

“Luhan!”

Lan Fan blocked his path. Steel met steel with a sharp ring. Luhan was driven back. Lan Fan stepped in. Fast. Luhan raised his sword again, but she knocked his wrist aside.

He clenched his teeth. “Move.”

“I will not.”

“Move!”

Their swords clashed again. Ed rushed in from the side and grabbed Luhan’s arm.

“Stop!”

“Get out of the way!”

“No!”

Luhan swung his arm. Ed hung on.

“This has nothing to do with you!”

“Don’t tell me that when you’re trying to kill someone right in front of me!”

Lan Fan came in again. Luhan was already exhausted. He had broken through the imperial soldiers. Ed had seized him. Lan Fan had blocked his way again and again.

Still, he kept going forward. Toward Ling.

Luhan’s gaze shifted. Lan Fan didn’t miss it. One step. The distance closed. His throat was within her grasp.

She could end it. She knew that distance. She had done it once before.

The man who had tried to kill the emperor. Kairun. She hadn’t hesitated then.

And Ling had said, Next time, don’t kill.

Her hand stopped for only an instant. Luhan didn’t waste it. He twisted. Lan Fan’s hand went for his shoulder instead of his throat. She caught it, but couldn’t wrench it all the way back. Luhan threw his body forward and broke free.

“Lan Fan!”

Ed lunged again, this time seizing Luhan around the waist. Luhan staggered. Lan Fan was immediately on him again. This time, she didn’t hesitate.

Stop him without killing him.

Just as she made that decision, a blade flashed to the side. A Bai warrior was aiming for an imperial soldier’s throat.

Lan Fan’s gaze snapped around. Her body moved first.

A crash. The blade flew aside. The soldier fell backward.

In that instant, Luhan tore Ed’s arms away. Ed rolled across the ground.

“Ling!”

Luhan ran. Lan Fan turned.

Too late.

The point of Luhan’s sword bore down on Ling. Only then did Ling draw his own.

Steel rang against steel. Ling was driven back one step. Luhan thrust again. Ling deflected it. The point grazed his collar.

“Are you really going to do this?”

Luhan didn’t answer. His sword came again. Ling caught it.

Luhan was strong. But he had already fought too much. His breathing was ragged. His wrist was slowing.

Ling was different. He blocked. Dodged. Waited.

At the third clash, Ling’s sword pushed Luhan’s blade outward. Luhan forced it back. At that instant, Ling’s scabbard struck his wrist.

A sharp crack. His fingers loosened. The sword fell.

Before Luhan could move, Ling’s point stopped in front of his throat.

Both were breathing hard. Everything around them fell silent.

Ling asked, “Do I still have to die?”

Luhan’s eyes wavered.

“If I die, can you protect the Bai?”

“…”

“Luhan.”

A long silence. Luhan looked at his fallen sword. Only moments ago, he had believed the answer lay at its point. Defeat Ling. Become emperor himself. Then he could protect the Bai.

He didn’t believe that now. But no other answer had taken its place.

“I don’t know.”

Ling lowered his sword. Luhan didn’t move. The imperial soldiers approached. He didn’t resist. His hands were bound. He didn’t bow his head. Didn’t apologize.

It was simply over.

“Lord Luhan!”

Behind him, a Bai warrior shouted. He still held his sword. The imperial soldiers braced themselves again.

Luhan didn’t turn.

“…Stop.”

The warrior didn’t move.

Luhan spoke again. “Stop.”

The sword point lowered. One. Two. The Bai lowered their weapons.

From the other side, someone shouted, “Now!”

It came from the An.

“The Bai have put down their weapons!”

People started to move. Chenluo stepped in front of them. It was he who had led the column here.

“Now what?”

“They started—”

“So we should draw our swords too?”

Chenluo turned to the people. “Is that what we wanted?”

He looked at the people he had led here. After a moment, he added quietly, “…I did it too.”

From the back, someone muttered, “Kairun was right. Even without an emperor…”

Chenluo didn’t turn around. “Kairun drew his sword alone. He said if we all came, we’d all die.”

At the rear of the column, Xiaolin stood with a hand over her mouth, tears smeared across her face.

No one answered.

He pointed at Luhan. “To become emperor?”

Silence.

“It wasn’t.”

One by one, the An lowered their weapons too.

Night fell. The clinic was still overflowing.

Chenluo sat beside one of the cots. On it lay an An child who had been knocked down and had hurt a leg when the column he had led surged forward. He said nothing.

On the next cot, the old man from the relay village was wrapping cloth around his grandson’s knee. It was the child Ed had saved.

The dead did not come back. They still didn’t know who had killed the Bai merchant. They still didn’t know who had set fire to the shop.

The land and water issues the An had raised remained unresolved. The future the Bai feared had not vanished. Luhan being wrong didn’t mean Ling had all the answers.

No one said they had won.

There was simply no one left willing to draw a sword.

At least, not that night.
